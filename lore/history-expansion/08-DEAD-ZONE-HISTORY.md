# Dead Zone historical development, 2046 onward
**Status:** Baseline historical expansion. Dead Zone starts in 2046; exact global conditions and regional state must be reconciled with the era source documents. Outcomes vary by campaign.

## 2046: a world of successors
The world is not uniformly empty or lawless. Some governments, councils, farms, clinics, schools, and trading networks persist or reconstitute. Other areas are dangerous, abandoned, or contested. Infrastructure functions where skills, parts, labor, and institutions sustain it. People remember earlier eras and argue about what should be preserved.

**Heritage Territory:** Nathan Mercer, approximately 61, and Rebecca Mercer, approximately 57, jointly rule most of a **fictional Texas county**. Their capital is the original 135-acre Heritage Ranch property, now known as the Heritage Settlement. Its original cattle operation provided food and funding for Elias's deliberately constructed survivalist compound. The county-wide regime grew later; it is not confined to the original ranch acreage.

## The Mercer regime
Nathan commands armed security, road control, logistics, and district enforcement. Rebecca directs ideology, education, membership, and civil administration. Both are more authoritarian than Elias, but their bases of power differ. The regime can supply food and protection while imposing travel controls, compulsory service, harsh punishment, and unequal access to resources.

The siblings cooperate because each needs the other. Nathan believes survival requires coercive security; Rebecca believes it requires uniform loyalty. Their disagreements offer political openings but do not make either an automatic ally of the players.

## Places of consequence
**The Old Ranch Gate:** The site where the original HERITAGE RANCH sign once stood. The replacement gate bears THE HERITAGE SETTLEMENT. Older members remember the change.
**The Founder's Residence:** A symbol of Elias's legacy, interpreted differently by official historians and former members.
**The Distribution Yards:** Food and supply allocation point, essential to residents and a source of political leverage.
**The District Roads:** Checkpoints and escort routes where the regime's claims of protection meet travelers' experiences.
**Independent Farm Belt:** Neighboring communities that trade, negotiate, comply, or resist. Exact map boundaries are not yet established.

## People of note
**Mara Ellis:** Former school administrator who preserves memories of the founding generation.
**Jonah Price:** Quartermaster whose ledgers can reveal food distribution patterns.
**Silas Trent:** Security commander whose district practices may exceed even official regulations.
**Dr. Celia Rourke:** Rural physician who insists medical care should not depend on political loyalty.
**Third-generation Mercers:** Potential adult heirs or reformers, not yet named or assigned fixed loyalties. Nathan and Rebecca remain the principal early Dead Zone rulers.

## Historical conflicts
**The County Road Conflicts (2039–2042):** Remembered as the turning point from negotiated protection to enforced territorial rule.
**The Settlement Compact (2043–2045):** A contested agreement imposing service and loyalty obligations on dependent communities.
**The Founder's Memory Dispute (2046 onward, proposed):** Elders and younger administrators disagree over whether Elias intended a voluntary community or a permanent governing order. Documents, sermons, and the missing wooden sign become political artifacts.
**The Border Harvest Disputes (post-2046, proposed):** Independent farms and Settlement administrators contest crop deliveries, seed access, and passage rights. Outcomes remain campaign-dependent.
