# Blackbox Protocol: People of Note
## An in-depth public biographical register, Core baseline 1 January 2026

**Setting:** Blackbox Protocol (Core)
**Status:** Expanded draft. Supplements `02-NOTABLE-PEOPLE.md` and the people sections of `lore/core/BLACKBOX-PROTOCOL-ALTERNATE-HISTORY.md`; replaces neither. The manuscript wins any conflict. Birth years, home regions, relationships and contacts marked **(proposed)** are new in this draft and stay editable.
**Audience:** Player-safe. Each entry records only what a well-read resident could learn by January 2026. Where the GM Secret History holds more about a person, this register says only that the public record is incomplete.
**Fiction notice:** Real historical figures appear only in their real public roles. They are not implicated in any fictional program, scandal or wrongdoing, and no private biography is invented for them. Every other person here is fictional.
**Rules notice:** Setting history only. Contact entries describe who a character might talk to, not new mechanics.

---

## How to use this register

Every fictional figure here is written to the editorial biographical standard in `02`: a birth year or explicit "unspecified", a home region, career milestones, affiliations, public reputation, relationships, and at least two playable contacts or records. Each entry also carries:

- **What people say**, the competing public views of the person;
- **What the record shows**, what can actually be documented;
- **At the table**, how characters might meet or use them.

None of these people is a secret villain by default. Several have done things they regret, some have been accused of things they did not do, and all of them have reasons for what they say in public.

## Part I — Real historical figures

These people appear in the setting exactly as they appear in history. Fictional scholars, journalists and officials study and argue about them, but none of them founded, ran, knew of or benefited from any Blackbox program. A GM should never write them into fictional misconduct.

| Figure | Real public role | Why the setting remembers them |
|---|---|---|
| **John F. Kennedy** (1917–1963) | US President during the 1962 Cuban Missile Crisis | Fictional continuity scholars study the crisis as the founding case of command under uncertainty. |
| **Nikita Khrushchev** (1894–1971) | Soviet leader during the same crisis | Studied alongside Kennedy as a decision-maker working through slow and uncertain channels. |
| **Mikhail Gorbachev** (1931–2022) | Last leader of the Soviet Union; architect of glasnost and perestroika | His reforms and the Soviet dissolution are the backdrop to the 1991 divergence. |
| **Nelson Mandela** (1918–2013) and **F. W. de Klerk** (1936–2021) | Leaders of South Africa's negotiated end to apartheid | Cited in civic debates as an example of transition through negotiation and institution-building. |
| **Václav Havel** (1936–2011) | Dissident playwright, later President of Czechoslovakia and the Czech Republic | A touchstone in arguments about civil society versus state power. |
| **Kofi Annan** (1938–2018) | UN Secretary-General, 1997–2006 | Associated with international coordination and UN reform debates. |
| **Wangari Maathai** (1940–2011) | Kenyan environmental and civic organiser; Nobel Peace laureate | An example of resilience built by citizens rather than by governments or contractors. |
| **Tim Berners-Lee** (born 1955) | Inventor of the World Wide Web | Anchors the networked society that later continuity systems have to serve. |
| **Angela Merkel** (born 1954) and **Barack Obama** (born 1961) | German Chancellor and US President in the 2000s and 2010s | Their real political eras form the context of public policy debates. |

**At the table.** A character writing a thesis, preparing testimony or giving a lecture might quote any of these people's real public words. They do not appear as NPCs with secret knowledge.

## Part II — The Blackbox-era public figures

These are the people most associated in the public mind with the Blackbox legacy and its scandals. Between them they cover the main public episodes: Port Meridian, Harrowgate, the oversight reforms and the long argument about custody of records.

### Nadia Serrano — the Harrowgate whistleblower

**Born:** 1979 (proposed). **Home region:** Grew up in Albuquerque, New Mexico; lives in the Washington, D.C., area (proposed).
**Affiliations:** Former technical auditor for a contractor operating a Blackbox-related system; now an independent compliance consultant (proposed).

**Career.** Serrano trained in computer science and information assurance and spent most of her early career auditing access controls for large contractors. By the mid-2010s she was a technical auditor whose job was to check that the people using a sensitive system were allowed to. In 2016 she preserved access logs that she believed showed civilians being monitored far beyond lawful authority, and submitted them through protected reporting channels. When those channels did not act in time, she took them to independent investigators. The resulting scandal became the Harrowgate Affair.

**What people say.** To supporters she is the person who proved that oversight rules could be enforced and paid for it with her career. To critics she bypassed procedures meant to protect sensitive systems and set a dangerous precedent. Some former colleagues admire her privately and will not say so publicly.

**What the record shows.** Court records and hearing transcripts confirm that the logs she preserved were authentic and that significant parts of her complaint were substantiated. Some allegations attributed to her by later commentators were never hers. She has never claimed to know the origins of the systems she audited, and she repeats that point in interviews.

**Since 2016.** She testified at two hearings, was named in civil litigation she eventually won, and now advises local governments and smaller firms on audit trails and whistleblower procedures. She declines most media requests. She is wary of anyone who claims to have "more Harrowgate documents", because forgeries have followed her for a decade.

**Relationships.** Professional respect for **Elias Ward**, whose reporting she credits with protecting her by insisting on corroboration. Occasional co-panellist with **Leena Okafor** on record-custody reform.

**At the table.**
- **Contact:** Will talk to investigators with a credible, documented question about access controls. She will not talk to anyone who opens with conspiracy theories.
- **Records:** Public hearing testimony (2016–2017); her published audit-practice guide (2021, proposed); civil court filings.
- **Hook:** Someone is circulating a new "Serrano file" in 2025, apparently tied to the Orion Data Dispute. She says it is fake. Is it?

### Elias Ward — the careful journalist

**Born:** 1971 (proposed). **Home region:** Baltimore, Maryland (proposed).
**Affiliations:** Former staff reporter at a national daily; now an independent investigative journalist and lecturer (proposed).

**Career.** Ward started on a city desk covering procurement and courts, and built a reputation for patience rather than scoops. He covered the late stages of the Port Meridian dispute as a young reporter and the Greybridge Hearings as a veteran. His Harrowgate series of 2016 was carefully corroborated: every key claim had at least two independent supports. When copycat "Harrowgate documents" began circulating, he published a widely read guide to spotting fabricated records.

**What people say.** Admired as the model of verification-first reporting. Criticised by some activists as too cautious, and by some officials as a man who made a career out of their worst day.

**What the record shows.** His published corrections, four in thirty years (proposed), are a matter of public record, and he discusses them openly. He has never published a claim about the 1991 Initiative that he could not support, which is why he has published so little about it.

**Not to be confused with** Elias Mercer of Heritage Ranch. The shared first name is a coincidence.

**Relationships.** Long-standing source relationship with **Daniel Rusk**; mentor to **Nia Park** (from the Core world foundation notes); professional friendship with **Rina Mercado**.

**At the table.**
- **Contact:** Teaches a verification workshop. A character who attends may gain an introduction.
- **Records:** The Harrowgate series archive; his fabricated-document guide; his notebooks, which he says he will donate to a university archive "when I stop needing them".
- **Hook:** Ward has been sent a box of Orion documents and is sitting on them because he cannot verify them. He needs help, and he is very particular about who he trusts.

### Martin Kessler — the guarded official

**Born:** 1948 (proposed). **Home region:** Northern Virginia (proposed).
**Affiliations:** Former senior continuity official; retired; occasional speaker at policy forums (proposed).

**Career (public).** Kessler spent a long career in government continuity and emergency planning, rising to senior posts in the 1990s. Public records show he left sensitive work around the turn of the century. He resurfaced in the 2010s as a speaker at policy forums on institutional memory and the legal ambiguity of emergency powers.

**What people say.** Some see him as an honest old hand warning about systems no one fully understands. Others think his careful, evasive answers mean he knows far more than he says. Online, he is the subject of elaborate theories.

**What the record shows.** Very little. His public remarks are precise and sometimes evasive. He has never been charged with anything. Speculation about him far exceeds what is documented, and his guarded testimony **should not be treated as a confession**. The public record about his departure from sensitive work is incomplete. (GM: see the GM Secret History.)

**Relationships.** Publicly cordial with former colleagues. Appears on panels with reformers and defenders of continuity systems alike.

**At the table.**
- **Contact:** Hard to reach; speaks at one or two forums a year. He answers questions about general principles and deflects questions about specific programs.
- **Records:** Forum transcripts (2012–2025); his name on declassified 1990s organisational charts.
- **Hook:** A historian working on the 1990s wants to interview him and has been refused three times. Kessler is in his late seventies; whatever he knows will not wait forever.

### Leena Okafor — the reformer of records

**Born:** 1969 (proposed). **Home region:** Born in Houston, Texas, to Nigerian-American parents; lives in Philadelphia (proposed).
**Affiliations:** Former compliance analyst in procurement oversight; independent consultant to local governments on records and audit reform (proposed).

**Career (public).** Okafor began her career in procurement oversight in the 1990s and later moved into compliance consulting. Her public work since the 2000s focuses on unglamorous things: who holds custody of a record, how long it must be kept, how an audit trail should be written so that someone twenty years later can follow it. Cities and counties hire her after something has gone wrong.

**What people say.** Respected by auditors, archivists and reform-minded officials. Little known outside those circles. Some people who know her career say she does not like to talk about the early part of it, and that has led to speculation.

**What the record shows.** Her consulting reports, conference papers and model retention schedules are public and widely used. She has never publicly discussed specific classified programs. (GM: see the GM Secret History for her earlier career.)

**Not to be confused with** Dana Okafor, oversight counsel in the Core world foundation, or Samuel Okafor, the pregenerated nurse. No family relationship is established (proposed: none).

**Relationships.** Professional ally of **Nadia Serrano** and **Adewale Bako**. Has testified before committees at which **Martin Kessler** also appeared; neither has ever publicly referred to the other.

**At the table.**
- **Contact:** Reachable through professional associations; generous with time for anyone fixing a records problem.
- **Records:** Her model retention schedule (2009, proposed); consulting reports for several municipalities.
- **Hook:** A county in the campaign hires her after losing a decade of maintenance records. She thinks it was negligence. The characters may find otherwise.

### Daniel Rusk — the voice of the docks

**Born:** 1960 (proposed). **Home region:** The fictional Port Meridian, where he worked for twenty-five years (proposed).
**Affiliations:** Port-union representative; later a national advocate for worker protections in emergency contracting (proposed).

**Career.** Rusk was a crane operator and shop steward when the Port Meridian modernisation project began. In the aftermath of the 1998 dispute, he became the public face of workers who had lost shifts, been reassigned or been asked to sign confidentiality agreements they did not understand. He spent the next decade pressing for records access and transparent chains of custody in emergency contracts. He testified at the Greybridge Hearings in 2024.

**What people say.** To dockworkers, he is the man who kept asking questions. To some contractors, he is a professional agitator. Many neutral observers simply find him persuasive.

**What the record shows.** His disputes with contractors are political, and he has never claimed that all contractors are corrupt. Union archives hold his correspondence, meeting minutes and an extensive file of records requests, many still unanswered.

**Relationships.** Source and friend of **Elias Ward**. Admirer of **Leena Okafor**'s retention work.

**At the table.**
- **Contact:** Retired, talkative and still angry. He will help anyone who wants to understand Port Meridian, starting with the workers' side.
- **Records:** Union newsletter archive; his personal file of records requests, 1998–2024.
- **Hook:** A subcontract from 1998 that Rusk has been chasing for twenty-five years has turned up in the Orion documents.

### Dr. Amara Bell — the logistics scholar

**Born:** 1966 (proposed). **Home region:** Atlanta, Georgia (proposed).
**Affiliations:** Professor of public-health logistics at a fictional university; adviser to emergency-management associations (proposed).

**Career.** Bell's studies from 2005 to 2023 compare emergency supply chains across many disasters, including the Indian Ocean tsunami, Hurricane Katrina and the COVID-19 pandemic. Her central finding, that stockpiles fail without trusted last-mile distribution, is now standard teaching. She is known in professional circles, not in celebrity media.

**What people say.** Colleagues regard her as rigorous and impatient with easy answers. Contractors sometimes find her findings inconvenient.

**What the record shows.** Published research, conference keynotes and advisory reports. She has publicly criticised some Blackbox-derived systems for rigidity during the 2020 pandemic while crediting others with real successes.

**Relationships.** Has co-authored with **Yuki Tanabe**. Has cited **Sofia Valdez**'s county-level work.

**At the table.**
- **Contact:** Can explain any campaign's supply problem in an afternoon; will want data in return.
- **Records:** Her comparative study series; her pandemic-era advisory memos (2020–2021).
- **Hook:** She has noticed a pattern in recent regional supply delays that does not fit any of her models.

### Rina Mercado — the Philippine investigator

**Born:** 1984 (proposed). **Home region:** Tacloban, Leyte; based in Manila (proposed).
**Affiliations:** Independent investigative reporter; contributor to Philippine and international outlets (proposed).

**Career.** Mercado grew up in Tacloban and covered the reconstruction that followed Typhoon Haiyan (real, 2013) as a young reporter. Her work examines public-private disaster contracts, coastal reconstruction and maritime supply chains, built on local sources and Filipino priorities. Her reporting on the aftermath of the 2022 Strait Lantern Disruption made her known regionally.

**What people say.** Fearless to her readers; a nuisance to some contractors and local officials. She has received threats and published anyway.

**What the record shows.** A body of careful, documented reporting. Her stories are entry points for international campaigns that do not depend on American institutions.

**Relationships.** Professional friendship with **Elias Ward**; source relationships with fishing cooperatives and coast guard personnel.

**At the table.**
- **Contact:** Will trade information with characters who can help her verify claims abroad.
- **Records:** Her reconstruction-contract series; Strait Lantern reporting.
- **Hook:** A reconstruction contractor she investigated has reappeared under a new name, bidding on maritime communications work.

### Adewale Bako — the engineer of repair

**Born:** 1975 (proposed). **Home region:** Born in Ibadan, Nigeria; works between Lagos and international conferences (proposed).
**Affiliations:** Civil-infrastructure engineer and consultant; frequent conference speaker (proposed).

**Career.** Bako has spent his career on water, power and transport systems in West Africa and elsewhere. He argues for repairable systems, locally trained technicians and transparent maintenance contracts, and against dependence on proprietary continuity systems that only their vendors can fix. His talks are widely shared among engineers.

**What people say.** Inspiring to young engineers and local officials; a sharp critic in the eyes of vendors.

**What the record shows.** Engineering papers, talks and project reports. He was a prominent voice in the 2023 sovereignty debates over infrastructure investment.

**Relationships.** Allied with **Leena Okafor** on maintenance records; has publicly debated Cindrix and Vantrell Group representatives.

**At the table.**
- **Contact:** Will help any community that wants to understand and maintain its own systems.
- **Records:** His conference talks; a maintenance-contract template he publishes freely.
- **Hook:** A city he advised cannot get the source documentation for a system it paid for.

### Yuki Tanabe — the teacher of signals

**Born:** 1973 (proposed). **Home region:** Sendai, Japan; spends part of each year in the US Pacific Northwest (proposed).
**Affiliations:** Disaster-communications instructor; author of public training materials (proposed).

**Career.** Tanabe trained volunteer radio teams after the 2011 Tōhoku earthquake and tsunami (real) and took part in international follow-ups to the fictional 2012 Cascadia Continuity Review. Their freely available training material on interoperable emergency communication is used by volunteer teams and municipal responders in many countries, including ORG-03-style networks.

**What people say.** Universally liked among radio volunteers. Rarely controversial.

**What the record shows.** Training manuals, workshop recordings and exercise reports.

**Relationships.** Co-author with **Dr. Amara Bell**; has trained teams in Calder County (proposed).

**At the table.**
- **Contact:** Runs workshops; a character who completes one has met Tanabe.
- **Records:** Their training manual; exercise after-action reports.
- **Hook:** During a training exercise, Tanabe's team picked up a scheduled transmission in an old format they could not identify.

## Part III — Regional figures: Texas and Heritage Ranch

### Elias Mercer — founder of Heritage Ranch

**Born:** 1964. **Home region:** Rural west-central Texas.
**Affiliations:** Founder and recognised leader of Heritage Ranch.

**Career.** A former military logistics officer, Mercer bought an existing 135-acre working cattle ranch in 2008 expressly to build a survivalist compound. He planned residences, storage, workshops and preparedness infrastructure from the start, and kept the cattle operation as a source of both food and income. The community he built emphasises faith, work, practical skills, local charity and voluntary participation.

**What people say.** Admirers call him a serious community builder who helps his neighbours. Critics point to the ranch's social isolation and the concentration of authority around him and his family.

**What the record shows.** Decades of public talks, local press coverage and community events. At the 2024 regional preparedness forum he praised neighbouring communities and voluntary mutual aid. He has repeatedly said people should be free to leave and that help should never require conversion or submission. Nothing in the public record establishes the ranch as a criminal enterprise.

**In January 2026.** Sixty-one or sixty-two. Still the recognised founder, but delegating much of daily life to his children.

**At the table.**
- **Contact:** Hospitable to respectful visitors; enjoys talking about cattle, logistics and scripture.
- **Records:** Recordings of his talks; local newspaper profiles; the ranch's founding documents (as filed with the county, proposed).

### Nathan Mercer — security and logistics

**Born:** 1985. **Affiliations:** Heritage Ranch; organiser of security, emergency drills, transport and access.

**Public profile.** By 2026 Nathan runs much of the ranch's security and logistics. He is competent, organised and increasingly uncompromising in what he says. He argues that porous boundaries invite theft and disorder. At the 2024 forum he called for stronger perimeter and supply-chain control. Some longtime members worry that his idea of security is more coercive than his father's. In Core he operates within the law and within his father's authority.

**Known disputes.** A public disagreement with **Caleb Voss** over access restrictions on shared roads.

**At the table.** Characters may meet him at a preparedness workshop or a road-access meeting. He respects competence and dislikes being questioned in public.

### Rebecca Mercer — teacher and administrator

**Born:** 1989. **Affiliations:** Heritage Ranch; teaching, worship, recruitment, family programmes and internal administration.

**Public profile.** Rebecca is a skilled teacher, organiser and speaker. Supporters credit her with making the community more organised; critics argue that dissent is becoming less welcome. At the 2024 forum she argued that membership carries obligations beyond those of an ordinary club or church. Claims about her private intentions remain unproven in the public record.

**At the table.** Characters may meet her at a recruitment event, a school board discussion or a community dinner. She is persuasive and listens carefully.

### Pastor Miriam Cole — independent minister

**Born:** 1958 (proposed). **Home:** A small town near the ranch (proposed).

Cole leads an independent rural congregation. She has cooperated with Heritage Ranch on food and storm-relief drives and publicly disagrees with any suggestion that religious conformity should be a condition of receiving help. She is trusted across the county because she helps everyone.

**At the table.** **Contact:** a natural first stop for characters new to the area. **Records:** her congregation's relief logs, which record who helped whom during storms and the pandemic.

### Caleb Voss — cooperative organiser

**Born:** 1977 (proposed). **Home:** A third-generation ranching family adjoining the county road (proposed).

Voss organises a ranching cooperative that shares equipment and labour among independent farms. He defends his neighbours' autonomy and fair access to roads and markets. His public disagreement with Nathan Mercer over access restrictions is a grounded local conflict, not a conspiracy.

**At the table.** **Contact:** knows every road and every family. **Records:** cooperative minutes; correspondence with the county about road access.

### Sofia Valdez — emergency manager

**Born:** 1981 (proposed). **Home:** County seat near the ranch (proposed).

Valdez is a county-level emergency-management specialist and regular regional conference speaker. She welcomes volunteer capacity, including the ranch's, while insisting on inspection, accountability and lawful limits. She distinguishes legitimate volunteer preparedness from unaccountable private enforcement, and says so in public.

**At the table.** **Contact:** the official most likely to listen to characters with a public-safety concern. **Records:** county exercise reports; correspondence with volunteer organisations.
