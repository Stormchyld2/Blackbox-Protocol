# Blackbox Protocol: Corporate Locations and Facilities
## The places where institutions become tangible, 2026

**Setting:** Core (2026), with conditional Signal Lost and Dead Zone continuities.  
**Status:** Location-development dossier. The seven headquarters cities recorded in 05E are **established working canon**. Other sites, addresses, site names and local operating arrangements described below are **proposed setting locations**, available for future adoption or individual campaigns; they are not retroactive historical facts.  
**Audience:** GM reference with player-facing descriptions that may be used openly. No new Looking Glass or Blackbox classified facilities are established.  
**Companions:** `05C-HALDEN-INSTITUTE-AND-CINDRIX-ORIGINS-GM.md`, `05D-HALDEN-FAMILY-OWNERSHIP-AND-INSTITUTE-FACTIONS-GM.md`, `05E-OTHER-CORPORATE-POWERS-AND-RETAILER-BIOS.md`, `05F-CORPORATE-RELATIONSHIPS-RIVALRIES-AND-INFLUENCE.md`, `notes/CORPORATE-AND-RETAIL-GUIDE.md`, `notes/CORE-WORLD-AND-CAMPAIGN-FOUNDATION.md` and `notes/ORGANIZATIONS.md`.

## I. What a corporate presence actually means

The largest companies in the setting occupy far more than office towers. Their work happens in warehouses that receive freight before dawn, laboratories with carefully controlled inventories, service counters where a customer needs one obscure replacement part, and regional offices whose staff are accountable to both distant executives and the people who live nearby.

A headquarters determines policy, but it rarely determines what happens when a truck breaks down outside a rural hospital. The people who can resolve that problem may work at a loading dock, a repair bench or a local dispatch desk. A useful corporate location therefore needs a reason to exist before it needs a secret. Its personnel should have jobs, schedules, budgets, neighbors and ordinary problems. Security should reflect the work conducted there. An office lobby is not a fortress; a clinical research laboratory has real access controls for reasons that have nothing to do with conspiracy.

The places in this dossier are designed to be encountered. Characters may apply for employment, inspect a delivery, attend a public hearing, interview a witness, repair equipment, negotiate access or seek shelter. Their significance can change with a campaign, but the setting should not assume that every building conceals a classified installation.

## II. Cindrix: a network rather than a single place

Cindrix began in 1984 as a wholesale supplier of electrical and communications equipment. Its later growth into a corporation with retail, logistics, cloud, communications, energy, infrastructure and other divisions did not erase that origin. Much of its physical footprint would be recognizable to anyone familiar with distribution and utility contracting: freight gates, fleet maintenance yards, equipment cages, customer-service offices and the unglamorous buildings where networks are monitored.

### The regional fulfillment center — proposed site type

A Cindrix fulfillment center serves a defined geographic area, receiving shipments from suppliers and redistributing stock to customers, commercial clients and smaller delivery stations. Its daily life is governed by arrival windows, inventory discrepancies, vehicle availability, labor schedules and safety procedures. A damaged pallet or mislabeled medical shipment may matter more to local staff than anything being discussed at corporate headquarters.

**Player-facing use:** Characters might work here, collect a shipment, interview an employee, investigate theft or coordinate emergency deliveries. A warehouse is not automatically a military installation. Restricted spaces should correspond to hazardous equipment, high-value stock, employee safety or actual contractual obligations.

**Possible tension:** A local manager has been instructed to reserve scarce stock for a large institutional customer while several smaller buyers have urgent needs. The instruction may be lawful and commercially sensible. Whether it is defensible in a specific emergency depends on facts the players can uncover.

### The infrastructure service district — proposed site type

A Cindrix Infrastructure or Communications district office might share a yard with contractors, service vehicles and stores of replacement cable, switching equipment or power components. Technicians understand local terrain and legacy installations that senior management has never seen. The district can be essential to a community while remaining a source of frustration over billing, delays or contract restrictions.

**Player-facing use:** A repair job, safety inspection, records request or dispute over access to a utility easement. A county may contract with Cindrix, a competitor or an independent operator; no nationwide monopoly is implied.

### Corporate offices and restricted projects

Corporate headquarters, board offices and research divisions should be located only after their organizational and geographic continuity is reconciled with 05C–05D. A Cindrix Research building does not automatically house Cindrix Advanced Systems, and neither is automatically part of a government-led Blackbox program. A proposed classified site requires its own GM-only authorization, chronology and chain of custody rather than an unexplained basement beneath an ordinary office.

The Halden Institute is a distinct nonprofit institution. Its facilities should include the ordinary functions of a research organization—offices, meeting rooms, archives, project workspaces and appropriate oversight—without conflating institute property with Cindrix corporate assets.

## III. Veydran Group: the geography of controlled knowledge

**Established headquarters: Cambridge, Massachusetts.** Veydran's location places it in a plausible environment of universities, hospitals, specialist suppliers and scientific talent. The setting does not claim that any real institution participates in Veydran's fictional activities.

### Cambridge research headquarters — proposed facility profile

A believable Veydran headquarters combines administration with research leadership, legal and licensing teams, technical meeting space and laboratories whose access depends on their actual work. Scientists, safety officers, facilities engineers and administrative staff have different reasons to enter. Some information is commercially sensitive; some is protected because it concerns patients or regulated studies.

Dr. Celia Veydran's executive responsibilities and Dr. Priya Menon's scientific responsibilities may intersect here, but a board decision does not substitute for laboratory validation. Independent ethics review should have its own channels and records. The most consequential dispute in a building may concern whether a study can proceed at all.

**Player-facing use:** Research employment, a licensing dispute, an academic collaboration, a safety inquiry or the search for a qualified expert. Visitors should encounter an ordinary professional institution before they encounter any mystery.

### Manufacturing and specialist distribution — proposed regional sites

Veydran products cannot simply travel from a laboratory bench to a patient. Depending on the product, manufacturing requires validation, quality assurance, controlled storage and traceable shipping. A regional specialist distribution center could hold temperature-sensitive stock or replacement components, while a separate contracted manufacturer performs production. Those facilities need not be owned by Veydran.

**Possible tension:** A shipment is delayed because a temperature record is incomplete. The receiving clinic needs it urgently; the quality team will not certify material it cannot verify. Characters can investigate the actual record and find alternatives without assuming sabotage.

## IV. Vantrell Group: where ownership becomes paperwork

**Established headquarters: New York, New York.** Vantrell's offices would look less like a vast industrial complex than a collection of professional departments whose decisions affect distant buildings and institutions. Investment analysts, risk officers, lawyers, accountants and compliance staff may work in the same building while having sharply different information and obligations.

### New York executive headquarters — proposed facility profile

The headquarters houses the parent company's leadership and functions appropriate to a publicly traded holding group. Julian Vantrell is CEO, Marissa Chen is CFO, and Eleanor Sloane chairs an independent board. Board committees require records and deliberation; the CEO does not personally command every asset associated with the group's investment portfolio.

**Player-facing use:** A shareholder meeting, a regulatory inquiry, a financing negotiation, a records dispute or an interview with an analyst. An ordinary visitor may enter a reception or meeting area without gaining access to confidential client files.

### Regional underwriting and property offices — proposed site types

A Vantrell-affiliated lender or insurer may have a regional office serving hospitals, utilities, manufacturers or property owners. A separately incorporated portfolio company might maintain a building under its own name, with its own managers and board. An address on a Vantrell investment report does not prove the property is operated by Vantrell personnel.

**Possible tension:** A town believes a vacant industrial property is controlled by Vantrell, while records show a chain of separate entities, leases and secured claims. The characters must determine who can lawfully authorize repairs or entry. The answer may be inconvenient without being fraudulent.

## V. Ridgeline Outfitters: the store as a source of judgment

**Established headquarters: Boise, Idaho.** Ridgeline began with Jack Hensley and Susan Whitcomb's practical knowledge of backcountry travel. A proposed Boise flagship should preserve that character: well-used demonstration gear, maps, equipment repair, staff training and customers who want advice before spending money. Nora Hensley's national leadership has not made the company a faceless warehouse operation.

A **regional distribution warehouse** would support stores without eliminating their discretion to stock equipment suited to local terrain. A **field instruction program** could partner with independent guides rather than pretending every instructor is a corporate employee.

**Player-facing use:** Equipment consultation, trip planning, a missing-person inquiry, an outdoor workshop or a rescue organized with local authorities. A store manager may close early to help stranded travelers and then face legitimate questions about inventory, liability and payroll.

## VI. Sentinel Supply Co.: the obligations behind professional equipment

**Established headquarters: Colorado Springs, Colorado.** Sentinel's origins in emergency-services logistics and civilian field contracting make institutional purchasing central to its identity. Its proposed headquarters should contain sales, procurement, contract-compliance and equipment-support functions rather than an invented operational command bunker.

An **inspection and distribution depot** can receive communications equipment, protective clothing and field medical supplies, verify specifications and dispatch orders. A **demonstration and training site** may allow customers to test equipment and learn its limitations. Neither facility automatically conducts armed operations or exercises governmental authority.

**Player-facing use:** Equipment verification, responder training, an urgent procurement dispute or a recall investigation. Teresa Cole's operational decisions and Daniel Reeves's board oversight can disagree without making one of them secretly corrupt.

## VII. Ironfield Supply: parts, repair and the industrial everyday

**Established headquarters: Columbus, Ohio.** Ironfield's roots in Walter Briggs's machinery business explain why the company has a substantial presence beyond consumer retail. A proposed Columbus industrial parts center might hold bearings, fittings, tools, welding consumables and maintenance components, while regional branches support contractors and municipal crews.

A contractor-service counter should feel different from a general marketplace. Customers bring measurements, failed parts and incomplete specifications; staff often solve problems by knowing what will fit. The company's public ownership creates real reporting and inventory pressures, even where local managers have practical discretion.

**Player-facing use:** Finding a replacement part before a water system fails, checking a supplier invoice, repairing a vehicle or identifying a tool used at a worksite. The challenge may be scarcity or error, not malice.

## VIII. Harvest & Homestead: a business rooted in producers

**Established headquarters: Des Moines, Iowa.** Harvest & Homestead grew from Arthur and Martha Fenwick's seed business into a national agricultural and homesteading supplier. Its proposed facilities should reflect that history: customer and grower-support offices, seasonal distribution, preservation-equipment supply and relationships with independent agricultural producers.

A **seed distribution facility** has practical concerns—storage conditions, labeling, seasonal demand and traceability. A **regional grower-support office** might help farmers obtain equipment and supplies without claiming ownership of their farms or cooperatives.

**Player-facing use:** A disputed seed lot, a cooperative purchasing meeting, livestock-supply shortages or planning a community food program. Laura Fenwick may have to balance a family company's finances against promises made to long-standing suppliers.

## IX. CircuitPoint: the knowledge behind the counter

**Established headquarters: Austin, Texas.** CircuitPoint's origins in computer components and small-business systems work make technical service a credible part of its national footprint. A proposed Austin headquarters may include merchandising, vendor management, technical standards and support departments. Sophie Lin leads the company; individual stores and service departments still have to solve customers' immediate problems.

A **regional electronics depot** handles imported components and finished equipment. A **technical-service center** diagnoses faults, replaces parts and documents repairs. Access to proprietary devices and customer data should be governed by ordinary authorization, not assumed unrestricted technical power.

**Player-facing use:** Recovering damaged records, identifying a counterfeit component, restoring a radio link or finding someone who can repair an obsolete device. A skilled technician's judgment may be the most important resource in the building.

## X. Regional presence and the existing campaign locations

The Core world foundation already proposes **Pittsburgh**, **Washington, D.C.**, **Manila**, **London**, **Nairobi** and the fictional **Briar Hollow, West Virginia** as campaign starting points. They are opportunities to place corporate activity in a lived-in world, **not evidence that every company maintains a branch in each city**.

**Pittsburgh:** A contractor-service location, parts supplier or regional technical office would fit the established infrastructure-modernization story. A proposed Cindrix or Ironfield presence must not displace the independent contractors or predetermine the investigation's culprit.

**Washington, D.C.:** Public hearings, regulatory filings, professional associations and contract offices offer more plausible corporate encounters than an invented secret headquarters. Any liaison office should remain subject to ordinary legal and institutional boundaries.

**Manila:** Shipping, local procurement and communications partnerships can connect the fictional firms to the established harbor-ledger story. Local companies, investigators and authorities remain the protagonists of their own region; a foreign corporate office is neither necessary nor automatically in charge.

**London:** A Vantrell-related financing or corporate-records question may suit the Ledger House scenario, but no specific subsidiary, property or controlling interest is fixed here.

**Nairobi:** A technology or emergency-communications partnership may involve an outside supplier, but the local research and procurement actors already established in the Core foundation lead the work.

**Briar Hollow:** A hardware store, delivery route, field technician or small supplier can be relevant to the town's land and utility disputes. Its county remains intentionally unnamed. Do not insert a major classified facility or a corporate monopoly merely because the town is remote.

These placements are scenario options. Actual addresses, ownership, staffing, contracts and local political consequences belong in later regional dossiers.

## XI. What players can learn at a facility

A useful site has several layers of information. The sign, opening hours, public entrance, visible staff and stated purpose are usually discoverable. Contracts, maintenance logs, shipment records and employment information may require lawful requests, cooperation or investigation. Trade secrets, protected personal information and restricted government material require their own access rules and justifications.

GM preparation should distinguish **what is true**, **what a particular employee believes**, **what the company says publicly** and **what the characters can actually establish**. A rumor about an underground laboratory is not evidence that one exists. An inaccessible door may lead to a server room, a hazardous-material store or a records archive. Sometimes the mundane explanation is the correct one.

For a developed location, record its operating entity, purpose, local management, staff and visitors, physical dependencies, public access, records, hazards, neighbors and relationships to nearby institutions. If a site becomes a playable faction asset, its staff, stock and facilities must be represented through existing F23 ledgers rather than an invented influence rating. F17, F18 and F24 remain authoritative for equipment, commerce and persistence. No numerical staffing, inventory, security, travel-time or pricing defaults are established here.

## XII. The buildings after the systems change

**Signal Lost (2031–2045):** A distribution center may still have stock but no reliable payments or fuel. A laboratory may preserve power while losing a specialist supply chain. A regional office may continue serving customers after contact with headquarters becomes intermittent. Some facilities close; others are adapted by employees, governments or communities. The timing and cause vary by campaign.

**Dead Zone (2046 onward):** A familiar logo may survive long after the corporation that installed it. A repair shop can become a settlement workshop. A seed warehouse may be administered by growers. An electronics service center might preserve skills that a new generation must learn from scratch. A former corporate office may hold useful records but no enforceable authority. Neither a company's former ownership nor the presence of its signage determines who controls a site now.

The most important continuity is human. Buildings can be abandoned, repaired or occupied by strangers. Knowledge survives only when someone teaches it, and institutions endure only when people find reasons to keep them.

## XIII. Editorial and continuity register

**Fixed by existing 05E working canon:** Veydran headquarters in Cambridge; Vantrell in New York; Ridgeline in Boise; Sentinel in Colorado Springs; Ironfield in Columbus; Harvest & Homestead in Des Moines; CircuitPoint in Austin. Existing founders, boards, ownership structures and house brands remain unchanged.

**Not fixed here:** Cindrix or Halden Institute present headquarters; street addresses; named corporate campuses; site counts; exact regional footprints; ownership of any specific warehouse, laboratory or property; classified locations; named local managers; new legal entities or contracts.

**Proposed for future development:** Facility archetypes and the potential use of existing campaign regions described above. These proposals are intended to be useful in play without silently adding settled canon.

**Next production pass:** Select any additional canonical sites only after checking the history and regional sourcebooks; then write individual location dossiers with public descriptions, staff, operating constraints, accessible records and scenario-specific GM information. Keep those dossiers separate from the historical corporate biographies.
