# Signal Lost historical development, 2031–2045
**Status:** Era expansion using approved dates; specific global trigger, failure sequence, and detailed regional outcomes remain subject to reconciliation with the existing Signal Lost design notes. Do not treat one speculative mechanism as established canon.

## 2031–2033: strain becomes persistent
Failures in supply, payment, communication, and institutional coordination occur unevenly. Some cities and regions remain comparatively stable; others face recurring shortages and disputed authority. National governments and international organizations continue to act, though their reach varies. People try repair, migration, mutual aid, and political negotiation before abandoning established institutions.

**Heritage Settlement:** Nathan and Rebecca Mercer have assumed practical control after the succession begun in 2028 and the 2029 renaming. Their compound, founded on the original 135-acre cattle ranch, has useful food, livestock, maintenance, and storage capacity. They offer assistance but increasingly demand membership obligations and compliance. The proposed South Granary Dispute of 2033 exposes tensions between relief and control.

## 2034–2036: competing institutions
A working date of **2034** marks Elias Mercer's death. Official Settlement memorials emphasize his vision of discipline while dissenting elders remember his insistence on voluntary membership. In wider society, municipalities, utilities, hospitals, private contractors, and local volunteers compete to keep services operating. The first successful community responses are not necessarily the most heavily armed; maintenance skills and trusted distribution matter.

**People:** Dr. Celia Rourke, a fictional rural physician, resists conditioning medical aid on political affiliation. Jonah Price, a fictional Settlement quartermaster, records both successful food distribution and politically motivated exclusions.

## 2037–2039: legitimacy contests
Communities argue over who may levy service obligations, adjudicate disputes, protect roads, and control shared stores. Some emergency councils become democratic local governments; others harden into patronage networks. The proposed **First Steward Walkout (2037)** creates a network of former Settlement administrators who favor independent local governance.

**Conflict:** Rival accounts of the Settlement's food and security agreements circulate among displaced families. Records may be incomplete, deliberately altered, or simply lost.

## 2039–2042: routes and regional influence
The proposed **County Road Conflicts** unfold across the fictional Texas county surrounding Heritage. Disputes over escorted shipments, road access, tolls, raids, and reprisals produce localized violence. Nathan's forces become more disciplined and more coercive. Rebecca's stewards introduce standardized hearings and duties. The conflicts should be written as multiple incidents with independent causes, not one battle that explains every territorial change.

**Elsewhere:** Coastal communities may depend on boat repair and fishing; highland settlements on passes, timber, and small hydroelectric plants; inland towns on grain stores and clinics. These are regional examples, not a single worldwide script.

## 2043–2045: successor authorities
The proposed **Settlement Compact** formalizes unequal protection and labor arrangements across Heritage-affiliated villages. Other regions follow different paths: surviving elected councils, cooperatives, corporate enclaves, trade associations, and opportunistic armed regimes. The original Blackbox legacy may leave infrastructure and archives, but no one program dictates the world's political future.

## Regional lenses
**United States:** Patchwork outcomes across cities, agricultural districts, coastal ports, and remote regions. Real state boundaries remain geographic reference points; fictional successor control must be mapped separately.
**Europe:** Cross-border supply coordination, local energy and transport, and contested authority vary by country.
**East and Southeast Asia:** Maritime commerce, industrial supply chains, local disaster experience, and state capacity shape different trajectories.
**South Asia:** Urban density, agriculture, water systems, and regional trade produce multiple responses.
**Africa:** Diverse national and local histories; no continental collapse narrative. Local engineers, cooperatives, and civic groups retain agency.
**Latin America and Caribbean:** Distinct port, agricultural, energy, and municipal networks face different pressures.
**Oceania:** Island logistics, maritime travel, and local food systems become especially important.

## Public-history method
Record an incident only when it has a date or range, location, involved communities, sources, consequences, and a plausible means of learning about it. Exact causes and rates of system failure require confirmation from the authoritative era rules before publication.
