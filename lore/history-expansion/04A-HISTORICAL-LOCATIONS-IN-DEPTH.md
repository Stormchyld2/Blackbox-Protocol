# Blackbox Protocol: Places of History
## An in-depth player-facing gazetteer of the Core world, 1 January 2026

**Setting:** Blackbox Protocol (Core)
**Status:** Expanded draft. Supplements `04-HISTORICAL-LOCATIONS.md`, the places section of `lore/core/BLACKBOX-PROTOCOL-ALTERNATE-HISTORY.md`, `notes/CORE-WORLD-AND-CAMPAIGN-FOUNDATION.md` and `notes/REGIONS-AND-SETTLEMENTS.md`; replaces none of them. Those sources win any conflict. Points of interest, residents and incidents marked **(proposed)** are new in this draft.
**Audience:** Player-safe, as of 1 January 2026.
**Fiction notice:** Real cities, regions and installations are used as geography and public context only. No real institution, agency, military unit, company or community is implied to have taken part in fictional programs or wrongdoing. No coordinates are invented, and no fictional region is identified with a real county.
**Rules notice:** Setting history only. Service ratings, where mentioned, come from the regions file and are not changed here.

---

## How to use this gazetteer

Each entry follows the gazetteer standard set in `04`:

- **Status** (real or fictional) and **era** coverage;
- **Map reference** where one exists, or a note that none is established;
- **Physical features**;
- **Governance**;
- **Economic role**;
- **Named residents** (fictional only);
- **Two or more historical incidents** tied to the place;
- **Publicly discoverable sources** a character can use.

Entries are grouped by how they appear in play: real anchor cities, fictional historical designations, and fictional campaign locations.

Core is the era that sets up the other two. Every place here is a functioning place in 2026. The gazetteer notes, without spoilers, what each place depends on, because those dependencies are what later campaigns test. Nothing here predicts what happens to any of them.

## Part I — Real anchor cities and regions

These places exist. Characters can walk their real streets, visit their real archives and meet fictional residents there. Invented meetings, offices and incidents are always identified as invented.

### Washington, D.C., United States — the Oversight Corridor

**Status:** Real. **Era:** Core, with archival roots back to 1962.
**Physical features:** The federal core, museums, research libraries and the neighbourhoods beyond them, where most residents actually live.
**Governance:** Federal government; District government.
**Economic role:** Government, contracting, advocacy, law, journalism, research.
**Named residents (fictional):** **Dana Okafor**, principled committee counsel; **Victor Hale**, retired procurement specialist who rationalises a past omission; **Nia Park**, reporter who verifies before publishing (all from the Core world foundation); **Nadia Serrano**, who lives in the region (proposed).
**Historical incidents (fictional):**
- Committee hearings during the 1996–1997 Civilian Boundary Debate.
- Harrowgate hearings and prosecutions, 2016–2018.
- US sessions of the Greybridge Hearings, 2024.
- An oversight committee receiving conflicting testimony about an old continuity-related contract (2025–2026, Core world foundation).
**Sources:** Hearing transcripts; court dockets; national archives; advocacy-group reports; newspaper archives.
**At the table:** Authenticate a document, protect a lawful whistleblower, identify a forged leak, represent an accused official.

### Brussels, Belgium

**Status:** Real. **Era:** Core.
**Physical features:** A compact capital housing international institutions, think tanks, embassies and a large press corps.
**Governance:** Belgian federal, regional and city governments; host to international bodies.
**Economic role:** Diplomacy, policy, law, lobbying, media.
**Historical incidents (fictional):**
- Parliamentary challenges to the Meridian Accords from European members, 2004–2006 (debated in national capitals; Brussels-based commentators led the public argument, proposed).
- European data-protection and procurement debates during the 2023 Sovereignty Reviews.
**Rule:** Invented Blackbox-related meetings in Brussels must be distinguished from real EU or NATO proceedings, which have no role in fictional programs.
**Sources:** Policy papers; press archives; conference programmes.

### Geneva, Switzerland

**Status:** Real. **Era:** Core.
**Physical features:** A lakeside city of humanitarian and diplomatic institutions, conference centres and hotels.
**Economic role:** Diplomacy, humanitarian coordination, finance, conferences.
**Historical incidents (fictional):**
- A recurring professional conference on emergency logistics where **Dr. Amara Bell** and **Adewale Bako** have both given keynotes (proposed).
- Side meetings during humanitarian forums at which Meridian Accords implementation was discussed (proposed).
**Rule:** A plausible conference setting; no claim that any real organisation endorsed Blackbox.
**Sources:** Conference proceedings; humanitarian reports; press coverage.

### Berlin, Germany

**Status:** Real. **Era:** Core, with Cold War layers.
**Physical features:** Remnants of the Wall, Cold War bunkers and infrastructure, archives of divided-era institutions, a modern capital built on top.
**Economic role:** Government, culture, technology, tourism.
**Historical incidents:**
- **1989 (real):** The Wall opens; the end of division changes every security assumption.
- **Early 1990s (fictional):** Planners worry about inherited infrastructure, archives and displaced technical staff, part of the climate in which the Blackbox Initiative is proposed.
**Sources:** Real public archives of the divided city; museums; fictional memoirs of continuity planners who visited (proposed).
**At the table:** Archival research, heritage tourism, and the physical texture of Cold War continuity planning.

### Manila, Philippines — the Harbor Ledger

**Status:** Real. **Era:** Core.
**Physical features:** A dense, dynamic capital on Manila Bay; port and ferry terminals; markets; universities; media offices.
**Governance:** National government; Metro Manila cities; barangays at the neighbourhood level.
**Economic role:** Commerce, shipping, government, education, media.
**Named residents (fictional):** **Dr. Isabel Reyes**, communications researcher; **Ramon dela Cruz**, port safety investigator; **Celina Bautista**, cooperative organiser protecting fishing livelihoods (Core world foundation); **Rina Mercado**, investigative reporter (proposed base).
**Historical incidents:**
- **1997 (real):** The Asian financial crisis reaches the Philippines.
- **2013 (real):** Typhoon Haiyan devastates the Visayas; reconstruction contracts become a subject of reporting.
- **2022 (fictional):** The Strait Lantern Disruption; Manila debates who should be trusted with regional communications.
- **2025–2026 (fictional):** Disputed cargo records threaten a locally managed coastal communications upgrade (Core world foundation).
**Principle:** Filipino characters lead local decisions. A foreign contractor is a possible partner or obstacle, never the centre of the story.
**Sources:** Port records; Philippine press archives; university research; Mercado's reporting.

## Part II — Fictional historical designations

These names belong to public history, but they are **designations, not settled map locations**. Their placement must be fixed in dedicated dossiers before anyone puts them on a map.

### Port Meridian

**Status:** Fictional. **Era:** Core; dispute in 1998.
**Map reference:** None established. An international port; country and jurisdiction pending a location dossier.
**Physical features (proposed):** A deep-water harbour; container terminals built in the 1998 modernisation and still working in 2026; older break-bulk piers; a union hall; a working waterfront neighbourhood.
**Governance:** A port authority under national and municipal law (details pending).
**Economic role:** Shipping, logistics, emergency logistics capacity.
**Named residents (fictional):** **Daniel Rusk**, retired crane operator and union representative.
**Historical incidents:**
- 1998 contract dispute; bid protests; worker reassignments.
- 2000–2003 records-access litigation.
- 2024 Rusk testimony at Greybridge.
**Sources:** Union archives; shipping manifests; litigation records; redacted review summaries.
**At the table:** The best-documented public entry point to the Blackbox legacy.

### Harrowgate

**Status:** Fictional designation. **Era:** Core; scandal in 2016.
**Map reference:** None. The name attaches to the scandal and its proceedings, not necessarily to one town.
**What it is:** The label for the 2016 unlawful-surveillance scandal, its hearings, prosecutions and reforms.
**Sources:** Court records; hearing transcripts; Elias Ward's series.
**Rule:** Do not assign it a jurisdiction without a location dossier.

### Greybridge

**Status:** Fictional designation. **Era:** Core; hearings in 2024.
**Map reference:** None. The name identifies the hearing series in several countries, not a city.
**Sources:** Public transcripts; committee reports.
**Rule:** Do not invent a city or national venue without a location record.

## Part III — Fictional campaign locations

### Briar Hollow, West Virginia

**Status:** Fictional town in a real state; county intentionally unnamed. **Era:** Core starting town (approved).
**Map reference:** None established. Appalachian West Virginia.
**Physical features:** Forest roads, a ridge overlook, an old rail spur, former industrial sites, a small town centre.
**Governance:** Town government; countywide authorities, courts and records appear when needed. The county is never named.
**Economic role:** Local businesses, public services, remnants of industry, commuting.
**Named residents (fictional, Core world foundation):** **Sheriff Helen Mercer**, accountable to the community (no relation to the Texas Mercers); **Owen Pike**, surveyor whose records conflict with an online map; **Ruth Calder**, local historian who resists sensational claims (no relation to Calder County).
**Points of interest:** County courthouse; volunteer fire hall; ridge overlook; old rail spur; family-owned diner.
**Historical incidents:**
- A historic utility easement recorded decades ago, now disputed (Core world foundation).
- A land transfer and road closure that generate contradictory rumours (2025–2026).
- A faded reference to an old federal continuity contract in county records, an optional thread (Core world foundation).
**Sources:** County property records; Ruth Calder's local history files; the closure notice; old maps.
**Dependencies (for later-era reference, not prediction):** one main access road, a county utility network and volunteer responders.
**Distinct from:** Calder County, Port Meridian, Heritage Ranch and Zero Day.

### Calder County (Region 1)

**Status:** Fictional region. **Era:** All three; Core state described here.
**Map reference:** Region R1 in the regions file.
**Physical features:** A river valley with farmland and low hills; a state highway and rail line crossing at the county seat; four seasons with snowy winters.
**Infrastructure:** A county grid with one substation per town; a county emergency radio and alert system on a hilltop site; treated river water; a regional hospital.
**Governance:** County government and town councils.
**Economic role:** Grain, dairy, timber, a quarry; farming, light industry, commuters.
**Organisations:** ORG-01 holds the utility and alert contract; ORG-02 supplies guards and trucking; ORG-03 runs the county volunteer corps.
**Settlements:**
- **Fenwick (county seat):** about 12,000 people; town hall, county emergency operations centre, regional hospital of about 60 beds, high school, rail yard, warehouse district. Services all at 5–6. Hooks: Zero Day's briefing site; records fraud; a hospital supply shortage; a quiet rail-yard theft ring.
- **Alder Crossing (farm town):** about 1,800 people; grain elevator, feed store, volunteer fire company, diner, elementary school. Services 5–6 except healthcare 3 (clinic two days a week). Hooks: feed-store gossip; a failing grain elevator; a stranded cross-country driver.
**Historical incidents (proposed):**
- The 1990s installation of the hilltop alert system under a county contract later taken over by ORG-01.
- A spring flood in the late 2010s that tested the volunteer corps and alert system.
- **Yuki Tanabe** running a radio training course for the volunteer corps.
**Sources:** County minutes; ORG-01 contract filings; ORG-03 training records; local newspaper.
**Dependencies:** fuel for the hospital generator, the rail line, the alert contract.

### Aldane Coast (Region 2)

**Status:** Fictional region. **Map reference:** Region R2.
**Physical features:** A sheltered bay, rocky coast, deep-water port and inland marshes; mild, damp climate with autumn storms.
**Infrastructure:** Port cranes and rail spur; municipal grid fed from the regional network; reservoir water; harbour radio station.
**Economic role:** Shipping, fishing, cargo handling, salt, tourism.
**Organisations:** ORG-02 runs port security and trucking; ORG-01 holds the water and port-controls contract; ORG-03 operates the harbour rescue corps.
**Settlements:**
- **Port Aldane:** about 60,000 people; container terminal, ferry, hospital, university campus, dense downtown. Services 5–6; strongly dependent on imports. Hooks: smuggling; a port-controls fault; missing cargo manifests; a research grant dispute.
- **Kestrel Bay:** about 900 people; fish dock, cannery, store, lighthouse, volunteer rescue boat. Services 4–5; healthcare 2. Hooks: a storm-damaged pier; a feud between boat owners; a missing boat.
**Historical incidents (proposed):**
- A storm in the early 2020s that damaged Kestrel Bay's pier and tested the harbour rescue corps.
- A dispute at the university over a research grant tied to port-controls work.
**Dependencies:** imports, the port-controls contract, the reservoirs.
**Distinct from:** Port Meridian. They are separate ports.

### Ridgeback Highlands (Region 3)

**Status:** Fictional region. **Map reference:** Region R3.
**Physical features:** Steep valleys, forest, one paved pass, old mill buildings, cold winters.
**Infrastructure:** A small hydro dam and local grid; one cellular tower; a radio relay on a ridge.
**Economic role:** Timber, water power, small manufacturing, seasonal visitors.
**Organisations:** ORG-01 maintains the dam and relay; ORG-03 runs mountain rescue; ORG-02 appears seasonally.
**Settlement:**
- **Hollis Gap:** about 2,500 people; sawmill, hydro dam, school, small clinic, general store, mountain rescue station. Services 5–6; transport depends on the pass. Hooks: a winter closure; a sawmill accident; hikers lost in a storm.
**Historical incidents (proposed):**
- The dam's construction in the mid-twentieth century and its later refurbishment under contract.
- A winter in the 2010s when the pass closed for nine days and the town ran on its own power.
**Dependencies:** the pass, the dam, the small maintenance crew.

### Heritage Ranch, Texas

**Status:** Fictional compound in a fictional county. **Era:** Founded 2008; Core state described here.
**Map reference:** None. Rural west-central Texas, broadly between the real cities of Abilene and San Angelo. No real county is assigned.
**Founding parcel:** An existing **135-acre working cattle ranch**, bought by Elias Mercer in 2008 expressly to build a survivalist compound. The cattle provide food self-reliance and income to fund construction and operations.
**Physical features in 2026:** A timber entrance sign reading **HERITAGE RANCH**; cattle pasture and handling facilities; residences; a meeting and worship hall; maintenance workshops; food storage; gardens and fields; security and communications infrastructure added over time. Footprints and capacities need a separate map. Land use must stay credible: 135 acres cannot support a county's population.
**Governance:** A private community under applicable law. Elias is the recognised founder; Nathan oversees security and logistics; Rebecca leads teaching, worship and administration.
**Economic role:** Cattle, food production, workshops, local purchases and paid local work, preparedness instruction.
**Named residents:** Elias, Nathan and Rebecca Mercer; members and families.
**Neighbours:** Independent farms and **Caleb Voss**'s cooperative; **Pastor Miriam Cole**'s congregation; county offices and **Sofia Valdez**; volunteers; small businesses.
**Historical incidents:**
- 2008 founding.
- 2012–2015 expansion; storm assistance to neighbours.
- 2016–2019 road and boundary dispute.
- 2020–2022 pandemic help and recruitment debate.
- 2024 regional preparedness forum.
- 2025 access and membership controversy.
**Sources:** County filings; local press; forum recordings; Cole's relief logs; cooperative minutes.
**At the table:** Attend a workshop, report on a zoning hearing, mediate a farm-access dispute, investigate an allegation responsibly or join a relief effort. Players need not oppose the ranch.
