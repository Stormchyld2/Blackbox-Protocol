# Conflicts, scandals, disputes and their aftermath
Public historical register. Entries labeled fictional are invented; criminal allegations remain allegations until an in-world ruling or independently established record.

## Port Meridian Contract Dispute — 1998 (fictional)
**Parties:** Procurement administrators, competing logistics contractors, port workers, legal advocates, and public oversight bodies. **Trigger:** Disputed allocation of emergency logistics capacity and contested access to contractual records. **Public course:** Contract challenges, worker testimony, reporting, and administrative review. **Outcome:** Reputational damage, incomplete transparency, and a precedent for later debates about emergency procurement. **Legacy:** Union archives, shipping manifests, competing witness accounts, and records-access litigation. GM-only misconduct is not revealed here.

## Meridian Accords jurisdiction debate — 2004–2006 (fictional)
**Parties:** Participating governments, legal advisers, emergency managers, and infrastructure providers. **Trigger:** Conflicting requirements for cross-border continuity assistance. **Public course:** Negotiations on data custody, contractor responsibilities, and mutual aid. **Outcome:** A framework whose implementation differs by jurisdiction. **Legacy:** Repeated argument about whether an agreement designed for emergencies creates excessive routine authority.

## Cascadia Continuity Review — 2012 (fictional)
**Parties:** Regional officials, public utilities, technical experts, contractors, and civic groups. **Trigger:** Concern over catastrophic earthquake preparedness and communication interoperability. **Outcome:** Recommended upgrades and an unresolved dispute over control of private technical records. **Legacy:** Engineering papers, budget hearings, and local preparedness plans.

## Harrowgate Affair — 2016 (fictional)
**Parties:** Affected civilians, investigators, unlawful-surveillance participants, legal counsel, journalists, and oversight officials. **Trigger:** Disclosures of improper collection or access. **Outcome:** Public scandal, prosecutions and reforms in the established Core historical foundation; individual responsibility must be drawn from the GM manuscript and subsequent legal dossier rather than generalized to every institution. **Legacy:** Trust deficits, whistleblower debates, evidence preservation disputes.

## Fragmentation Audit — 2017 (fictional)
**Parties:** Auditors, archival custodians, technical operators, and oversight bodies. **Trigger:** Inconsistent record custody and inherited emergency permissions. **Outcome:** Some remediation, disputed residual risks. **Legacy:** An institutional mystery with mundane explanations and genuine misconduct side by side.

## Greybridge Hearings — 2024 (fictional)
**Parties:** Legislators, affected workers, professional witnesses, public-interest advocates, and corporate representatives. **Trigger:** Demands for transparent emergency contracting and information governance. **Outcome:** Contested recommendations; no universal dissolution of the Blackbox legacy. **Legacy:** Public transcripts that PCs can consult.

## Heritage Ranch access controversy — 2025 (fictional)
**Parties:** Mercer leadership, former residents, neighboring landholders, and regional officials. **Trigger:** Complaints about internal discipline, access rules, and social pressure. **Outcome:** Conflicting accounts, community debate, and no automatic finding of criminal guilt. **Legacy:** A playable local dispute with witnesses who have different interests.

## Later-era conflicts (player knowledge varies by date)
**The Mercer Succession (2028 onward, fictional):** Elias loses practical control; Nathan and Rebecca reshape the institution. The 2029 renaming to The Heritage Settlement is a visible break.
**South Granary Dispute (proposed 2033, fictional):** Allocation of food to affiliated and unaffiliated households becomes contentious.
**First Steward Walkout (proposed 2037, fictional):** Administrators resign over compulsory membership and education policy.
**County Road Conflicts (proposed 2039–2042, fictional):** A sequence of disputes over access, shipments, and security becomes violent. Responsibility varies by incident; do not depict all opponents as criminals or all Settlement residents as complicit.
**Settlement Compact (proposed 2043–2045, fictional):** Dependent communities trade autonomy and service obligations for protection and access to resources.

## Conflict design standard
Each detailed adventure treatment must distinguish documented acts, claims, rumor, and GM truth; specify affected civilians and economic consequences; and include multiple nonviolent as well as confrontational avenues for player action.
