# Everyday life, culture and social history
Player-facing setting supplement: Core baseline 2026.

## Home and family
The setting is recognizable contemporary Earth. Households differ by region, income, age, religion, disability, and culture. Rent, mortgage payments, caregiving, school schedules, food prices, transport, and employment remain consequential. A continuity contractor's decisions can affect ordinary people without turning their lives into a conspiracy plot.

## Technology and information
**1960s–1980s:** Television, radio, landlines, paper archives, and industrial control systems coexist with specialized computer networks. Records can be lost through ordinary neglect as easily as deliberate concealment.
**1990s:** Personal computing and the public web change commerce, employment, and research. Y2K planning produces documentation that later historians can consult.
**2000s:** Mobile phones, digital logistics, online marketplaces, and networked finance spread unevenly. Cindrix Market's fictional rise begins in 1998.
**2010s:** Smartphones, social platforms, cloud services, and data-driven advertising create new opportunities for organization and surveillance. Harrowgate makes privacy a public controversy.
**2020–2026:** Remote work, digital public services, supply-chain concerns, and emerging AI tools coexist with cash businesses, paper records, and people who cannot afford reliable access.

## News, entertainment and public memory
Major real-world films, literature, music movements, sports traditions, and cultural debates provide a recognizable background. Do not invent false real-world awards or scandals. Fictional public culture can include **The Meridian Ledger** (proposed independent procurement newsletter), **Countyline Radio** (proposed rural regional call-in program), and **Repair Commons** (proposed volunteer-maintenance publication). These are working fictional media names requiring collision checks before final publication.

Historical scandals are remembered differently: a union member may remember lost work, an engineer a technical failure, a journalist an unanswered records request, and a family the practical consequences. Public memory is not a single official chronology.

## Religion and community
Religious congregations, secular clubs, civic associations, cooperatives, and volunteer networks provide real social support. No religious tradition is inherently a security threat. Heritage Ranch is a **fictional survivalist compound** with its own leadership history; it is not a stand-in for a real denomination or Texas community. Elias's voluntary mutual-aid ideal and his children's later coercive governance must be distinguished.

## Work and trade
Cattle ranching, trucking, healthcare, teaching, maintenance, retail, farming, technology, public service, and skilled trades sustain the world. Characters can pursue labor disputes, family obligations, professional advancement, or community repair without interacting with classified history.

## Emergency preparedness as ordinary culture
People store water and food, attend first-aid classes, maintain generators, learn radio procedures, and volunteer after storms. Preparedness practices vary by budget and environment. Some groups emphasize independence, others neighborhood cooperation; most are not political extremists.

## Era transitions as lived experience
**Signal Lost:** Changes are felt through irregular wages, unavailable replacement parts, rationing arguments, disrupted school routines, and mutual-aid networks, not just dramatic battles.
**Dead Zone:** Historical knowledge survives in family recipes, trade apprenticeships, schoolbooks, memorials, oral histories, and preserved documents. New social orders differ by region. Do not presume everyone has forgotten modern science or institutions.

## Playable cultural artifacts
A family photograph of the original Heritage Ranch sign; a union newsletter about Port Meridian; a Greybridge hearing transcript; an independent farm ledger; a maintenance manual annotated by three generations; a recorded local radio interview; and a school assignment comparing conflicting historical accounts can all reveal history without requiring a secret conspiracy.
