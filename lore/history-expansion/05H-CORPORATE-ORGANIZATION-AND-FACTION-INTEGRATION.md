# Blackbox Protocol: Corporate Organization and Faction Integration
## Authority, loyalty, and the people between them — Core, 2026

**Status:** Working organizational dossier and F23 integration plan. Existing corporate leadership, governance, ownership and ORG-01 through ORG-04 identities are preserved. Proposed internal departments, disputes and campaign examples are not new binding faction records or mechanical rules.  
**Audience:** GM reference. Public organizational facts may be adapted for player-facing material; Halden Institute's classified history remains GM-only.  
**Continuity:** `05C`–`05G` corporate history dossiers; `notes/ORGANIZATIONS.md`; `notes/CORPORATE-AND-RETAIL-GUIDE.md`; `notes/DESIGN-NOTES.md`; `docs/foundation/F01-GAME-IDENTITY.md`.

## I. An organization is not a person

An institution can act consistently for decades without its members agreeing on what it is for. A director protects a budget because she believes the work is indispensable. A technician refuses an instruction because he knows the equipment will fail. A lawyer delays publication because the documents contain private information as well as evidence of misconduct. Their decisions may appear contradictory from outside, but they are not necessarily evidence of separate conspiracies.

The same distinction governs Blackbox Protocol's corporate world. A parent company's board sets direction and appoints leadership. Executives turn those decisions into policy. Divisions compete for funding and staff. Regional managers interpret policy in the circumstances they face. Employees may comply, object, report wrongdoing or leave. Contractors and suppliers have their own obligations. None of these groups can be treated as a single mind.

The faction system must preserve this human scale. A corporation's national reputation does not automatically tell the GM how a particular warehouse manager, research officer or store employee will respond to the characters. Relationships should be established at the level where a decision can actually be made.

## II. Cindrix Corporation: control without unanimity

Cindrix's authority is shaped by its ownership as much as by its size. The Halden family exercises its established influence through **Halden Holdings LLC** and the voting arrangements described in the Halden family dossier. **Thomas Halden** chairs the Cindrix board, and **Adrian Mercer** serves as CEO. Corporate officers remain accountable to the board and to the company's lawful obligations. The Halden Institute is a separate nonprofit with its own trustees and executive director; family influence over both institutions does not make them one organization.

Cindrix's divisions have competing practical interests. Cindrix Market and Logistics depend on efficient purchasing and delivery. Communications and Infrastructure must maintain equipment and service contracts whose failures can affect entire communities. Health has responsibilities associated with medical products and customers. Security, Operations and Response work under different contractual and legal authorities; they are not interchangeable armed branches. Research and Advanced Systems may handle sensitive work, but a division's name does not prove that every project is classified or connected to Blackbox.

A plausible internal disagreement begins with a damaged regional communications installation. A field engineer wants to shut down the affected equipment. A contract manager fears penalties and the loss of a municipal customer. A logistics supervisor can obtain the replacement part but only by delaying other deliveries. The executive office may receive a summary that omits the engineer's warning. No single participant has to be dishonest for the situation to deteriorate; if someone deliberately conceals the warning, the evidence must establish who did so.

**GM application:** Treat the parent company as the continuing institutional identity, but track an active dispute through the relevant division, local site, named decision-maker and contract. An instruction from Cindrix headquarters does not confer authority over independent organizations.

## III. The Halden Institute: three schools, no simple sides

The institute's Board of Trustees, executive director, Research Council and Legal and Ethics Committee retain the functions established in 05D. **Margaret Halden** chairs the trustees, and **Dr. Miriam Calder** is executive director. Its historical schools of thought are useful because they show how an institution's members can disagree about the same founding mission.

The **Stewardship School** emphasizes accountability, legitimate authority and the ability to correct institutional mistakes. The **Intervention School** places greater weight on preventing foreseeable harm before conventional decision-making catches up. The **Institutional Continuity School** seeks to preserve expertise, archives, personnel and the institute's capacity to act. Each has produced useful work and serious errors. Their members do not vote as uniform blocs, and a disagreement over one project need not predict a person's position on another.

These schools are **not new corporate subsidiaries, armed factions or additional F23 organizations by default**. They are internal alignments within an existing institution. If a campaign requires them to act independently—controlling a separate budget, facility, personnel or operational objective—the GM should document that separation explicitly rather than granting each school an invented strength score.

The institute cannot command a government program because it once supplied research to it. Nor can its trustees automatically command Cindrix's employees. Classified records, lawful oversight and personal relationships may create influence, but they do not erase those boundaries.

## IV. Veydran Group: science and commercial responsibility

**Dr. Celia Veydran** leads the private investor-backed company and chairs its nine-director board. **Dr. Priya Menon**, as chief scientific officer, has responsibilities that are not identical to those of the commercial leadership. Independent clinical-ethics and audit committees provide review paths that can complicate executive plans.

The company can be understood through four functions: research and development; clinical, regulatory and ethics review; manufacturing and quality assurance; and commercial licensing and distribution. These are **functional groupings for GM interpretation**, not newly approved named subsidiaries. Their boundaries matter because the scientist who identifies a problem may not control a shipment, while the executive responsible for a customer relationship may lack authority to overrule a safety hold.

A dispute over releasing an experimental treatment can divide conscientious people. Researchers may argue that the evidence is incomplete. Physicians may believe a patient has exhausted other options. Financial officers may worry that abandoning the program will close a laboratory whose other work is valuable. Characters should be able to examine the evidence and advocate for an outcome without discovering that every participant belongs to a hidden faction.

## V. Vantrell Group: capital is not command

**Julian Vantrell** is CEO, **Marissa Chen** CFO, and **Eleanor Sloane** chairs the eleven-director public-company board. The board's risk, audit and investment committees constrain the company's financial decisions. Its portfolio companies retain their own governance and management. A Vantrell investment or loan creates specific rights under law and contract, not unrestricted control over the people who work for a borrower.

For play, distinguish the parent investment office, the financial institution actually making a loan, the company receiving financing and any property-owning entity. These may be different legal persons with different records. A campaign involving a disputed factory sale should establish which entity holds title, which holds a security interest, who manages the property and which public authority can intervene.

Vantrell's internal divisions may disagree over acceptable risk, reputational harm and the time required for an investment to produce returns. A risk officer who rejects a profitable deal may be protecting the firm and its clients. A restructuring specialist may believe an unpopular sale will preserve more jobs than insolvency. Neither claim should be accepted without examining its consequences.

## VI. Independent retailers: five different chains of accountability

**Ridgeline Outfitters** remains privately held by its founding families and management. **Nora Hensley** leads it, while **Susan Whitcomb** chairs an advisory board that does not replace lawful owner or director authority. Store-level knowledge is part of the company's value. An experienced manager may have sound reasons to stock different gear than a national purchasing office recommends.

**Sentinel Supply Co.** is privately held with minority outside investors. **Teresa Cole** directs the company and **Daniel Reeves** chairs its five-director board. Procurement and contract compliance are central to its business. A sales manager cannot independently commit Sentinel to a security operation merely because its customers include security organizations.

**Ironfield Supply** is publicly traded. **Rebecca Briggs** serves as CEO, **Owen Patel** as COO, and independent **Helen Shaw** chairs the nine-director board. Regional purchasing and service decisions occur within company policy. The Briggs family's historical importance does not establish majority ownership or a hereditary right to office.

**Harvest & Homestead** is family-controlled. **Laura Fenwick** leads operations, and **Martha Fenwick** chairs a five-director board with outside directors. Independent growers and cooperatives are partners, not employees or subsidiaries. A conflict between corporate purchasing and a producer's needs should give both parties their own agency.

**CircuitPoint** is publicly traded. **Sophie Lin** is CEO, **David Ross** chief merchandising officer, and independent **Grace Whitaker** chairs its nine-director board. Technical-service staff may have expertise the merchandising office lacks, but expertise does not grant them access to every customer record or corporate system.

These differences should remain visible in adventures. A family owner, an independent board chair, a contract-compliance officer and a regional technician do not answer to the same kind of authority.

## VII. What the F23 record must represent

The existing organization records already establish **ORG-01** as a regional infrastructure contractor, **ORG-02** as a private security and logistics firm, **ORG-03** as a civic emergency volunteer network and **ORG-04** as Cindrix Corporation. Their identities must not be reassigned. In particular, ORG-01's optional *CindrixOps* name does **not** connect it to Cindrix Corporation. Its Zero Day history remains intact.

The approved faction approach measures capacity through **actual ledgers**: members, available labor, facilities, stock and other accountable resources. There is no separate abstract strength rating. The existing clock targets are **4 for minor, 6 for significant, 8 for major and 12 for regional objectives**. These values are cited as existing rules, not changed or assigned to new corporate missions here.

A future corporate faction record should answer concrete questions:

1. **Who is acting?** Identify the legal organization, division or genuinely independent group. Record its existing ID if one has been assigned.
2. **Who can authorize the action?** Identify a responsible office or person and the limits of that authority.
3. **What can it actually use?** Record relevant personnel, labor, equipment, facilities, stock and money using the established systems. A nationwide company cannot automatically use every asset in a local encounter.
4. **What does it want now?** State an objective with a reason, deadline or dependency. If a clock is appropriate, use the existing F23 procedure rather than inventing one.
5. **Who else has a stake?** Include employees, customers, regulators, suppliers, neighbors and rival organizations when relevant.
6. **What do the characters know?** Separate public information, testimony, disputed claims and GM-only facts.
7. **What changes if it succeeds or fails?** Update the relevant records and relationships; do not assume the setting's future is predetermined.

A **department** normally remains part of its parent organization's record. A **regional branch** is a location and operating unit unless its independence is established. A **subsidiary** may be a separate legal entity without needing a separate gameplay faction if it has no independent objective in the campaign. An **informal school of thought** is not a faction merely because its members disagree. These distinctions prevent the system from multiplying organizations without a gameplay purpose.

### Relationship records

For a useful relationship, record the participating entities, the type of tie—contract, financing, ownership, research, employment, procurement or informal cooperation—its scope, duration, public visibility and the authority each party actually holds. Mark **confirmed**, **alleged**, **proposed** or **GM-only** as appropriate. Rivalry should be attached to a disputed interest, not assumed to be permanent hostility between entire companies.

### One incident, several institutions

Suppose a county needs replacement communications equipment. ORG-01 has a maintenance contract. ORG-02 transports equipment under a separate arrangement. ORG-03 volunteers are preparing a temporary communications site. Cindrix Business can supply a component, but the order has not yet been placed.

Each organization retains its own objectives and resources. The county can reject a proposal. A technician can report a safety issue. The volunteers may offer labor but lack authority to enter a restricted site. If a GM adds a new supply contract, that contract becomes a specific relationship; it does not make ORG-01 a Cindrix subsidiary or turn ORG-03 into a corporate auxiliary. The incident can succeed, fail or change direction through player decisions.

## VIII. Three eras, changing authority

In **Core (2026)**, the companies function within recognizable legal, commercial and professional structures. Boards can dismiss executives, regulators can intervene, contracts can be challenged and employees can leave. The difficulty of accountability is a source of story, not proof that accountability never works.

In **Signal Lost (2031–2045)**, distance between headquarters and local operations may become decisive. A manager may preserve a depot's workforce by making agreements the parent company cannot approve in time. A research team may share findings with a hospital against commercial policy. A lender may be unable to enforce an otherwise valid claim. Whether these acts become reform, breach, cooperation or exploitation depends on circumstances.

In **Dead Zone (2046 onward)**, a surviving facility need not represent the corporation whose sign remains above its entrance. Staff cooperatives, settlement councils, guilds, local successors and abandoned sites are all possible. The F23 record should track the organization that actually acts and controls resources. If a successor separates from a parent, document when and how it happened; do not silently transfer all of the former corporation's assets or identity.

The **Cascade**, **Quiet Spread** and **Insolvency** scenarios remain distinct. This dossier does not choose one or establish a universal corporate survival outcome.

## IX. Implementation and continuity register

**Established:** ORG-01 through ORG-04 identities; the existing ORG-01–03 three-era records; Cindrix corporate identity; 05C–05E leadership and governance; the Halden Institute's internal schools; F23 ledger-based capacity and existing clock scale.

**Not established by this dossier:** New ORG identifiers for Veydran, Vantrell, the Halden Institute or retailers; complete national staff counts; subsidiary incorporation records; new facilities; stock quantities; corporate faction clocks; legal ownership stakes between unrelated firms; secret intercompany alliances.

**Next implementation pass:** Reconcile the available F23 organization identifier namespace and authoritative faction schema, then author the full ORG-04 Cindrix record using existing ledgers and the approved three-era template. Only afterward should additional organizations receive IDs and fully specified faction records. The current document is the narrative and organizational basis for that work, not a substitute for it.

The question for every corporate faction is the same: **who can make a decision, who must live with it, and what can the characters do about it?**
