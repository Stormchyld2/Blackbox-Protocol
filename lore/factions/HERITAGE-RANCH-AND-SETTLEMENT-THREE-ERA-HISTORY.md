# Heritage Ranch / The Heritage Settlement
## Three-era faction history, public record and generational continuity

**Setting:** Blackbox Protocol / Signal Lost / Dead Zone  
**Status:** Approved creative direction; detailed dates and local names are working continuity pending full era chronology reconciliation.  
**Fiction notice:** The Mercer family, ranch, settlement, county, incidents, and all associated fictional people are invented. Abilene and San Angelo are real Texas cities used solely as broad geographic orientation. No real county, religious group, or community is represented by this faction.

## Canonical identity

**Heritage Ranch** is the original name of a rural west-central Texas agricultural, religious, and preparedness community founded in 2008 by **Elias Mercer** (born 1964), a former military logistics officer. Its wooden entrance sign reads **HERITAGE RANCH**. Its purpose under Elias is self-reliance, voluntary membership, work, faith, practical instruction, and mutual aid. It is socially conservative and often insular, but neither every member nor every action is villainous.

**The Heritage Settlement** is the name adopted under his children, **Nathan Mercer** (born 1985) and **Rebecca Mercer** (born 1989), during a contested succession beginning around 2028. The change marks a real constitutional and cultural transformation, not just a new sign. Nathan develops the armed security and territorial apparatus; Rebecca develops the governing doctrine, education, membership requirements, and administrative hierarchy. **Both become more authoritarian than their father**, through complementary but competing methods. Their regime eventually dominates **most of a fictional Texas county** by the Dead Zone era.

The historical trajectory is a baseline for linked three-era campaigns, not a mandatory outcome if Core-era players substantially change events. The faction is not a secret arm of the Blackbox Initiative, Cindrix Corporation, ORG-01, or any real church; possible business contacts do not imply organizational control.

## Core: 2008–2026 — a ranch, not a state

**2008 — Establishment.** Elias acquires land and forms a working ranch community. Early operations combine livestock, food cultivation, maintenance, religious gatherings, and practical emergency instruction. The ranch's name reflects his belief that families preserve skills and responsibility by practicing them. He rejects public claims that the property is a sovereign enclave.

**2012–2015 — Growth.** Additional residences, workshops, food storage, and a meeting hall are built. Neighbors have differing experiences: paid local work and storm assistance on one hand; social distance and concern about internal authority on the other. The ranch develops a reputation in regional preparedness circles.

**2016–2019 — Leadership divergence.** Nathan assumes increasingly extensive responsibility for transport, perimeter security, drills, and visitor procedures. Rebecca oversees religious education, recruitment, family programs, and member discipline. Elias retains final authority but often delegates routine decisions.

**2020–2022 — Crisis reputation.** During pandemic-era disruption and shortages, the ranch is better supplied than many nearby households and sometimes helps them. Recruitment rises. Critics question whether members are pressured to surrender too much independence; supporters point to concrete services and voluntary participation. Avoid treating unproven allegations as convictions.

**2023–2025 — Internal strains become public.** A regional preparedness forum in 2024 exposes disagreements over outside cooperation. In 2025, former-member complaints about restrictions become a subject of local reporting. These disputes are important clues, not proof of later crimes.

**2026 — Playable Core.** Elias remains recognized as founder; Nathan and Rebecca have substantial but not unlimited influence. The entrance sign still says HERITAGE RANCH. Visitors can meet generous families, skeptical neighbors, respected tradespeople, strict leaders, and dissenting members. The player characters are not required to oppose the ranch. Outcomes remain open.

### Core figures and local relationships

- **Elias Mercer:** Charismatic founder, convinced that resilience depends on voluntary service, disciplined preparation, and faith. His central flaw is institutional: he assumes his children will honor principles he never wrote into enforceable safeguards.
- **Nathan Mercer:** Competent logistical and security organizer. He argues that porous boundaries invite theft and disorder. His rhetoric becomes increasingly uncompromising, but in Core he must still operate within law and his father's authority.
- **Rebecca Mercer:** Skilled teacher, organizer, and speaker. She sees loose affiliation as an existential weakness and seeks common doctrine, records, and duties. In Core her institutional program is emerging, not yet an accomplished dictatorship.
- **Pastor Miriam Cole:** Independent minister who sometimes cooperates on food drives but challenges the idea that receiving help requires adherence to one fellowship.
- **Caleb Voss:** Local agricultural cooperative organizer who resists attempts to control access across independent properties.
- **Sofia Valdez:** Regional emergency-management professional who distinguishes legitimate volunteer preparedness from unaccountable private enforcement.

## The Mercer Succession: proposed 2027–2035 sequence

**2027 — The charter dispute.** Nathan and Rebecca push for a written governing covenant that ties residence, work assignments, community schooling, and security obligations more closely to membership. Elias favors a simpler voluntary association. Members divide over whether formal rules protect families or restrict their ability to leave.

**2028 — The Mercer Succession begins.** A health episode, administrative dispute, or leadership vote reduces Elias's practical control. This is the baseline *historical event*, while its exact immediate trigger should be settled in the GM chronology. Nathan assumes broad operational command; Rebecca chairs a new governing council. They claim to be protecting their father's legacy.

**2029 — The sign and the name.** The siblings formally adopt **THE HERITAGE SETTLEMENT** and replace the ranch's timber entrance sign with a more imposing permanent gateway. The public rationale is that the community has grown beyond a ranch into a residential settlement. Older members understand the change as a break with Elias's founding vision. The original sign survives in private storage, according to later oral accounts; its whereabouts are disputed.

**2030 — The membership code.** The Settlement introduces mandatory service obligations, formalized internal hearings, and tighter controls on access and departure. Some residents leave; others remain because their homes, employment, and families are there. The extent of coercion is contested in early public accounts.

**2031–2032 — The emergency argument.** As Signal Lost conditions deteriorate, the siblings present their restrictions as necessary protection. The Settlement can provide food, shelter, repair capacity, and guarded transport that many neighbors cannot. Relief and leverage become increasingly intertwined.

**2033–2035 — Elias's final years and death.** Elias objects to the direction of the community but no longer controls it. Baseline working date: **2034**. Rebecca's memorial addresses present him as the visionary author of a disciplined new order, omitting his defense of voluntary membership. Nathan invokes his father's logistical competence and duty to defend the community. A minority of original members preserve different accounts.

## Signal Lost: 2031–2045 — from refuge to regional authority

**2031–2034 — Protected dependency.** The Settlement offers storage, organized labor, guarded routes, food, and emergency housing. Initially, many neighbors request assistance willingly. Agreements gradually require labor quotas, access permissions, exclusive trade, or recognition of Settlement adjudication. A settlement that refuses may lose services without being attacked.

**2035–2038 — The ring of affiliated properties.** Debt, abandoned land, negotiated transfers, and security arrangements extend the Settlement's influence beyond its original boundaries. Nathan builds a chain of controlled depots and patrol districts. Rebecca creates settlement registries, schooling standards, approved religious observances, and a hierarchy of local stewards. Independent families negotiate exceptions, sometimes successfully.

**2039–2042 — The County Road Conflicts.** A series of fictional disputes over bridges, grain shipments, road access, and armed escorts escalates into localized violence. Neither side is uniformly blameless: some rival groups prey on travelers, while Settlement officers increasingly treat dissent as hostile action. The conflicts harden Nathan's command structure and provide Rebecca with a justification for emergency courts.

**2043–2045 — The Settlement Compact.** The leadership imposes a common system of obligations on subordinate villages and farms. The Compact guarantees specified food allocations, basic defense, and access to repair services in exchange for quotas, service, travel controls, and loyalty to the governing council. The terms are unequal and enforcement is often coercive. The Settlement has become a territorial regime, though its borders remain contested.

### Signal Lost conflicts and people of note

- **The South Granary Dispute (fictional, proposed 2033):** An argument over whether grain reserved for Settlement members can be distributed to unaffiliated families. A relief convoy's fate becomes the subject of competing public accounts.
- **The First Steward Walkout (fictional, proposed 2037):** Several locally appointed stewards resign over Rebecca's education and membership requirements. Their departure creates a small network of independent communities.
- **The County Road Conflicts (fictional, proposed 2039–2042):** A sequence of access disputes, raids, retaliation, and negotiations, not one continuous conventional war.
- **Mara Ellis (fictional):** Former ranch school administrator who documents changes to the curriculum and later preserves founding-era records.
- **Jonah Price (fictional):** Settlement quartermaster whose records show that food distributions can be both effective and politically selective.
- **Dr. Celia Rourke (fictional):** Physician serving multiple rural communities who challenges the use of medical access as a bargaining tool.
- **Silas Trent (fictional):** Nathan's trusted district security commander, praised for keeping routes open and criticized for collective punishments.

## Dead Zone: 2046 onward — territorial power

By the Dead Zone baseline, the Heritage Settlement controls **most of its fictional Texas county**, though precise borders, county name, and population must be established in a dedicated atlas. The original ranch is its symbolic capital, administrative center, and seat of the Mercer family. Outlying farms, market settlements, depots, and crossroads may be directly governed, subordinate, or openly resistant. Its authority is not universal throughout Texas.

### The dual leadership

**Nathan Mercer, approximately 61 in 2046:** Commander of the territorial security organization. He oversees road control, armed escorts, patrol districts, logistics, and enforcement. He sees himself as a realist who prevented chaos. He is capable of protecting civilians from genuine threats while ordering disproportionate punishment against communities that resist his rule.

**Rebecca Mercer, approximately 57 in 2046:** Chief ideological and civil authority. She governs approved teaching, local administrators, membership status, internal tribunals, and the interpretation of Elias's legacy. Her power rests on institutional memory, loyal stewards, and the ability to define disobedience as betrayal of the community. She is not merely Nathan's propagandist: she has an independent power base and may veto his choices.

Their alliance is durable but unstable. Nathan believes security must come first; Rebecca believes loyalty must come first. Both fear that the other might eventually dominate the whole Settlement. Neither is a caricature or a supernatural figure. Their regime is ruthless because it has developed institutions that reward coercion and punish dissent, not because every follower is cruel.

### Territory and ordinary life

The regime's strengths are real: cultivated land, livestock, seed stores, skilled mechanics, workshops, local communications, transport organization, medical practitioners, and experienced administrators. A resident may receive a stable food ration, apprenticeship, and physical protection while also lacking freedom to travel, criticize leadership, or refuse assigned work. Some households support the regime sincerely. Others accommodate it, resist quietly, or try to escape. The region is not an undifferentiated prison.

The Settlement controls checkpoints, storage depots, and market permits. Unauthorized travel can be punished; collective penalties and forced labor exist in the established Dead Zone trajectory. Individual commanders differ in severity, creating both opportunities for negotiation and opportunities for abuse. Its influence may extend through dependent settlements beyond the county, but no broader territorial claim should be presumed without a later map.

### Third generation, not yet the ruling generation

Children of Nathan and Rebecca may be in their twenties or thirties by Dead Zone, depending on their future birth years. They serve as potential officers, administrators, educators, negotiators, reformers, or succession claimants. **No names, number of children, or final loyalties are fixed yet.** This preserves room for future character development. The ruling generation remains Nathan and Rebecca throughout the early Dead Zone baseline.

### Heritage's contested memory

Some older residents still say **Heritage Ranch** rather than **The Heritage Settlement**. The old wooden sign, if found, can serve as evidence that the institution once understood itself differently. Elias's surviving sermons, private letters, and former-member recollections are valuable because Rebecca's official history presents an unbroken line from founder to regime. The public debate is not simply whether Elias was good; it is whether a founder's imperfect ideals can be reclaimed after the institutions built in his name become oppressive.

## Player-facing historical knowledge by era

**Core player history (2026):** Founding in 2008; voluntary agricultural and religious mission; public growth and controversies; Elias, Nathan, Rebecca and their current roles; no future outcomes. Public sources do not know that the ranch will become a territorial regime.

**Signal Lost player history (2031–2045):** The succession, 2029 renaming, contested membership code, relief operations, regional expansion, and documented conflicts as they become known. Rumors, official accounts, and independent witnesses may disagree on specific incidents.

**Dead Zone player history (2046 onward):** The Heritage Settlement is an established territorial authority with Nathan and Rebecca as joint rulers. Its administrative reach, coercive practices, economic competence, resistance movements, and conflicting accounts of Elias's legacy are publicly observable to those in or near its territory.

## Campaign entry points without compulsory villainy

In Core, characters may attend a preparedness workshop, report on a zoning hearing, mediate a farm-access dispute, investigate an allegation responsibly, or join a relief effort. In Signal Lost, they may negotiate a food-sharing agreement, protect refugees, document a road conflict, help independent stewards organize, or serve the Settlement and confront its compromises. In Dead Zone, they may negotiate passage, investigate an abusive district commander, recover historical records, support reformers, rescue threatened families, or explore the siblings' rivalry. Campaign outcomes remain player-driven.

## Continuity and integration requirements

2. Do not equate Heritage Ranch with Briar Hollow, Calder County, Port Meridian, Cindrix Corporation, ORG-01, or any real religious group.

The precise 2028 succession mechanism, 2034 death date, proposed named conflicts, and supporting NPCs remain editable until the era chronology and county atlas are reconciled.
4. Avoid attributing invented wrongdoing to real people, communities, military installations, or counties.
5. Do not assume a global collapse or faction victory is inevitable for campaigns beginning in Core; the published multi-era history is a default campaign trajectory.
