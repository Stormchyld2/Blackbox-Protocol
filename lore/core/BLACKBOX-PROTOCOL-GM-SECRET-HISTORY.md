# BLACKBOX PROTOCOL
## Game Master's Secret History and Campaign Foundation
### Classified setting companion to *A History of the Divergent World, 1962–2026*

**Audience:** Game Masters and setting developers only  
**Era:** Core, January 2026 baseline; compatible with Signal Lost and Dead Zone  
**Status:** Proposed GM-facing canon for review, not a modification of existing game mechanics  
**Spoilers:** Contains the intended truth behind central historical mysteries.

> **GM principle:** The world is not secretly evil. It is inhabited by people who make consequential choices, including brave, decent, frightened, ambitious, negligent, and criminal people. Blackbox is a historical system, not a universal explanation for everything.

---

# 1. How to Use This Document

This companion answers the foundational questions deliberately left unresolved in the public history. It is a **setting reference**, not a mandatory plot. The players may never hear the words *Project Looking Glass*, and a campaign about a family business, a military deployment, or a local investigation can still be a complete Blackbox Protocol campaign.

Each entry distinguishes four kinds of information:

- **GM truth:** What happened in the shared setting before January 1, 2026. Treat as the default continuity unless the group intentionally chooses an alternate setting.
- **Public account:** What a well-informed character could plausibly know through public sources.
- **Restricted knowledge:** What particular individuals, agencies, or contractors know or reasonably believe. Their understanding may be incomplete.
- **Open variable:** A question the GM can answer for a particular campaign without contradicting foundational history.

**Do not convert an allegation into fact merely because a player investigates it.** A witness can be mistaken, an official can be honest, a classified document can be routine, and a sensational leak can be fabricated. Give players evidence that can be tested, not an endless chain of insinuations.

The source history ends at the start of 2026. No future event, including societal collapse, is guaranteed by this document. Nothing here changes established F17/F18/F20/F21/F22 rules, damage categories, skill progression, or equipment statistics.

# 2. The Canonical Answer: What Blackbox Is

**GM truth:** Blackbox began as a set of continuity studies in the early 1960s, became a disputed paper architecture in the 1970s, was activated as the **Blackbox Initiative** in 1991, and developed formal **Blackbox Protocol** procedures in 2005–2006. By 2026, the name refers to a fragmented lineage of programs, agreements, systems, personnel, and records. It is **not** a single secret agency, a shadow government, or a corporation.

The original purpose was legitimate: preserve critical services and decision-making capacity when ordinary institutions could not coordinate during an extraordinary crisis. The fundamental design flaw was not an evil intent. It was a system of exceptional permissions, divided custody, and secrecy whose accountability depended on the very institutions it was intended to survive.

**The decisive moral failure occurred in 1998.** A restricted capability was improperly used to favor a commercial consortium, and senior administrators suppressed a complete inquiry to protect the program's secrecy. That choice established a damaging precedent: conceal misconduct today to preserve a supposedly vital capability tomorrow.

There is no single mastermind behind Blackbox's later failures. There are distinct crimes, honest disagreements, organizational inertia, conflicting jurisdictions, and opportunists who use the Protocol's reputation to imply powers they do not possess.

# 3. Before the Divergence: Project Looking Glass, 1962–1990

## 3.1 The original circle, 1962–1969

Following the Cuban Missile Crisis, a restricted interagency study group brings together civil-defense officials, communications engineers, military logisticians, intelligence analysts, legal advisers, and allied observers. The designation **Project Looking Glass** is adopted for the research effort. It studies what would happen if authorities retained legal responsibility but temporarily lost the practical ability to communicate, move supplies, or verify records.

The earliest work is deliberately unglamorous: redundant records, transport priorities, continuity of hospitals, succession procedures, authentication of emergency instructions, and methods of distinguishing genuine orders from forged ones. Much of it produces useful conventional policy.

**GM truth:** The group is divided from the beginning. Its members are not secretly planning to replace elected governments. Some fear unauthorized action more than paralysis; others fear paralysis more than unauthorized action. Their disagreement survives every later reorganization.

### Foundational figures (fictional)

**Dr. Eleanor Vale — systems engineer, United States.** A leading advocate for redundant communications and locally verifiable records. Vale believes resilience requires decentralization. She becomes one of the earliest critics of opaque emergency authority. By 2026 she is deceased; her technical memoranda survive in dispersed archives.

**Adrian Mercer — continuity planner, United States.** A disciplined advocate for exceptional, time-limited emergency delegation. Mercer insists that delayed action can be as deadly as unauthorized action. He does not intend permanent emergency rule, but later administrators cite his work selectively.

**Miriam Saye — civilian legal adviser, United Kingdom.** Insists that every emergency authorization have a lawful origin, a documented scope, an expiration, and review after the crisis. Her objections prevent several proposals from becoming operational. She is not a conspirator; her papers become crucial to later reformers.

**Dr. Tomas Aranda — logistics and public-health specialist, Philippines.** Participates through allied technical exchanges and advocates for local control, community-level redundancy, and respect for national sovereignty. His contributions demonstrate that continuity planning is not solely an American project. He is not a representative of a secret Philippine national program.

These figures are **proposed setting characters**, not people who existed in real history. Their biographies can be expanded later without changing the underlying chronology.

## 3.2 The Blackbox Architecture, 1970–1990

By the late 1970s, compartmentalized research has been consolidated into the **Blackbox Architecture**: an abstract design for continuity records, verified instructions, emergency logistics, and interoperable communications. It is not yet a global network.

Its most controversial proposal is a **last-resort continuity authority**. It is conceived for circumstances in which multiple lawful authorities cannot be reached and delay threatens catastrophic loss of life. Legal objections prevent its blanket activation. Different national reviewers propose incompatible safeguards, leaving the architecture unresolved.

**GM truth:** No operational Continuity Zero system exists in this period. There are competing drafts, a small number of simulations, and records of arguments about whether any such provision could ever be legitimate.

**Potential evidence:** A 1973 legal memorandum with redacted annexes; an engineering diagram whose obsolete terminology reappears in a 2006 contractor manual; an oral-history interview in which a former official insists the controversial proposals were rejected. All three can be authentic without proving a later conspiracy.

# 4. The 1991 Authorization: The First Real Divergence

The Soviet Union dissolves along broadly familiar historical lines. The divergence is the decision by a limited coalition of officials to activate selected parts of the dormant architecture as the **Blackbox Initiative**.

## 4.1 What was authorized

**GM truth:** The initial authorizations are narrow and separately grounded in each participating government's domestic legal framework. They permit defined emergency communications interoperability, archival preservation, infrastructure coordination, logistics planning, and restricted exchange of information about immediate systemic risks. They do **not** lawfully authorize domestic political surveillance, commercial favoritism, election interference, general military command, or the suspension of constitutional protections.

The program has no universal charter overriding national sovereignty. The coalition is smaller and less cohesive than later rumor suggests. Some governments participate in one technical area but reject others; some decline entirely.

## 4.2 Why it mattered

Unlike ordinary bilateral coordination, the Initiative creates a durable cross-border contracting and technical ecosystem. Its effects are cumulative: standards become procurement requirements, contractors acquire specialized expertise, records become difficult to migrate, and successive administrations inherit dependencies they did not design.

**GM truth:** The first decade's most important change is institutional, not technological. Blackbox does not invent the internet, satellite communications, or modern banking. It alters who maintains certain critical systems, who can access restricted interfaces, and how oversight is divided.

## 4.3 The missing master charter

There is **no single master document granting unlimited Blackbox authority**. A collection of national authorizations, memoranda, restricted contracts, and later annexes forms the legal record. Several documents refer to earlier studies by obsolete names. This creates real confusion for historians and investigators.

A forged “supreme charter” is a plausible campaign artifact. It is not genuine canon.

# 5. The First Scandal: Port Meridian, 1998

The public history records the Port Meridian Contract Dispute as an improper use of restricted analytical support in a legitimate port modernization bid. The GM account establishes the responsible decision chain.

## 5.1 What actually happened

A senior fictional continuity official, **Deputy Director Martin Kessler**, approves a narrowly described “strategic resilience assessment” for a consortium seeking a major international port contract. The assessment is supposed to evaluate emergency shipping reliability. In practice, Kessler allows a restricted team to supply nonpublic competitive intelligence and logistical projections that favor one bidder.

**Asterion-linked financing** is present in part of the consortium's capital structure, but **Asterion as a whole does not direct the scheme**. A small investment team benefits, and one executive knows enough to recognize an improper advantage. Other investors and contractors are unaware.

The winning bid includes legitimate modernization work; the project is not a fake. The misconduct lies in the acquisition and misuse of restricted information and the concealment of conflicts of interest.

## 5.2 Who resisted

**Leena Okafor**, a junior compliance analyst working in the fictional procurement oversight structure, notices inconsistencies between the authorized study and the information supplied to bidders. She documents them and escalates through official channels.

**Daniel Reeve**, an internal legal reviewer, agrees the conduct exceeds authorization and recommends a formal referral. He is overruled by administrators who fear that a public investigation will expose sensitive communications arrangements unrelated to the bid.

**GM truth:** Okafor's findings are substantially correct. Reeve's objections are legally well founded. Neither knows the entire Blackbox network.

## 5.3 The cover-up

Kessler is removed from sensitive work. A narrow administrative settlement is reached, but the complete evidentiary record is classified rather than properly referred. A small number of participants knowingly obstruct accountability. Others believe, incorrectly, that a lawful closed review has resolved the matter.

This is the first decisive institutional corruption of the Initiative, **not the first moment anyone in its history has ever behaved badly**.

## 5.4 What survives in 2026

- An incomplete procurement file held by a successor oversight office.
- Okafor's contemporaneous notes, stored outside the original contractor system.
- Reeve's legal memorandum, referenced in a later review.
- Legitimate engineering records showing the port's real modernization benefits.
- A deliberately misleading summary circulated by a former contractor that exaggerates the affair into proof of worldwide corporate control.

**GM revelation:** The affair is provable with enough lawful corroboration. Its evidence does not prove that every later Blackbox contract was corrupt.

# 6. The Meridian Accords and the Name Change, 2001–2010

The attacks of September 11, 2001 occur in broadly recognizable form. **Blackbox did not cause, stage, or knowingly enable those attacks.** This is fixed canon. Security priorities change afterward, and existing continuity systems attract new funding and responsibilities.

## 6.1 The Meridian Accords, 2004

The fictional Accords formalize limited standards for international continuity cooperation. Public clauses concern disaster resilience and infrastructure security; classified annexes address restricted technical access and records handling. States adopt different limits, and some decline participation.

**GM truth:** The Accords are neither a world-government constitution nor inherently illegal. Their weakness is that cross-border responsibility can be unclear when a contractor acts under overlapping instructions.

## 6.2 Blackbox Protocol, 2005–2006

Administrators adopt **Blackbox Protocol** as the formal designation for sensitive procedures governing emergency authorization, authentication, access, and records. Personnel increasingly use it as shorthand for the whole successor network, although this is technically imprecise.

Older officials say *the Initiative*. Engineers and auditors say *the Protocol*. The public generally hears neither term.

## 6.3 The first reform attempt

A 2006 internal review revisits Port Meridian. Its authors correctly identify the secrecy-first response as a failure. Several programs implement improved logging and review. Others retain incompatible record systems and resist external scrutiny.

**GM truth:** The reform is real but incomplete. Do not portray every oversight body as powerless; some reforms later enable genuine prosecutions.

# 7. The Corporate Landscape: Influence Without Omnipotence

These are broad setting roles, not declarations of alignment. Preserve established corporate names and divisions from the repository when preparing the final manual.

## Cindrix

**Halden lineage (approved 2026-11-26):** The Halden Institute was founded around 1960 by Malcolm Halden, the American-born son of comfortable Norwegian immigrants, following a career in electrical engineering, communications reliability and multidisciplinary systems research. Its original mission was humanitarian continuity planning. Institute research contributed to the early Project Looking Glass intellectual framework, but Looking Glass remained a government-led effort. Disagreement within the institute over accountability, secrecy and preemptive intervention gradually enabled corruption. The Halden family and institute network originated Cindrix Corporation in 1984 as a legitimate wholesale communications and electrical components supplier. The family retains ownership and influence in both institutions; the institute quietly continues in 2026. Cindrix's later conflict-zone contracting and its role as the **principal private contractor to the 1991 Blackbox Initiative** grew from genuine technical and commercial capacity. Neither the institute nor the corporation possesses universal Blackbox authority. Full account: `lore/history-expansion/05C-HALDEN-INSTITUTE-AND-CINDRIX-ORIGINS-GM.md`.



A major technology, communications, and infrastructure enterprise. Cindrix has supported genuine emergency restoration and valuable commercial services. Certain divisions hold long-term contracts derived from Blackbox-era standards.

**GM truth:** A Cindrix network-maintenance unit has retained an obsolete restricted interoperability component after a contract transition. Most employees believe it is simply legacy infrastructure. One compliance manager wants it decommissioned correctly; a business executive resists the cost and reputational risk. Neither position proves a grand conspiracy.

## Veydran

A research-focused enterprise with legitimate medical, materials, and biotechnology achievements. Its involvement in Blackbox-related continuity planning primarily concerns emergency medical supply chains, research preservation, and specialized materials.

**GM truth:** A small research-administration team has misrepresented how a restricted grant was spent. The research itself may be beneficial. Internal scientists disagree about disclosure, patient welfare, and intellectual property. A GM may build an ethical investigation without introducing fantastical technology.

## Asterion

A diversified financing and holdings network. Its influence often arises from ownership structures, financing covenants, and acquisitions rather than direct command.

**GM truth:** One historical Asterion-linked investment group benefited from Port Meridian. The parent network is neither universally informed nor collectively culpable. By 2026, an internal risk officer is independently examining inherited liabilities and may become an ally or an obstacle depending on how characters approach the case.

## Corporate continuity rule

A corporation may have admirable departments, compromised executives, independent subsidiaries, internal whistleblowers, and victims of misconduct simultaneously. **Do not assign a universal moral alignment to a brand.** Use documented individuals and decisions as the source of culpability.

# 8. Harrowgate, 2016: What the Public Did Not Learn

## 8.1 The unlawful conduct

A contractor operating a Blackbox-related information service extends monitoring beyond its authorized scope and collects data about civilians without the required legal basis. A manager knowingly conceals the excess access from reviewers. Other personnel object or report it.

**GM truth:** The misconduct is real, specific, and limited enough to investigate. It is not evidence that all citizens are secretly monitored by a unified Blackbox system.

## 8.2 The whistleblower

**Nadia Serrano**, a fictional technical auditor, preserves access logs and submits them through both protected reporting channels and, after those channels fail to produce timely action, to independent investigators. She believes the violations are serious. She does not know the full history of Looking Glass or Continuity Zero.

A journalist, **Elias Ward**, publishes a carefully corroborated series. Some later imitators fabricate “Harrowgate documents” to attract attention. This gives GMs a fair distinction between reliable reporting and opportunistic disinformation.

## 8.3 The consequences

Public hearings and prosecutions result in genuine accountability. Several officials resign, a contractor loses access, and new reporting requirements are adopted. Some accusations are disproven. Restricted hearings also uncover historical links to the 1991 Initiative, but that broader lineage remains classified.

**GM truth:** A small evidence archive survives in multiple lawful custodial locations. It contains enough to connect Harrowgate to the Protocol's contracting history, but not enough to reconstruct every descendant system.

# 9. The Fragmentation Audit, 2017

A multinational classified audit attempts to inventory programs descended from the Initiative. Its investigators find incompatible naming systems, retired contracts, corporate acquisitions, separate national classification rules, and ambiguous ownership of certain technical components.

**GM truth:** The audit's principal finding is correct: **no single body can reliably list every surviving Blackbox descendant in 2017**. This is a governance failure, not proof that an unseen ruler has hidden the complete list.

The audit identifies three broad classes:

1. **Retired or archived:** Programs with no active operational authority, though records or equipment may survive.
2. **Legitimate successors:** Active systems under ordinary legal authority, sometimes with inherited technical standards.
3. **Unresolved lineage:** Systems whose provenance, access rights, or ownership cannot be established without additional review.

The third class creates mysteries. It does not automatically signify illegality.

**GM truth:** Some unresolved items are mundane documentation failures. A small number conceal misconduct. At least one genuinely useful continuity capability remains under unclear ownership and is being actively reviewed by responsible officials.

# 10. Continuity Zero: The Deepest Secret

## 10.1 What it originally meant

**Continuity Zero** is the designation later applied to the architecture's most controversial last-resort continuity provision. Its conceptual roots date to the 1970s, but it was not an operational worldwide override system in that era.

The provision contemplated a strictly bounded process for authenticating emergency instructions and preserving essential coordination when multiple ordinary authorities were unreachable. It was never intended to authorize political control, civilian repression, or independent warfare.

## 10.2 What happened after 1991

**GM truth:** Selected Continuity Zero concepts were incorporated into later continuity procedures, but **the original unified provision was never activated as a single global command system**. Different programs adopted different safeguards and terminology. Some inherited components were tested, some retired, and others redesigned.

The phrase survives in classified records, often without adequate context. A document marked “CZ” might concern archival continuity, an old simulation, or an unrelated local abbreviation.

## 10.3 What exists in January 2026

**Fixed GM truth:** There is no secret switch that can seize control of the world's governments or infrastructure. No actor possesses universal Blackbox command authority.

**Fixed GM truth:** At least one authentic, incomplete Continuity Zero design archive survives, and at least one successor system uses concepts derived from it under a narrower legal mandate.

**Open campaign variable:** Whether a specific disputed successor installation remains operational, has been safely retired, or is being misused. A GM can choose an answer for that installation without rewriting the whole world.

**Open campaign variable:** Whether a particular individual is seeking the archives for reform, profit, historical curiosity, blackmail, or a genuine emergency-preparedness objective.

## 10.4 The intended revelation

The strongest revelation is not “someone controls everything.” It is that **the original designers anticipated the danger, documented safeguards, and failed to create a governance structure that could reliably preserve those safeguards across decades of institutional change**. Their successors inherited both valuable capabilities and unresolved obligations.

# 11. Internal Schools, Networks, and Conflicts

These are intellectual and professional tendencies, **not unified secret societies**.

**Custodians:** Officials, engineers, auditors, and lawyers who favor lawful authorization, independent review, transparent succession, and local resilience. They can be overly cautious or politically naive, but their core objective is accountability.

**Continuity advocates:** Professionals who prioritize preserving services under extreme conditions. Many are conscientious and risk-aware. A minority rationalize excessive secrecy or exceptional powers.

**Institutional defenders:** Administrators who fear that disclosure will undermine useful systems or expose legitimate sensitive operations. Some protect genuine security interests; others protect careers.

**Opportunists:** Specific people who exploit contracts, access, or ambiguity for profit or influence. They are not a single coordinated faction.

**Independent investigators:** Journalists, inspectors, scholars, civic organizations, and whistleblowers. They may cooperate, disagree, make mistakes, or expose one another's blind spots.

A character may belong to more than one tendency. An official can support continuity planning and still expose unlawful surveillance. An investigator can be courageous and still publish a mistaken conclusion.

# 12. 2026: The Secret World Beneath Ordinary Life

The Core era opens in a world that still works. Most people have jobs, families, schools, ordinary disagreements, and plans for the future. International tensions and institutional distrust are meaningful but do not define every neighborhood or every day.

**The GM's central task is to make the world worth exploring even when the characters are not following a major plot.** The setting should offer museums, businesses, neighborhoods, regional history, sports, local politics, transportation, workplaces, rural communities, and distinctive cultural life alongside secrets and danger.

A classified facility is interesting partly because the nearby town is real to its residents. A corporate scandal matters because employees, customers, and patients have lives affected by it. A military mission matters because its participants, local partners, and civilians are people rather than props.

## Regional campaign entry points

**United States:** A procurement audit, a disputed emergency-communications contract, a lawful federal investigation, a local community threatened by an infrastructure closure, or a military command balancing competing obligations.

**Philippines:** A maritime communications disruption, a port modernization dispute, a regional disaster-response partnership, or journalists investigating foreign and domestic contractors. Local communities and Philippine institutions must be protagonists, not merely scenery for foreign operators.

**United Kingdom and continental Europe:** Parliamentary oversight, privacy regulation, cross-border data custody, and the challenge of separating legitimate security cooperation from overreach.

**Russia and neighboring states:** Post-Soviet archival uncertainty, competing historical interpretations, cross-border logistics, and the legacy of strategic infrastructure. Avoid portraying entire populations as agents of a government.

**South Asia:** Regional emergency planning, major transport and technology investments, scientific cooperation, and national debates about data sovereignty.

**Africa:** Locally led infrastructure projects, regional research networks, trade corridors, anti-corruption efforts, and disputes over the terms of foreign financing. Treat each country as distinct; research specific places before publication.

**Latin America and the Caribbean:** Disaster resilience, shipping networks, investigative reporting, public contracting, and communities negotiating the effects of international finance.

**Middle East and North Africa:** Competing security partnerships, energy and water infrastructure, humanitarian coordination, and domestic debates about sovereignty and oversight.

**Oceania and the Pacific:** Island resilience, emergency communications, maritime access, and regional diplomacy.

These are **entry points**, not exhaustive national histories. Detailed regional guides must be researched before final publication.

# 13. A Practical GM Revelation Structure

Avoid delivering the entire secret history through a single exposition scene. Let the players learn through action, comparison, and choice.

### Layer 1 — An ordinary problem

Begin with a tangible, local objective: a missing shipment, disputed contract, suspicious break-in, failed communications relay, damaged medical supply chain, or contradictory historical record. The characters need not suspect Blackbox.

### Layer 2 — Competing explanations

Provide at least three plausible interpretations: an ordinary error, an intentional but limited crime, and a classified yet legitimate activity. Let evidence eliminate or support possibilities. Do not decide that the most dramatic interpretation must be true.

### Layer 3 — A human decision

Identify the person or group that made the consequential choice. What did they know? What authority did they actually possess? Who benefited? Who objected? What records survive?

### Layer 4 — Historical connection

Only when earned, reveal a connection to older procurement, a Meridian annex, Harrowgate, Port Meridian, or a Looking Glass memorandum. The connection should clarify the present problem rather than replace it with a larger mystery for its own sake.

### Layer 5 — A meaningful outcome

Players can expose misconduct, protect innocents, defend a lawful operation, negotiate reform, decline involvement, or pursue an unrelated goal. Their decisions affect relationships and future circumstances, but no single ending is required.

**Fair-play rule:** Important conclusions should be supported by multiple independently obtainable clues. If one clue is missed, the scenario should not collapse. Contradictory evidence must have an explainable cause, not exist solely to prolong suspense.

# 14. Campaign Frameworks

These are narrative structures, not new rules modules.

## A. The Quiet County — Civilian investigation

**Opening:** A small community discovers that a defunct telecommunications site has changed ownership several times, and records disagree about its former use.

**Truth:** Most of the site's history is mundane. An obsolete Blackbox-derived relay component was left in place after a lawful contract ended. A current executive is hiding improper disposal expenses, not planning a national takeover.

**Complication:** A genuine emergency-services group wants the component preserved until a replacement is installed. A sensational online figure claims the site controls local elections, which is false.

**Player choices:** Document the facts, protect emergency coverage, expose fraud, negotiate a safe transfer, or walk away.

**Possible Blackbox connection:** A maintenance ledger references a 1995 North Atlantic Relay standard.

## B. Pacific Sentinel — Military and command play

**Opening:** A series of maritime communications failures raises tensions among regional actors. Characters may be field personnel, analysts, logistics specialists, or officers at a forward base.

**Truth:** Some incidents stem from equipment faults, one from criminal interference, and another from a deliberately misleading report. A hostile state is not automatically responsible for every failure.

**Complication:** A lawful operation must account for civilian shipping, diplomatic constraints, and imperfect intelligence.

**Player choices:** Verify information, protect people, recommend restraint or action through appropriate command channels, and manage consequences.

**Possible Blackbox connection:** A contractor's contingency procedures derive from the Meridian Accords.

## C. The Harrowgate Files — Journalism and legal accountability

**Opening:** A new source offers records allegedly proving that Harrowgate never ended.

**Truth:** The source possesses one genuine historical record, one misinterpreted technical document, and one fabrication planted by a private opportunist. A current access-control violation may nevertheless be real.

**Complication:** Premature publication could injure an innocent person and undermine a legitimate case.

**Player choices:** Corroborate, protect sources, pursue lawful disclosure, and decide what can responsibly be made public.

**Possible Blackbox connection:** A chain of custody leads to the 2017 Fragmentation Audit.

## D. The Veydran Grant — Corporate ethics

**Opening:** Employees discover that a research grant's accounting does not match its approved purpose.

**Truth:** A manager diverted funds to conceal a failed project. The company's medical work remains legitimate and potentially lifesaving.

**Complication:** A poorly timed disclosure could interrupt a treatment trial, while silence would allow continued misconduct.

**Player choices:** Preserve evidence, protect patients, seek independent review, and navigate competing obligations.

**Possible Blackbox connection:** An inherited continuity contract created the grant's confusing oversight structure.

## E. Port Meridian Revisited — Historical investigation

**Opening:** A retired reviewer dies, leaving notes that contradict the accepted account of a 1998 procurement dispute.

**Truth:** The misconduct and secrecy-first cover-up are real. Some dramatic allegations in the notes are speculative or incorrect.

**Complication:** Living witnesses disagree in good faith about what they were told. Some fear legitimate exposure of restricted infrastructure details.

**Player choices:** Build a reliable record, identify culpable individuals, protect uninvolved parties, and determine whether historical accountability is still possible.

## F. Ordinary Lives — A campaign without Blackbox revelations

**Opening:** Characters start a business, work in emergency services, operate a transport company, pursue a career, or build a community organization.

**Truth:** Their challenges need not be connected to the secret history at all.

**Complication:** Local economics, competition, personal obligations, and changing regional conditions provide enough drama.

**Player choices:** Define their own ambitions. The GM can leave Blackbox entirely in the background.

# 15. Evidence, Rumors, and Source Reliability

A convincing conspiracy thriller requires **the possibility that the conspiracy theory is wrong**. Prepare evidence with an identifiable origin and a reason it exists.

| Source | Usually reliable about | Possible limitations |
|---|---|---|
| Contemporary signed contract | Parties, dates, stated obligations | Classified annexes; side agreements; forged copies |
| Audit log | Recorded access and timestamps | Missing retention periods; bad configuration; stolen credentials |
| Witness statement | What the witness personally observed | Memory, fear, mistaken inference, incentives |
| Investigative reporting | Corroborated findings and public context | Incomplete sources; editorial errors |
| Official statement | Authorized position and confirmed public facts | Limited disclosure; bureaucratic caution; occasionally deception |
| Anonymous leak | A lead worth testing | Fabrication, selective excerpts, unknown motives |
| Archived technical manual | How a system was designed to function | May not reflect current deployment |
| Financial filing | Reported ownership and transactions | Complex subsidiaries; reporting limits; unlawful concealment |

**Example:** A genuine 2005 Blackbox Protocol manual does not prove its procedures were used unlawfully in 2026. A credible witness can establish that a contractor visited a site without knowing why. Two sources agreeing may still be repeating the same original error.

For major revelations, offer at least two independent evidentiary paths. Distinguish *access*, *capability*, *intent*, and *actual conduct*. A person able to misuse a system is not automatically guilty of doing so.

# 17. What the GM Must Not Assume

- That every government, military, intelligence service, or corporation is corrupt.
- That every allegation of a false-flag operation is true.
- That an entire country shares one political or moral position.
- That Blackbox controls elections, all communications, or global finance.
- That modern real-world tragedies were secretly caused by the fictional program.
- That Signal Lost or Dead Zone must occur after a Core campaign.
- That players must pursue a hidden conspiracy to have a meaningful adventure.
- That a classified activity is automatically unlawful, or that an official account is automatically accurate.
- That a corporation's subsidiary or employee represents the knowledge or intent of its entire workforce.
- That every historical mystery needs a spectacular revelation.

**Do not rewrite established mechanics in the name of setting realism.** If an adventure requires a capability not represented by the existing rules, handle it through the established adjudication framework and flag any proposed new mechanic for separate design review.

# 18. Continuity with Signal Lost and Dead Zone

Blackbox-era records and infrastructure can be meaningful in the later eras, but the link must remain conditional.

**Core:** The setting is a functioning world in which people have agency, institutions matter, and history can be discovered. Blackbox's legacy supplies context, not an inevitable end state.

**Signal Lost:** Certain continuity systems may protect lives; other inherited dependencies may complicate recovery. The specific trigger and chronology of the era remain governed by its own established canon. Do not retroactively declare Continuity Zero responsible for the crisis without a separate approved design decision.

**Dead Zone:** Old depots, records, technical standards, and institutional memories may become resources, hazards, or historical curiosities. Communities, survival, rebuilding, and regional politics remain the center of play. A cache of documents is not necessarily a clue to a global mastermind.

**Transition rule:** The shared historical timeline through January 1, 2026 is common ground. Later campaign outcomes can branch. A Core group may prevent a crisis, fail to prevent one, or never encounter it.

# 19. A GM Campaign Preparation Worksheet

Before a session or arc, answer these questions privately:

1. **Where are the characters, and what makes that place distinctive beyond the main plot?**
2. **What do the characters want?** Their goals need not involve Blackbox.
3. **What concrete event demands attention now?**
4. **What actually happened?** Write a one-paragraph answer before designing misleading clues.
5. **Who knows which parts of the truth, and why?**
6. **What are the ordinary, criminal, and legitimate classified explanations?** Which are supported?
7. **What independent evidence could confirm or disprove each explanation?**
8. **Who could be helped or harmed by the characters' choices?**
9. **What happens if the players ignore the situation?** Keep it plausible, not punitive.
10. **What can change because of player action?** Preserve meaningful agency.
11. **Does this story actually need a Blackbox connection?** If not, leave it out.
12. **Does anything contradict the public history or existing rules?** Reconcile before play.

# 20. Canon Register and Outstanding Editorial Decisions

## Fixed foundations proposed for approval

1. Looking Glass begins as continuity research after the 1962 crisis.
2. The Blackbox Architecture exists as a disputed framework before 1991.
3. The Blackbox Initiative is formally authorized in 1991; this is the principal alternate-history divergence.
4. Blackbox Protocol becomes the formal procedural designation in 2005–2006.
5. The Initiative's original purpose is legitimate; the design carries real accountability risks.
6. Port Meridian in 1998 is the first major established abuse and concealment precedent.
7. The Meridian Accords are limited agreements, not a supranational constitution.
8. Harrowgate in 2016 involves genuine unlawful monitoring and real accountability.
9. The 2017 Fragmentation Audit correctly finds no complete system inventory.
10. Continuity Zero was a real last-resort design concept, not a universal takeover switch.
11. No single actor controls all Blackbox descendants in 2026.
12. The setting contains ethical and unethical actors in every major institution.
13. Core's future is open; the later eras are not predetermined outcomes.

## Closing Note to the Game Master

The deepest truth of Blackbox Protocol is not that the world is secretly controlled by evil people. It is that institutions built to protect society can outlive their original safeguards, and that their legacies can be used for good, abused for private ends, or misunderstood by people trying to make sense of incomplete records.

The players should encounter a world larger than any one mystery: a world of ordinary pleasures, hard choices, genuine courage, preventable failures, competing loyalties, and places worth discovering. The secret history exists to make that world coherent. It must never replace the players' freedom to shape their own stories.
