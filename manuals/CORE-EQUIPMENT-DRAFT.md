# Blackbox Protocol — Core Equipment Manual

**Cross-system editorial revision · 2026-11-25 · Playtest draft, not publication-certified**

## Using equipment

Equipment is recorded by stable catalogue ID, name, unit, quantity, compatibility, owner/custodian, location, access, condition and maintenance state as required by F17 and Design Notes §7.1. The authoritative item-record mass uses **US customary pounds and ounces per unit**. Use pounds for load calculations, converting ounces to fractional pounds as needed (16 oz = 1 lb). Dimensions use inches and feet, and distances use feet or miles as appropriate. No metric conversion is required. Optional bulk 0–3 is used only in Advanced or Simulation, not automatically in Standard. The contemporary Blackbox Protocol era is the reference for the listed **fictional game-estimate USD price bands**; these are not verified retail quotations. The Signal Lost and Dead Zone eras use the catalogue's availability codes and established campaign economy rather than invented future prices. An item's description is not permission to bypass a skill check or create an unlisted bonus.

**Availability:** C = Common; R = Restricted (requires appropriate game-world access); Ra = Rare; U = Unavailable. A listing is not a guarantee that a merchant has stock. The GM establishes access and actual stock according to the era and scenario.

**Carrying and ammunition:** Use the existing F17 load bands and §7.1 mass accounting; do not create a new carrying-capacity formula. Count a firearm's listed unloaded mass once, including its specified included empty magazine or feed box. Count carried ammunition and separately acquired accessories in addition. A spare magazine is empty unless a bundle explicitly includes loads. Moving ammunition between a pack and a magazine never creates ammunition. Standard uses the existing abstract encounter ammunition procedure; Advanced tracks individual loads. Do not convert Standard uses into a fixed number of rounds.

### Recoverable arrows and bolts

Each purchased arrow or bolt pack contains six projectiles. Standard uses the existing abstract ammunition procedure. In Advanced, record each fired projectile as **in field**, then determine whether it can be recovered. A recovered projectile has one of four conditions: **Serviceable** (usable), **Worn** (usable without a numeric penalty), **Damaged** (unusable pending repair), or **Destroyed** (unusable). Soft impact does not worsen condition, hard impact worsens it one tier, and extreme impact worsens it two tiers, capped at Destroyed. These are the owner-approved **projectile-specific** wear events, not a replacement for the shared F17 condition states. An inaccessible projectile is unrecovered, not necessarily destroyed. Use the existing task and repair procedures for uncertain retrieval and repair; no new fixed repair difficulty is introduced.

## Equipment checks, harm and protection

Use the canonical D100 skill and difficulty ladder. Improvised tools impose the established −10 situational modifier; absent required tools impose −20 or make the task infeasible, as §7.1 specifies. A good kit removes the relevant penalty but grants no free positive modifier. Use the approved harm profiles: improvised/unarmed Low profile, light hand weapons Moderate profile, heavy hand weapons, bows and handguns High profile, and long guns Extreme profile, subject to any specifically approved item exceptions. Resolve one D20 harm roll (base 1–4 by quarter-band), add the item's harm-profile adjustment and existing success-degree bonus (0/1/2/3), and apply applicable armor A=0–3 once; final HP loss cannot be negative. The injury severity is based on actual post-armor HP loss, not raw rolled harm. Equipment does not change those mechanics unless an explicit approved entry says so. The existing range-band modifiers and R03 difficulty rules apply; do not add firearm-specific ballistics calculations.
