# Development instructions

Consult the foundation summary, notes/DESIGN-NOTES.md (combined design notes and decision register, including the Outstanding work list), existing rules and publication files before changes. Preserve approved decisions; do not duplicate mechanics or silently introduce unapproved rules. User retains final authority; detailed recommendations and audit resolutions may be developed under explicit delegation. Existing approved mechanics remain unless a documented correction is needed. Numerical defaults require playtesting; unresolved audit items are not completed rules. Keep tabletop independent, software offline-first, and public repository free of restricted third-party content. Audit reused code for compatibility, security and licensing. Document dependencies, probability, examples, tests and migrations. Maintain one authoritative source per mechanic. See docs/foundation/F01-GAME-IDENTITY.md for the twenty guardrails.

## Standing publication and artwork rule

Before creating or modifying any Blackbox Protocol publication, page layout, illustration, cover, table, icon, or other publication asset, read and follow [Publication & Artwork Standards](docs/Approved-Book-Sizes.md). This is the authoritative visual and production reference for all rulebooks, expansions, and sourcebooks across all three eras. Preserve approved master templates and original logo assets; do not independently regenerate, distort, or redesign them. Keep manuscript text editable, apply the documented quality-control checklist, and do not treat pending production specifications as approved. Any deviation from approved visual standards requires explicit project-owner approval.

## Standing editorial rule — Claude-authored writing as the style guardrail

**Applies to every future Blackbox Protocol lore, corporate history, character biography, faction description, campaign scenario, and other narrative/documentation writing, including revisions to existing drafts.** Before drafting, inspect the relevant existing Claude-authored material in this repository and use its established prose as the primary editorial reference for voice, pacing, structure, specificity, and treatment of character motivations. Read the actual source, not a remembered summary. When authorship is unclear, use the project's approved narrative files as the reference and do not claim a particular passage was written by Claude without evidence.

Preserve continuity and approved canon, but also match the quality of the source prose: grounded human causality, credible institutional detail, historically plausible development, meaningful internal disagreement, and nuanced motives. Favor coherent narrative paragraphs over repetitive template language, generic corporate boilerplate, slogan-driven prose, or mechanical lists where storytelling is needed. Use headings and tables when they genuinely improve a reference document; do not let a checklist replace a developed explanation. Avoid recycled character names, accidental family connections, invented certainty, omniscient organizations, and treating ordinary commercial relationships as proof of conspiracy.

For each substantial narrative addition, check the result against the source writing and revise passages that sound generic, repetitive, thin, or tonally inconsistent **before committing**. Follow any more specific project editorial instructions and preserve player-facing versus GM-only information boundaries. This is a standing rule; the user does not need to repeat it.
