# F02.02 canon principles and Session Zero

## Part 1 — The fifteen canon principles (approved 2026-11-25)

Status: **Approved.** The original thirteen semicolon-separated clauses are preserved in meaning; the combined fourth clause is separated into three distinct principles. This resolves the historical fifteen-versus-thirteen discrepancy without adding new setting commitments.

Source: DESIGN-NOTES.md, F02.02 (also docs/foundation/F02-SETTING-STRUCTURE.md). The original combined wording was: “no mandatory ideology, political outcome or global conspiracy.”

1. Flexible starting year.
2. Real history as context.
3. Distinguish fiction from fact.
4. No mandatory ideology.
5. No mandatory political outcome.
6. No mandatory global conspiracy.
7. GM selects official events.
8. Divergent campaign timelines.
9. Regional/national/global collapse.
10. Recurring fictional organizations that may evolve or disappear.
11. Optional fictional CindrixOps.
12. Optional supernatural explanations.
13. Original alternate histories.
14. Supplements do not invalidate campaigns.
15. Campaign history is authoritative for that campaign.

**Editorial clarification:** Items 4–6 were formerly one compound clause, not three newly invented policies. The list above is the authoritative numbered interpretation; the earlier thirteen-item presentation is superseded.

## Part 2 — Session Zero (approved 2026-11-25)

Status: **Approved by Jason as a recommended campaign-preparation procedure, not a mandatory gameplay requirement.** Sources: F02.02, F03, F26, F28. Owning books: GM Handbook (full procedure and worksheet), Core Rulebook (short summary).

Session Zero is a conversation, held before play, where the group agrees how the campaign will work. It is optional and can be short. Nothing agreed is permanent: groups revisit it any time (F03). The GM keeps final adjudication; players keep ownership of their characters' choices.

**Distinction from other early play (F02.02).** *Session Zero* is the planning conversation, with no play. A *playable prequel* is an earlier-dated adventure that is played; its outcomes may carry forward into campaign history. An *opening adventure* (such as Zero Day) is the first scenario of the campaign itself.

### Worksheet topics
| Topic | Questions to settle | Related rules |
|---|---|---|
| Setting | Which era to start in, the starting year (default 2026), real or fictional geography, optional collapse scenario | F02, 8.1–8.3 |
| Tone | Grounded, tense, hopeful, dark; how serious or light | F01 |
| Realism | How strictly to apply injury, shortage and logistics | F28 modes |
| Complexity | Standard, Advanced or Simulation; which optional modules to enable | F28 |
| Player expectations | Investigation, survival, politics, combat, trade or a mix; how much combat | F01, CCDD-15 |
| Character relationships | How the characters know each other and why they work together; no mandatory party roles | F07 |
| Content boundaries | Topics to avoid or handle off-screen; safety tools the group uses; consent | F03 |
| Optional horror and anomalies | Whether anomalies are in play and which readings (grounded, ambiguous, supernatural) | F25, ANOMALY-CATALOGUE |
| Campaign objectives | Short case, long campaign, sandbox; whether the group wants a transition between eras | F26, F02.05 |
| Character continuity | New characters or persistent ones; how deaths, retirement and successors are handled; carrying records between eras | F10, Section 5 |
| Table logistics | Physical or digital dice, BlackBox software use, schedule | F31 |

### Output
A one-page **campaign agreement**: era and date, tone, modes and modules, boundaries, resource tier (constrained, ordinary or well-supported, Section 2.6), objectives, and any house rules. The GM keeps it and updates it when the group changes it (F27 campaign overrides).
