# Zero Day — design brief (approved direction, 2026-11-25)

Status: **Approved by Jason as the working basis for the Zero Day manuscript.** Plot details are original and may be revised in the manuscript. Owning book: Zero Day. Uses only existing rules: F15 evidence and clue routes, F26 teaching sequence, F16 social rules, Chapter 2 resolution (DESIGN-NOTES R01–R06), Section 4 for any harm. Contemporary, default year 2026, no mandatory collapse or metaplot.

## Premise: "Dead Air"
Late in the evening a false emergency-alert message goes out across a rural-and-suburban county. It is quickly corrected, and officials call it a software glitch. The next morning the technician who maintains the county's emergency-radio and alert systems is reported missing. The player characters are brought in by someone with a reason to care: for example a county emergency manager, a newspaper editor, an insurer or the technician's family. Characters can be any occupations; the briefing offers hooks for investigators, responders, technicians, civilians and others.

What really happened (GM truth): a regional infrastructure contractor (optionally named CindrixOps, per DESIGN-NOTES 8.3) deferred maintenance on the control systems that link the county's grid, water and alert networks, and falsified inspection records. The technician found the faults, copied the evidence and was preparing to report them. A contractor manager, afraid of losing the county contract, had the technician's work credentials revoked and pressured them to stay quiet. The technician went into hiding rather than being harmed, which keeps the case grounded. The false alert was a symptom: a failing control server pushed an old test message. The same fault can cause a county-wide outage if it is not repaired.

## Essential conclusions and clue routes
Each conclusion has at least two independent routes (F15), so no roll or single clue can block the case. A failed roll costs time, trust or resources, never the path.

| Conclusion | Route A | Route B | Route C (optional) |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 The alert was a fault, not an attack | Controller log shows the old test message re-queued | Radio-site engineer explains the schedule and the missing backup | Contractor's own incident e-mail |
| C2 The technician found real faults and left on purpose | Workspace: copied inspection photos, and a packed go-bag is missing | Family or friend: the technician said they had a problem to report | Gas-station camera or toll record shows a voluntary departure |
| C3 The contractor falsified inspections and a bigger failure is coming | Signed inspection records dated when the site was closed | Parts invoices for parts that never arrived | A junior contractor employee who will talk if treated fairly |

## Locations (four)
1. County emergency operations center: briefing and the alert log.
2. The technician's workspace and home: evidence, personal clues.
3. Hilltop radio site: the faulty equipment and the physical hazard.
4. Contractor regional depot: records, the manager, the junior employee.

## NPCs (seven)
| NPC | Role | Wants | Will do |
|---|---|---|---|

| Missing technician | Witness | To report safely | Meets only if approached with care |

## Danger and resolution
The hazard is physical but grounded: the faulty radio site suffers an unsafe equipment failure while the party is there (Section 4.2 environmental harm at a declared severity, with a Search or Electronics warning clue beforehand). Combat is optional: the manager can send a heavy-handed intimidator, but the scene can be resolved by persuasion, retreat or involving the authorities (Section 4.1 surrender and retreat). Nobody is forced to fight.

## Endings
- Evidence delivered to authorities: the contractor loses the contract and the repair prevents the outage.
- Evidence withheld or lost: the fault stays; a regional blackout can follow within weeks in the GM's timeline. This is a Cascade warning sign (DESIGN-NOTES 8.3) and may be used to open a Signal Lost campaign, but is never forced.
- Mixed outcomes are expected. Consequences go to the F24 world update.

One session of about 3–4 hours for 3–5 characters; scalable by dropping location 3 or optional clues.
