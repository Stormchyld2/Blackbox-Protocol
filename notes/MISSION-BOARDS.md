# Mission boards — 10 sample missions per era

Status: **Proposed (requested by Jason 2026-11-25); drafted as an optional GM tool, awaiting approval.** A mission board presents choices with stakes and deadlines, never forced quests (F26). Players may decline, negotiate or pursue something else, and the world moves on without them (F24). Missions use only existing rules and the records in REGIONS-AND-SETTLEMENTS.md (settlements R1–R3) and ORGANIZATIONS.md (ORG-01 contractor, ORG-02 security and logistics, ORG-03 volunteer network). **Rewards are described in kind, not as prices:** in the Blackbox Protocol era the GM sets a fee in local currency from the catalogue (A2); in Signal Lost and Dead Zone the GM sets goods, labor or favors from local conditions (A3). No fixed amounts are approved. Every mission offers at least two approaches, and no essential information is gated behind one roll (F15, R02).

Columns: **Where**, **Client**, **The job**, **Stakes and deadline**, **Approaches**, **Pay in kind**.

## Participation and useful skills (approved design direction, 2026-11-25)
All mission boards are **occupation-neutral**: any character may accept, decline, negotiate or contribute to any mission. The skills below are examples of useful approaches, **not eligibility requirements**, automatic successes or mandatory rolls. The GM chooses appropriate checks from actual actions and established rules; alternative creative approaches remain valid.

## Core mission resolution notes (GM-facing draft, 2026-11-25)
These are **optional, illustrative world-state outcomes**, not predetermined solutions. No new skill checks, reward amounts, permissions or mandatory quests are introduced. On refusal, advance the relevant deadline and faction/settlement situation using the established world-update procedure (F24). A partial success should preserve something important while leaving a credible unresolved cost. Players may use any occupation and alternative approaches.

| Mission | Success | Partial success | Failure | Declined / ignored | Access, hazard and noncombat guidance |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| B-01 Alert failures | The cause is documented, corrective work is accepted and the emergency manager can schedule a verified retest | One failure mode is resolved, but coverage remains uncertain before the storm | The system remains unreliable and public warning is compromised when the storm arrives | Officials proceed with incomplete confidence; the storm deadline advances | Logs require authorization; interview staff and review public alert records before visiting a hazardous hilltop site |
| B-02 Missing container | The container's chain of custody is established and the client can recover or formally account for it | The last confirmed handoff is found, but recovery or responsibility remains disputed | The container remains missing and the renewal dispute escalates | The contract review proceeds with the discrepancy unresolved | Port and customs areas remain controlled; pursue bills of lading, voluntary interviews and lawful requests rather than trespass |
| B-03 Damaged grain | The cause is substantiated and deliveries are protected through an agreed remedy | A pattern is identified and losses slow, but the cause or restitution remains disputed | Further deliveries are damaged and the store's viability worsens | Harvest approaches without a solution; the owner seeks another response | Do not assume sabotage; inspect packaging, storage and route conditions with consent |
| B-04 Winter readiness | Critical shelter and pass gaps are documented and responsible parties agree on workable preparations | Some sites improve, but an access, staffing or supply gap remains | Readiness remains poor as snowfall arrives | Volunteers and road crews prepare independently, with gaps uncoordinated | Site owners authorize entry; use drills, interviews and a negotiated work plan rather than forced access |
| B-05 Abandoned boat | Ownership and lawful disposition are confirmed; the dock obstruction is removed by authorized parties | The owner is identified but removal is delayed or contested | The dock stays obstructed and storm exposure increases | Harbor staff initiate their own legal process as the deadline approaches | No unauthorized boarding or towing; registry checks, notices and consensual arrangements are valid paths |
| B-06 Medication delivery | Authorized custodians verify receipt and chain of custody without loss or diversion | Delivery arrives late or documentation needs reconciliation, but custody remains intact | Shipment is lost, diverted or rejected; the hospital must activate contingency supply procedures | The hospital arranges another escort or accepts the delivery risk | Restricted medication stays with lawful custodians; player escorts gain no prescribing, dispensing or seizure authority; verify seals and paperwork nonviolently |
| B-07 Stolen research drives | Drives or lawful copies are recovered and provenance is documented before the grant deadline | Essential data are recovered but custody or completeness remains uncertain | Data remain unavailable and the research deadline is jeopardized | Researchers pursue internal investigation or backup recovery | No blanket permission to hack or search; use interviews, purchase records, consent and negotiated return |
| B-08 Inspection allegation | Independent evidence establishes what occurred, allowing accurate publication or correction | Some claims are verified, others remain unproven; publication is qualified | The allegation cannot be substantiated and an inaccurate report risks harm | The editor runs a narrower story, delays, or drops it as the deadline passes | Protect source confidentiality and consent; distinguish suspicion from fact; use public records and corroboration |
| B-09 Fair generator | Generator is safely restored or a permitted substitute is operating before the event | Temporary power supports only priority activities | The event loses planned power and organizers cancel or scale back activities | Organizers seek a rental or accept a reduced event | Qualified repair, lockout and owner consent apply; parts sourcing and borrowing are alternatives to repair |
| B-10 Nighttime dam output | Operators receive a substantiated diagnosis and can implement a safe corrective plan | A likely cause is isolated but output remains unstable pending repairs | Output continues to dip and mill contracts are disrupted | The operator continues monitoring as the contract deadline approaches | Dam access and instrumentation require operator authorization; compare logs, loads and measurements without presuming sabotage |

**World update:** Record the chosen result in the relevant settlement, organization and deadline notes; update resource availability, trust, access or service continuity only where the fiction supports it. Rewards remain negotiated and GM-set, and no entry guarantees a particular outcome from one roll.
