# Mission boards — 10 sample missions per era

Status: **Core and Signal Lost mission boards approved by project owner 2026-11-25; Dead Zone board remains proposed pending approval.** A mission board presents choices with stakes and deadlines, never forced quests (F26). Players may decline, negotiate or pursue something else, and the world moves on without them (F24). Missions use only existing rules and the records in REGIONS-AND-SETTLEMENTS.md (settlements R1–R3) and ORGANIZATIONS.md (ORG-01 contractor, ORG-02 security and logistics, ORG-03 volunteer network). **Rewards are described in kind, not as prices:** in the Blackbox Protocol era the GM sets a fee in local currency from the catalogue (A2); in Signal Lost and Dead Zone the GM sets goods, labor or favors from local conditions (A3). No fixed amounts are approved. Every mission offers at least two approaches, and no essential information is gated behind one roll (F15, R02).

Columns: **Where**, **Client**, **The job**, **Stakes and deadline**, **Approaches**, **Pay in kind**.

## Core mission resolution notes (GM-facing draft, 2026-11-25)
These are **optional, illustrative world-state outcomes**, not predetermined solutions. No new skill checks, reward amounts, permissions or mandatory quests are introduced. On refusal, advance the relevant deadline and faction/settlement situation using the established world-update procedure (F24). A partial success should preserve something important while leaving a credible unresolved cost. Players may use any occupation and alternative approaches.

## Dead Zone board as a survival economy (editorial implementation, 2026-11-25)
The Dead Zone board is not merely a sequence of ten adventures. It is a **continuing source of livelihood, essential services, barter opportunities, labor exchanges and settlement obligations**. Any occupation can take any job; occupations and competencies shape how work is done, not who may accept it. The ten missions below are illustrative major postings, **not a limit on the number of available jobs**.

A GM can present postings in four practical streams: **immediate survival** (food, water, shelter, heat, care), **maintenance and production** (repairs, farming, fishing, tools), **trade and transport** (caravans, salvage, routes, delivery), and **community stability** (mediation, education, watch, mutual aid). Smaller recurring jobs can arise naturally from the same settlements and their existing needs. Record who requests work, what tangible goods or services they can actually offer, how urgent it is, and what changes if nobody responds. Never assume every settlement can pay, or that accepting work guarantees survival.

**Posting lifecycle:** available → accepted, negotiated, declined or expired → resolved or carried forward. Use existing F24 world updates and faction clocks where applicable; this is an editorial tracking pattern, **not a new mechanical subsystem**. An unclaimed job may worsen, be attempted by another group, or disappear when its resources are lost. A completed job can create follow-on work without imposing a quest chain. Compensation can be food, safe lodging, medicine access where lawful, repairs, labor, passage, materials, information, trust or a negotiated share, subject to actual availability. Keep obligations and payment terms explicit; distinguish a promised reward from goods actually delivered.

### Dead Zone community exchange and work postings (proposed GM examples)
A settlement board should connect people who **need something**, people who **have something**, and people willing to **work for compensation**. The ten D-series entries are substantial missions, not the whole board. Ordinary work, requests, offers and high-risk expeditions can appear side by side. Posting does not mean a buyer, seller, employer or reward is guaranteed.

| Posting type | Example notice | Plausible exchange | What the GM tracks |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wanted / request | Household seeks a replacement stove grate | Spare part, fabrication time or repair in exchange for preserved food or labor | Condition and fit of part; who can supply it |
| For sale / trade | Fisher offers preserved catch | Fish exchanged for salt, cloth, fuel or other agreed goods | Preservation, actual stock and perishability |
| Service offered | Mechanic offers bicycle and pump repairs | Repair labor for parts, meals, lodging or agreed payment | Parts consumed, time and workmanship |
| Day labor | Cooperative needs hands to unload grain | Food allotment, other goods or agreed local payment | Hours, safe handling and payment on completion |
| Standing work | Clinic seeks regular water delivery and cleaning | Recurring goods, meals or agreed compensation | Reliable supply, sanitation and schedule |
| Delivery / courier | Settlement needs a sealed package taken to a neighboring community | Negotiated payment, return cargo or passage | Route conditions, recipient, proof of delivery |
| Procurement / bounty | Workshop seeks a specific working belt or bearing | Agreed reward only for a verified usable item | Exact specifications, ownership and scarcity |
| Expedition / high-risk mission | Caravan seeks escorts or scouts through an uncertain route | Negotiated goods, passage or share | Threat evidence, informed consent and alternate routes |
| Community mutual aid | Residents request help repairing a communal well | Shared access, reciprocal labor or voluntary contribution | Materials, water safety and community benefit |

**Trade and money:** Barter is a viable option, but not every exchange needs to be a direct item-for-item swap. A character may earn locally accepted money **where a settlement actually uses it**, or negotiate goods, labor, credit, access or a future obligation. Do not assume one universal Dead Zone currency or fixed price list; those are separate economy decisions, not established by this board. A person may post an offer without accepting the first bid, and a requested item may be unavailable at any price.

**Posting record (GM-facing, no new mechanics):** requester or offerer; location; wanted or offered item/service; quantity and condition where relevant; proposed compensation; deadline; fulfillment requirements; permission or safety considerations; current status; and resulting relationship or obligation. An item can be acquired through an offer, a search for a willing supplier, paid work, barter or a mission, but never materializes merely because someone posted a request.

**Realism checks:** Food and medicine can spoil or expire; repair work consumes parts and time; transport requires capacity and viable routes; skills and credentials still matter for regulated or hazardous tasks; security work has consequences and should not become an automatic source of loot. Repeat postings should reflect season, harvest, infrastructure wear, labor availability and settlement inventories. Keep combat-oriented missions possible but **not the default way to earn a living**. Offer negotiation, reconnaissance, recovery, escort and avoidance routes when they make sense.

### Party-based jobs and cooperative play (design clarification, 2026-11-25)
The normal unit of play is a **group of player characters**, not a lone contractor. Dead Zone postings are offered to the party, or to individuals whose commitments may affect the party. The board is a shared community interface, not a separate solo job queue for each player. A group can negotiate one contract, divide responsibilities, pool tools and transport, and agree how compensation is shared. Individual members may also trade, work or maintain contacts independently without excluding the rest of the table.

**GM presentation for group play:** State the objective, location, urgency, risk, available information and offered compensation so players can make a joint decision. Identify multiple meaningful contributions (for example, a road-clearing contract might need surveying, negotiation, lifting/repair work, lookout and hauling). Do not require every character to roll or invent a mandatory role; only call for checks when the existing rules and actual actions warrant them. A player whose occupation is not obviously relevant can contribute through preparation, logistics, communication, observation or relationships. If the group splits, track elapsed time and consequences for each subgroup without granting duplicated rewards.

**Party compensation:** Agree whether payment goes to a shared pool, individual workers, a supplier, or a combination before the job when feasible. Track consumed supplies, promised shares, outstanding obligations and who owns recovered property. A settlement may offer one reward for the entire contract, not one identical reward per player. Group reputation and individual relationships can both change through play, subject to what actually happened.

**Example party contract:** A cooperative needs a water pump restored before storage tanks empty. One character inspects the pump, another locates or trades for a fitting, another organizes transport, and another negotiates access and compensation. They may instead buy a replacement, arrange a temporary delivery or decline. The cooperative offers an agreed quantity of supplies or services to the **group**, with actual stock verified by the GM. This is a sample situation, not an additional approved numbered mission.

**Action-oriented contracts:** Escort, scouting, recovery and settlement-defense jobs can support tense tactical encounters for the whole party, including combat if the situation and player choices lead there. They should also allow planning, negotiation, withdrawal and aftermath; never assume every job is a firefight or that combat loot substitutes for agreed payment.
