# Corporate powers, retailers and marketplace development guide

Date: 2026-11-25. Status: approved creative direction and retailer histories; detailed mechanics and implementation remain pending. Companion to ORGANIZATIONS.md, EQUIPMENT-CATALOGUE.md, F17 Equipment and Inventory, F18 Economics and Trade, F23 factions and F24 Persistent World Simulation. This guide does not override existing mechanical rules.

## 1. Setting purpose

Three major fictional corporations shape national life through overlapping economic, scientific and institutional influence. They operate legitimate businesses and maintain confidential programs. They are potential employers, allies, competitors and antagonists, not mandatory villains. Ordinary employees are not presumed complicit. No corporation is automatically responsible for the collapse or every anomaly. Preserve player agency and scenario-dependent continuity across Blackbox Protocol (2026), Signal Lost and Dead Zone. The Corporate Triad is a working editorial label, not necessarily an in-world organization or formal alliance.

## 2. The three corporate powers

### Cindrix Corporation (ORG-04)
Slogan: Everything. Within reach.
Influence through dependency. Nationwide commerce, logistics, communications, infrastructure, security and civilian contracting, with international reach. Its public identity emphasizes convenience, innovation, safety, employment and reliability. Potential hidden conflicts include monopolistic conduct, concealed research, corporate espionage, institutional corruption and political influence. Internal divisions may genuinely help communities or disagree with executive policy.

### Veydran Group
Influence through knowledge. National scientific and medical powerhouse specializing in biotechnology, pharmaceuticals, genetics, medical technology, advanced materials and experimental science. Public-facing research and healthcare coexist with confidential facilities and programs. Potential campaign hooks include whistleblowers, disputed research ethics, missing personnel and isolated laboratories. Its research may intersect with anomalies but does not define their origin.

### Vantrell Group
Influence through ownership. National financial and institutional power broker operating through investment, banking, insurance, media ownership, lobbying, real estate and institutional financing. Less publicly recognizable than Cindrix; influence derives from ownership and capital. Potential campaign hooks include conflicts of interest, contested ownership, financial misconduct and institutional pressure.

### Relationships and continuity
The three cooperate when interests align and compete when they diverge. Sector dominance is not exclusivity: Cindrix may have healthcare and finance divisions while Veydran and Vantrell remain dominant in those sectors. Vantrell supplies capital, Veydran knowledge, Cindrix distribution and operational capacity; these are narrative relationships, not fixed contracts or a secret unified command.

These outcomes are possibilities, not mandatory campaign events. Full F23 records, leadership, assets, ledgers, clocks and scenario variants remain to be authored.

## 3. Retailer histories and identities

### Cindrix Market
Slogan: Everything. Within reach.
Founded 1998 as an online general merchandise retailer. Expanded through acquisitions, competitive pricing and distribution investment, laying the foundation for the wider corporate empire. By 2026 a household name, relied on by millions and by smaller marketplace sellers. Its logistics are indispensable in emergencies, but market dominance, data collection and supplier influence attract criticism.
Ownership: Cindrix Corporation. Reputation: convenient, affordable, dependable, increasingly monopolistic. House brand: Cindrix Essentials. Specialties: general merchandise, household supplies, clothing, electronics, basic tools. Signal Lost: shortages, interrupted delivery, priority distribution contracts. Dead Zone: abandoned warehouses, distribution hubs and corporate supply enclaves.

### Ridgeline Outfitters
Slogan: Beyond the beaten path.
Founded 1978 by two wilderness guides as a small outdoor shop serving hikers, hunters and backcountry travelers. Built around field-tested equipment and knowledgeable staff; grew into a national retailer. Has resisted acquisition attempts and values independence.
Ownership: privately held, independent. Reputation: reliable outdoor equipment and knowledgeable staff. House brand: Trailforge. Specialties: camping, hiking, fishing, hunting, navigation, outdoor clothing and wilderness survival. Signal Lost: surging emergency equipment demand. Dead Zone: former stores provide wilderness supplies and practical knowledge.

### Sentinel Supply Co.
Slogan: Equipped for the mission.
Founded 2004 by former emergency services personnel and civilian contractors. Originally supplied emergency responders, security firms and government agencies; expanded into civilian sales of protective clothing, communications, medical supplies and field gear. Professional image; some private security relationships attract scrutiny.
Ownership: independent with major corporate and government contracts. Reputation: professional quality, higher prices. House brand: Vanguard Field Systems. Specialties: professional communications, protective equipment, duty clothing, field medical and civilian operator equipment. Signal Lost: institutional orders increasingly prioritized. Dead Zone: former employees and facilities support organized communities.

### Ironfield Supply
Slogan: Built for the work.
Founded 1956 as a regional hardware and agricultural machinery supplier. Grew with construction and manufacturing, acquiring smaller hardware stores and industrial distributors. Serves tradespeople, contractors, mechanics and homeowners nationwide.
Ownership: publicly traded. Reputation: durability, practical pricing, parts availability. House brand: Hardline Tools. Specialties: tools, machinery, construction materials, automotive parts and welding equipment. Signal Lost: infrastructure and repair contracts dominate stock. Dead Zone: stores and depots become repair, manufacturing and reconstruction centers.

### Harvest & Homestead
Slogan: Grow. Build. Sustain.
Founded 1983 as a mail-order seed and agricultural supply business. Expanded into livestock, food preservation, rural equipment and sustainable living. Growth accelerated with interest in gardening, self-sufficiency and preparedness. Maintains relationships with regional producers, farmers and cooperatives.
Ownership: family-controlled private corporation. Reputation: practical agricultural knowledge and dependable rural supplies. House brand: Hearthroot. Specialties: agriculture, seeds, livestock, preservation, renewable energy and homesteading. Signal Lost: rising demand for agricultural and preservation supplies. Dead Zone: supplier networks may become cooperatives and regional trading networks.

### CircuitPoint
Slogan: Technology without limits.
Founded 1992 as a computer components retailer. Developed a loyal following among enthusiasts, technicians and small businesses, expanding into consumer electronics, professional computing, networking, storage and diagnostics. Nationwide by 2026, with institutional customers and dependence on international manufacturing.
Ownership: publicly traded technology retailer. Reputation: technical selection and specialist knowledge. House brand: Voltaris Technologies. Specialties: computers, cameras, storage, networking, electronic components and diagnostics. Signal Lost: imported components scarce; repair services important. Dead Zone: remaining stock supports communications, electronics repair and technological recovery.

## 4. Passive equipment navigation and product differentiation

Storefronts act as intuitive equipment categories without forcing players to browse a rulebook taxonomy. Cindrix Market is broad, not universal. Ridgeline serves wilderness needs; Sentinel professional field needs; Ironfield trades and construction; Harvest agriculture and self-reliance; CircuitPoint electronics. Cindrix Health, Business and Fresh provide medical, bulk commercial and grocery channels respectively.

Each retailer has a substantially distinct inventory with selective overlap. Different products in the same archetype may differ in price, weight, quality, durability and availability only where supported by the rules. The same product may be sold by multiple retailers without duplicating its authoritative record. House brands are exclusive by default; independent manufacturers may supply multiple stores. Do not silently change the approved retailer ownership histories; any later acquisition or hidden ownership relationship requires a setting decision.

## 5. Catalogue and ordering architecture

Retain all 145 existing EQ records as base archetypes. Proposed expansion target: 30–50 fictional manufacturers, 400–600 individual products, six primary storefronts. Targets are planning estimates, not required minimums or completed work. Separate manufacturer, retailer, product and retailer listing records. Product references archetype and manufacturer; listing references product and retailer. Keep stable IDs, authoritative gameplay references, descriptive specs, weight in lb/oz, contemporary USD price, package quantity, condition, availability and restrictions. Avoid duplicating mechanics across listings. Representative estimates are allowed under the approved equipment policy.

The Equipment Compendium remains authoritative for printed equipment rules. Proposed interactive marketplace uses the same data, supports category/search/filter browsing, shopping carts, total costs and carrying weight, order histories and inventory export. Actual implementation, schema and integration require software foundation review.

Cindrix Plus proposed benefits: free standard shipping, expedited delivery, exclusive discounts and priority fulfillment when available. Membership fees, shipping charges, lead times, returns, payment rules and regional stock remain undecided. Do not invent binding bonuses or costs before economics integration.

## 8. Dead Zone commerce — future decision (discussion record)
User direction: small traveling merchants, camps, settlement trading posts, independent specialists and regional hubs should form the ordinary Dead Zone trade network. Corporate survival is deliberately undecided. Cindrix may outlast the fall through warehouses, distribution and commercial enclaves; Ridgeline Outfitters may become frontier suppliers, Ironfield Supply repair and reconstruction hubs, and Harvest & Homestead agricultural cooperatives or seed networks. Sentinel and CircuitPoint remnants are possible but not established. No universal monopoly or guaranteed surviving company. Determine regional survival, ownership, trade routes, currencies and merchant inventories during Dead Zone development. Future local-browser marketplace may expose a GM-managed merchant and barter interface using the same catalogue; no nationwide online delivery assumed. This is a discussion record, not finalized setting canon.


## Halden ownership reference (Core, 2026)

Cindrix Corporation (ORG-04) is the 1984 family-origin commercial parent, not the nonprofit Halden Institute or the later Cindrix Market storefront. Under the **adopted 05D working governance model**, Thomas and Margaret Halden jointly chair the seven-director Halden Holdings board, Thomas chairs Cindrix's nine-director board, Margaret chairs the institute trustees, and Dr. Miriam Calder directs the institute; these appointments and board structures are adopted for Core 2026, while precise legal drafting remains provisional. A working model in GM-only `lore/history-expansion/05D-HALDEN-FAMILY-OWNERSHIP-AND-INSTITUTE-FACTIONS-GM.md` assigns family vehicles 38% of Cindrix economic equity and approximately 56% ordinary voting power through enhanced-voting shares; the capitalization model is adopted as working canon, while final legal drafting remains subject to continuity review. The nonprofit has independent governance and may not distribute charitable assets to the family.

**Scope discipline for other retailers:** Ridgeline Outfitters, Sentinel Tactical, CircuitPoint Electronics, Ironfield Supply, Harvest & Homestead and other stores require concise player-facing company histories centered on founding, public reputation, product categories, store experience and three-era availability. Do not retrofit them into the Halden/Looking Glass/Initiative lineage without a separately approved canon decision. Their mechanical inventory, economics and F23/F24 dependencies remain authoritative.
