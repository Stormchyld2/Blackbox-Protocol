# Fictional regions and sample settlements

Status: **Approved by Jason 2026-11-25 (three fictional regions, five sample settlements); content drafted as the working basis and provisional.** Owning book: GM Handbook, with settlement entries reused in Signal Lost, Dead Zone and Zero Day. All names, places and populations are original and fictional. The default setting is a US-style baseline (F02.03); GMs may relocate or rename. Each settlement is written as an F22 settlement record in the Blackbox Protocol era plus **example states** for Signal Lost and Dead Zone. The later states are illustrations for one possible path under the scenarios in DESIGN-NOTES 8.3. Population and service changes are never automatic (F22): the GM records the events that cause them. Service ratings use the F02.07 0–6 scale (6 operational) and indicators from DESIGN-NOTES 8.1. Organization IDs refer to ORGANIZATIONS.md. The Zero Day case (ZERO-DAY-BRIEF.md) takes place in Region 1.

## R1 Calder County
Geography: river valley, farmland, low hills, a state highway and a rail line crossing at the county seat. Climate: four seasons, snow in winter. Infrastructure: county grid with one substation per town, county emergency radio and alert system on a hilltop site, treated water from the river, a regional hospital. Resources: grain, dairy, timber, a quarry. Economy: farming, light industry, commuters. Organizations: ORG-01 holds the utility and alert contract (Zero Day), ORG-02 supplies guards and trucking, ORG-03 runs the county volunteer corps.

### Fenwick (county seat)
- **Blackbox Protocol era.** Population about 12,000. Town hall, county emergency operations center, regional hospital (about 60 beds), high school, rail yard, warehouse district. Services all at 5–6. Hooks: Zero Day's briefing site; records fraud; a hospital supply shortage; a quiet rail-yard theft ring.
- **Signal Lost example.** Population swells with evacuees from outer towns. Hospital at 3, water 4, grid 2–3 after repeated failures; the hospital runs on a generator and fuel becomes the pressing ledger entry. Hooks: fuel convoys with ORG-02, rationing disputes, a county council losing authority.
- **Dead Zone example.** The town holds a council built from ORG-03 and surviving officials; population fraction of the original; hospital reduced to a clinic with a few trained staff; rail yard salvaged for metal. Hooks: clinic staffing, rail-yard salvage rights, expansion into abandoned suburbs.

### Alder Crossing (farm town)
- **Blackbox Protocol era.** Population about 1,800. Grain elevator, feed store, volunteer fire company, diner, elementary school. Services 5–6 except healthcare 3 (clinic open two days a week). Hooks: feed store gossip, a failing grain elevator, a stranded cross-country driver.
- **Signal Lost example.** Becomes a food hub and holding point for people leaving Fenwick; population doubles temporarily; storage and sanitation strain. Hooks: sharing the harvest, hoarding conflict, a sick traveller.
- **Dead Zone example.** A farming cooperative (TPL-B) with the grain elevator as its heart; seed and tool exchange is its main trade. Hooks: seed keeping, a blight, trade disputes with a bandit-hit convoy route.

## R2 Aldane Coast
Geography: sheltered bay, rocky coast, a deep-water port, marshes inland. Climate: mild and damp, storms in autumn. Infrastructure: port cranes and rail spur, municipal grid fed from the regional network, desalination-free fresh water from reservoirs, a harbour radio station. Resources: fish, cargo handling, salt. Economy: shipping, fishing, tourism. Organizations: ORG-02 runs port security and trucking, ORG-01 holds the water and port-controls contract, ORG-03 operates the harbour rescue corps.

### Port Aldane
- **Blackbox Protocol era.** Population about 60,000. Container terminal, ferry, hospital, university campus, dense downtown. Services 5–6; strongly dependent on imports. Hooks: smuggling; a port-controls fault; missing cargo manifests; a research grant dispute.
- **Signal Lost example.** Imports fail, population leaves or crowds shelters; the port becomes a contested resource. Water 3, healthcare 2, public order 3. Hooks: convoy security, shelter management, a cargo of unknown value.
- **Dead Zone example.** A few thousand people around the harbour and the university farm; the port serves fishing boats and small coastal traders. Hooks: salvaging container goods, harbour governance, a trade guild (TPL-C) controlling boats.

### Kestrel Bay (fishing village)
- **Blackbox Protocol era.** Population about 900. Fish dock, cannery, store, lighthouse, volunteer rescue boat. Services 4–5; healthcare 2. Hooks: a storm-damaged pier, a quiet feud between boat owners, a missing boat.
- **Signal Lost example.** Cut off from supply; fishing and gardens become survival. Hooks: sharing the catch, fuel for boats, a refugee boat arriving.
- **Dead Zone example.** A self-reliant fishing community with salting and drying sheds; sheltered; trades fish for grain and tools. Hooks: harbour defence, a boat-building project, a sickness that the one medic cannot handle.

## R3 Ridgeback Highlands
Geography: steep valleys, forest, a single paved pass, old mill buildings, cold winters. Infrastructure: small hydro dam and local grid, one cellular tower, radio relay on a ridge. Resources: timber, water power, game, hardy crops. Economy: timber, small manufacturing, seasonal visitors. Organizations: ORG-01 maintains the dam and relay; ORG-03 has a mountain rescue team; ORG-02 appears seasonally.

### Hollis Gap
- **Blackbox Protocol era.** Population about 2,500. Sawmill, hydro dam, school, small clinic, general store, mountain rescue station. Services 5–6; transport depends on the pass. Hooks: a winter closure, a sawmill accident, hikers lost in a storm.
- **Signal Lost example.** The pass becomes the only link and may be blocked by snow or damage; the dam is vital. Hooks: dam maintenance with limited crews, shared heating wood, a trapped convoy.
- **Dead Zone example.** The dam still powers a small community with a skilled maintenance crew, giving it real leverage; wood and water power support workshops. Hooks: defending the dam, trade through the pass, a rival settlement wanting the power.
