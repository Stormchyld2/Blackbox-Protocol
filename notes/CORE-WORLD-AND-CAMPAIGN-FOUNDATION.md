# BLACKBOX PROTOCOL: CORE — WORLD & CAMPAIGN FOUNDATION
Status: Proposed Core setting expansion for editorial review, 2026-11-25. **Core-only**; no Signal Lost or Dead Zone lore is established here.
Scope: Fictional alternate-history 2026, with recognizable real geography and national institutions. All named incidents and fictional people below are invented. This supplement does not amend F17/F18/F20/F21/F22 or other game mechanics.

## 1. The playable promise
Core is not a life simulator and not a compulsory conspiracy campaign. It is a contemporary geopolitical and investigative adventure setting in which players pursue meaningful objectives in a familiar but historically divergent world. Adventure can be civilian, criminal, investigative, humanitarian, corporate, security, diplomatic or military. World events provide opportunities, consequences and context; the GM never forces a single plot.

The setting's moral baseline is neutral: governments and corporations contain honest and dishonest people, competing mandates, imperfect incentives, and real oversight. Good actions matter. Corruption can be exposed and punished. A secret is not automatically evidence of wrongdoing; a credible threat is not automatically fabricated.

## 2. History at a glance: public-facing 2026

**Complete history source:** [`lore/core/BLACKBOX-PROTOCOL-ALTERNATE-HISTORY.md`](../lore/core/BLACKBOX-PROTOCOL-ALTERNATE-HISTORY.md). This section is a navigation overview, **not a replacement** for the complete manuscript. The separate [`GM Secret History`](../lore/core/BLACKBOX-PROTOCOL-GM-SECRET-HISTORY.md) is classified GM-only material and must not be reproduced in player-facing publications.

1962: Project Looking Glass begins as classified continuity research in the Cold War. 1975–1990: related contingency planning continues under compartmentalized programs. 1991: the Blackbox Initiative becomes an operational international continuity effort and marks the fictional timeline's principal divergence. 1990s: public-private contracting expands; several projects are legitimate, while oversight becomes uneven. 2001–2015: the phrase Blackbox Protocol comes to denote sensitive operating procedures and, eventually, the wider legacy. 2016: the fictional Harrowgate Affair exposes unlawful surveillance by particular actors and prompts investigations and reforms. 2026: daily life continues, but geopolitical competition, civic distrust and critical-infrastructure dependencies generate unease.

Public knowledge is fragmentary. Most citizens have never heard the formal designation. Do not present classified details as common knowledge. This overview does not establish future collapse.

## 3. World regions and agency
These are starting points, not complete portrayals. Future regional books should be developed with locally grounded sources and diverse voices.

**United States:** Political disagreement, regulatory oversight, regional economic disparities, corporate contracting, independent media and federal/state jurisdiction create multiple legitimate campaign approaches. Different communities experience national events differently.

**Philippines and maritime Southeast Asia:** Trade, fishing, disaster response, maritime law, labor migration and regional diplomacy create stories beyond military conflict. Manila, Cebu and coastal communities provide distinct scales of play. Local protagonists, institutions and expertise lead their own stories; foreign actors are not the default heroes.

**Europe:** Energy policy, data privacy, financial regulation, defense partnerships and historical memory complicate cross-border investigations. London, Brussels and Warsaw have different institutional perspectives.

**Eastern Europe and Russia:** Diplomacy, contested security interests, commerce, journalism and ordinary civilian life coexist. Moscow is not a shorthand for villainy; state actors and individuals must be differentiated.

**Africa:** Locally led technology, medicine, conservation, infrastructure investment and political reform intersect with competing external interests. Nairobi and Lagos are potential starting hubs; countries and communities require individual research.

**Latin America:** Urban entrepreneurship, environmental protection, regional trade, investigative journalism and organized crime can each anchor campaigns. Bogotá and São Paulo are not interchangeable.

**Middle East:** Diverse governments, communities and institutions navigate trade, security, water, energy, science and diplomacy. Avoid treating an entire region as one conflict.

**Oceania and Pacific:** Island communities, disaster preparedness, fisheries, telecommunications and strategic logistics create civilian and military stories with local agency.

## 4. Organizations: power is not morality
Use the established Corporate and Retail Guide for corporate identities; this chapter adds only story-facing tensions.

**Cindrix Corporation:** Its logistics, cloud, infrastructure, response and security divisions deliver useful services at immense scale. A public-interest operations director demands auditability; a contracts executive quietly suppresses adverse reports; ordinary workers face conflicting incentives. Neither character speaks for every division.

**Veydran Group:** Researchers pursue real medical advances. An ethics board challenges an aggressive acquisition program. A senior investigator worries that confidentiality is being misused to obstruct safety reporting.

**Asterion Holdings:** Investment can revitalize failing infrastructure. Some managers favor long-term stewardship; a deal team pressures local governments through opaque financing. Its investors are not a single conspiratorial bloc.

**Government and public institutions:** Legislators, regulators, courts, local officials, service members, emergency responders and investigators possess distinct authorities and constraints. An honest inspector and a compromised contracting officer can work in the same agency. Public accountability can succeed.

**Independent organizations:** Local newspapers, labor associations, humanitarian groups, universities, neighborhood coalitions and independent retailers are active participants, not scenery.

**Criminal actors:** Criminal enterprises have specific motives, resources and victims; avoid assigning collective guilt to ethnicities, migrants or protest movements.

## 5. Six starting locations
Each location supports at least one ordinary, one investigative and one high-stakes campaign.

### 5.1 Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania — The River Exchange
**Public face:** A working metropolitan region with advanced manufacturing, universities, hospitals and river commerce.
**Local tension:** An infrastructure modernization contract has stalled amid competing technical assessments.
**Explore:** A public riverfront exhibition, a machine shop, a municipal records room, a contractor's training center, a historic tunnel with no secret significance unless the GM chooses.
**People:** Mara Bell, municipal engineer who insists on transparent testing; Eli Navarro, contractor compliance officer under pressure to sign incomplete reports; Councilor Judith Vale, sincere redevelopment advocate facing political backlash.
**Adventure paths:** Help validate a bridge sensor failure; trace fraudulent invoices; escort a safety team to a damaged site. The faulty equipment and the fraud need not share a culprit.
**Consequences:** Publishing accurate findings may delay construction but prevent injury; suppressing them preserves schedules at a cost.

### 5.2 Washington, D.C. — The Oversight Corridor
**Public face:** Institutions, museums, public records, advocacy groups, contracting offices and journalists.
**Local tension:** An oversight committee receives conflicting testimony about an old continuity-related contract.
**Explore:** Hearings, archives, public-interest offices, research libraries, neighborhoods beyond federal buildings.
**People:** Dana Okafor, principled committee counsel; Victor Hale, retired procurement specialist who rationalizes a past omission; Nia Park, reporter who verifies evidence before publication.
**Adventure paths:** Authenticate a document, protect a lawful whistleblower, identify a forged leak, represent an accused official. Different outcomes can vindicate or implicate different actors.

### 5.3 Manila, Philippines — The Harbor Ledger
**Public face:** A dynamic capital and transport hub linked to commerce, universities and regional diplomacy.
**Local tension:** Disputed cargo records threaten a locally managed coastal communications upgrade.
**Explore:** Port offices, public market streets, a university laboratory, local media offices, a ferry terminal.
**People:** Dr. Isabel Reyes, Filipino communications researcher; Ramon dela Cruz, port safety investigator; Celina Bautista, cooperative organizer protecting fishing livelihoods.
**Adventure paths:** Resolve shipping discrepancies, investigate a false customs allegation, coordinate a storm-response exercise, negotiate access for local repair teams.
**Principle:** Filipino characters lead local decisions. A foreign contractor is a possible partner or obstacle, not the narrative center.

### 5.4 London, United Kingdom — The Ledger House
**Public face:** Financial services, journalism, science, civil society and multinational commerce.
**Local tension:** An acquisition obscures who owns a data-storage subcontractor.
**Explore:** Corporate filings, university seminars, a neighborhood legal clinic, historical sites.
**People:** Amara Singh, forensic accountant; Peter Wren, company secretary guarding lawful client confidentiality; Dr. Oliver Price, cybersecurity scholar.
**Adventure paths:** Untangle lawful shell structures from fraud, defend a wrongly accused employee, trace a genuine procurement conflict.

### 5.5 Nairobi, Kenya — The Resilience Lab
**Public face:** Technology, research, logistics and regional partnerships.
**Local tension:** A locally designed emergency communications pilot is being squeezed by conflicting investor terms.
**Explore:** Innovation hub, community meeting, public transit corridor, regional research partnership.
**People:** Wanjiku Mwangi, systems engineer and project founder; David Otieno, public procurement auditor; Leila Hassan, emergency-response coordinator.
**Adventure paths:** Secure a fair contract, investigate counterfeit components, test equipment in realistic field conditions.
**Principle:** Local innovation is the driver, not a backdrop for outsiders.

### 5.6 Briar Hollow, West Virginia — Fictional Appalachian town
**Public face:** A fictional small town in West Virginia, surrounded by forest roads, local businesses, volunteer responders and former industrial sites. The town's county is intentionally unnamed; countywide authorities and records may appear when needed.
**Local tension:** A land transfer, a road closure and a historic utility easement become the subject of contradictory rumors.
**Explore:** County courthouse, volunteer fire hall, ridge overlook, old rail spur, family-owned diner.
**People:** Sheriff Helen Mercer, accountable to the community; Owen Pike, surveyor whose records conflict with an online map; Ruth Calder, local historian who resists sensational claims.
**Adventure paths:** Locate missing survey records, resolve a property dispute, investigate vandalism, assist a search-and-rescue operation.
**GM truth:** At least one apparent conspiracy is a mundane clerical error. A separate procurement irregularity may be real. Keep them independent.

## 6. Recurring cast: relationships over alignment
The following characters can cross campaigns but are not mandatory quest-givers.
- **Mara Bell** — municipal engineer; strength: technical integrity; blind spot: distrust of politics; can become ally or adversary over disclosure timing.
- **Dana Okafor** — oversight counsel; strength: procedural fairness; blind spot: assumes institutions can always correct themselves.
- **Dr. Isabel Reyes** — communications researcher; strength: local technical expertise; blind spot: protective of her team.
- **Wanjiku Mwangi** — founder and engineer; strength: practical ingenuity; blind spot: accepts personal risks to keep a project alive.
- **Eli Navarro** — compliance officer; strength: knows contracting systems; compromise: concealed a report under pressure; capable of restitution.
- **Victor Hale** — retired procurement specialist; strength: institutional memory; compromise: concealed a damaging decision he once believed necessary.
- **Nia Park** — journalist; strength: source verification; vulnerability: public deadline pressures.
- **Ruth Calder** — historian; strength: distinguishes evidence from legend; vulnerability: limited access to classified archives.

Characters have goals and limits; none is guaranteed to be correct. Introduce recurring relationships through earned trust and concrete decisions.

## 7. Mystery architecture: clues, not rails
**The Harrowgate remainder:** Public scandal files contain an unexplained contract number. Possible truths: a lawful successor contract; an improperly retained data store; a forged reference. GM selects the truth before play and plants multiple independent clues.

**The Meridian account:** Asterion-linked financing appears beside an old infrastructure consortium. Possible truths: ordinary refinancing; conflicts of interest; embezzlement by two managers. The corporation's entire leadership is not presumed involved.

**The missing survey:** Two property maps conflict. The truth may be a recording error; a deliberate fraud; or a real but unrelated restricted easement. Player investigation determines what becomes public.

**The phantom shipment:** A container is listed at two ports on the same date. Possible truths: reused tracking ID; smuggling; deliberately falsified customs records. Do not assume a geopolitical plot.

**The old designation:** A declassified document uses 'Blackbox Initiative' while later papers use 'Blackbox Protocol'. This is a legitimate historical breadcrumb, not proof of wrongdoing. It may lead to Looking Glass archives only after corroboration.

Every mystery must have: a factual GM answer, at least three independently discoverable clues, a plausible innocent explanation until disproven, and consequences for mistaken accusations. No required skill check should be the only route to essential evidence; use existing adjudication rules.

## 8. Campaign frameworks
**The Public Record (investigation):** Journalists, attorneys, auditors or civic volunteers investigate a procurement scandal. Opening: conflicting bids. Midpoint: credible exculpatory evidence. End: disclosure, prosecution, negotiated correction or justified closure.

**Frontier Response (humanitarian):** Search-and-rescue and logistics specialists support communities during a severe regional emergency. Opening: broken supply coordination. Midpoint: competing priorities. End: restored service, accountability and lasting relationships.

**Forward Watch (military):** Service members and supporting civilians at a fictional forward operating base respond to escalating regional incidents. Opening: ambiguous maritime report. Midpoint: disputed intelligence and command pressure. End: lawful de-escalation, rescue, detention or mission failure, subject to player choices. Use fictional units and operations; do not equate nationality with guilt.

**Inside Cindrix (corporate):** Employees, contractors and investigators confront competing obligations over a public infrastructure bid. Opening: a compliance alert. Midpoint: management conflict. End: reform, resignation, exposure, or a defensible project outcome.

**Briar Hollow (civilian exploration):** Residents and visitors pursue local work, relationships and historical discoveries. Opening: road closure. Midpoint: property records and community tensions. End: several unrelated mysteries resolved, with the town changed by players' choices.

**Cross-Border Ledger (global):** A mixed team investigates falsified trade documents across Manila, London and Nairobi. Opening: mismatched manifests. Midpoint: competing jurisdictional claims. End: commercial correction, criminal exposure or unresolved legal dispute.

Each framework can run without Blackbox secrets; the GM may add them as optional layers.

## 9. First three sessions: Briar Hollow (ready-to-run narrative outline)
**Session 1 — The Closed Road:** A town access road closes after an embankment inspection. PCs have personal reasons to visit: work, family, reporting, survey, volunteer response. Introduce Mercer, Pike and Calder. Three accessible leads: closure notice, local map, eyewitness account. End with a real discrepancy between a recorded easement and the physical marker.
**Session 2 — Paper and Ground:** Players choose to inspect records, speak to residents or survey the area. One rumor proves false: a misdated photograph. An invoice reveals a separate billing irregularity. An independent witness confirms a contractor changed a marker without authorization. No combat required.
**Session 3 — What the Town Keeps:** Players decide whether to disclose, seek formal review, negotiate a correction, or pursue criminal allegations. Evidence distinguishes the land issue from procurement misconduct. The town responds through plausible institutions. A faded reference to an old federal continuity contract can be an optional future thread, not a mandatory cliffhanger.

GM preparation: Decide the exact document forgery culprit (if any), the billing irregularity's beneficiary, and the personal stakes of each PC. Keep a truthful paper trail. Do not change the culprit merely to defeat player reasoning.

## 10. Living world without a new rules subsystem
At each campaign interval, the GM records: (1) one local development, (2) one national or regional development, (3) one global headline, (4) which organizations acted, and (5) one consequence the players could notice. Use existing world-simulation and faction rules where defined; this is a preparation checklist, not a new numerical mechanic.

Example week:
- Local: town council commissions independent engineering review.
- National: public debate over procurement transparency.
- Global: maritime safety talks resume after a tense incident.
- Organizational actions: Cindrix Response volunteers equipment; an unrelated contractor challenges the tender.
- Observable effect: revised road access and a public hearing.

Not every headline is a plot hook; ordinary good news and successful governance must appear.

## 11. Player-facing versus GM-only publishing boundary
**Player's World Guide:** Public timeline, everyday culture, accessible locations, known organizations, contested public events, common rumors clearly labeled, travel and occupational opportunities. No assertion of inevitable collapse.
**GM Core Handbook:** Confirmed truths of individual scenarios, faction secrets, historical classified material, Continuity Zero, NPC motives, optional clues, consequence guidance, and an optional classified late-Core strategic risk assessment. The warning is probabilistic, not prophecy.
**Future eras:** Signal Lost starts in 2031 and Dead Zone starts in 2046 per approved era chronology. These dates are reference metadata only; no events, causal explanations or future lore are authored here. Core campaigns may continue beyond 2030.
