# F07 — Character Creation: Remaining Requirements

Status: Canonically reconciled foundation draft; catalogue-level and playtest acceptance pending.
Companion: F07.1-Character-Creation-Sequence.md.
These proposed subsections follow the approved creation sequence. They do not supersede existing foundation mechanics.

## F07.2 — Attribute Allocation

Load the canonical attribute definitions, generation method, starting limits and budget from the applicable ruleset version. Record base values, expenditures and separately authorized adjustments. Reject unknown attributes, invalid values, overspending and unauthorized adjustments. Require any mandatory allocation to be resolved before finalization. An attribute change must revalidate dependent selections and recalculate derived values. Do not infer a refund schedule or substitute a new generation method.

Acceptance: a legal allocation passes; an out-of-range value or expenditure above the canonical budget fails; a changed prerequisite is flagged.

## F07.3 — Occupation Selection

Select from the canonical occupation records available under campaign configuration. Record the occupation identifier, version, relevant qualifications and selected alternatives. Distinguish historical experience from present authority, licensing and access. Apply mandatory grants exactly once using their source identifiers. Where qualification evidence is required, retain its status and supporting record. An unavailable occupation cannot be silently substituted. Changing occupations removes only grants attributable to the former selection and revalidates dependent choices under the canonical rules.

Acceptance: repeated application does not duplicate grants; a disallowed occupation fails; unrelated personal selections survive an occupation change unless their prerequisites cease to hold.

## F07.4 — Skill Allocation

Separate occupational grants, occupational alternatives and discretionary allocations in the character ledger. Use canonical skill identifiers, starting proficiency limits and expenditure rules. Resolve mandatory alternative selections from the occupation's permitted options. Apply canonical stacking and duplicate handling; never assume duplicated points become spendable. Record each expenditure and resulting proficiency. Validate the remaining budget and prerequisite relationships.

Acceptance: a permitted alternative passes; an unlisted alternative fails; an overspent allocation fails; identical inputs yield identical proficiency.

## F07.5 — Competency Selection

Resolve competencies against canonical records and the applicable starting acquisition route. Record source, prerequisite skill, qualification and evidence where required. Apply occupation grants according to their documented scope. Discretionary competencies must satisfy the governing cost and eligibility rules. Distinguish possession of a competency from availability of resources necessary to perform a particular task. Missing task resources do not erase a valid competency.

Acceptance: a valid grant is retained; an unsupported discretionary selection fails; a missing task resource is represented separately from competency ownership.

## F07.6 — Specializations

Use canonical specialization identifiers and parent skill or competency relationships. Apply granted and discretionary specializations through their authorized routes. Validate eligibility, costs, limits and scope. Do not broaden a specialization into a general skill or unrelated competency. Parent changes trigger dependent revalidation. Record each specialization's provenance and resulting value under the governing rules.

Acceptance: a specialization with a valid parent and eligibility passes; an orphaned or ineligible specialization fails; its benefit is restricted to its defined scope.

## F07.7 — Derived Statistics

Maintain a dependency list for each derived statistic, referencing the canonical formula and ruleset version. Compute from validated source values, including only authorized modifiers. Apply canonical rounding and bounds at the prescribed calculation stage. Store or display the calculation trace. Recompute affected values after source changes; do not allow manual edits to replace computed values without an authorized exception.

Acceptance: identical inputs produce identical outputs; changing a source updates dependent values; a stale or manually altered result is detected.

## F07.8 — Starting Resources

Load the campaign's authorized starting-resource rules and applicable catalog. Record each resource, quantity, ownership or access status, and source of entitlement. Apply canonical cost, availability and capacity restrictions where applicable. Distinguish owned, borrowed, shared and institutionally controlled items. Occupational experience alone does not confer equipment or access. Unavailable resources cannot be counted as possessed. Record any authorized exception explicitly.

Acceptance: a legal resource allocation passes; an unauthorized entitlement or exceeded limit fails; institutional access is not represented as personal ownership.

## F07.9 — Character Background

Record identity, history, education, employment, relationships, motivations and relevant limitations. Link mechanical claims to an authorized source. Verify occupational advantages using their specific verification requirements and campaign availability. A contact's existence does not guarantee cooperation or access. Narrative revisions trigger revalidation only where they affect qualifications, advantages or other mechanical claims. Keep optional narrative details distinct from required mechanical evidence.

Acceptance: unsupported benefits are rejected; a verified advantage retains its scope; narrative-only edits do not alter numerical values.

## F07.10 — Character Validation

Validate in dependency order: ruleset and campaign, identifiers, attributes, occupation, grants, skills, competencies, specializations, derived statistics, resources, background claims and exceptions. Report findings with affected record, violated rule, severity and corrective action. Errors block finalization. Warnings require resolution or an authorized exception where permitted; informational findings do not block. Preserve invalid selections in draft state for correction rather than silently deleting them. Revalidate after corrections. Finalization records the ruleset version, resolved selections and validation outcome.

Acceptance: invalid identifiers, duplicate unauthorized grants, missing prerequisites, stale calculations and excess expenditures are detected; a valid record finalizes; repeated validation without changes is deterministic.

## F07.11 — Worked Examples

The following are procedural examples, not numerically validated character sheets. Replace symbolic values with canonical values after reconciliation.

### Example A — Agricultural worker

Select an available agricultural occupation and document prior experience. Allocate attributes using the authorized method. Apply the selected record's mandatory grants and resolve its permitted alternatives. Spend discretionary resources on eligible personal skills and competencies. Verify any occupation advantage. Calculate derived statistics from the resulting values. Assign only authorized starting resources. Validate and finalize.

### Example B — Administrative worker

Select an available administrative occupation. Apply its documented grants without assuming access to former employer records or equipment. Use discretionary allocations to represent personal interests, subject to eligibility. Verify any claimed contact. If the campaign lacks the former institution, record the resulting access limitation separately from the character's retained professional competence. Validate and finalize.

### Example C — Revision and rejection

Begin with a valid draft. Change an attribute that supports a selected competency. Revalidate the competency and any dependent specialization. If eligibility is lost, flag the affected selections and prevent finalization until corrected using the canonical refund or replacement rules. Recalculate derived statistics. Revalidate the complete record before finalization.

## Canonical reconciliation — controlling implementation references

The following clarifications supersede any ambiguous wording above and in F07.1. Source: notes/DESIGN-NOTES.md, delegated character mechanics, Section 3 derived-stat revision, CCDD-12/19/20/22 and subsequent creation audit resolutions. The source rules remain authoritative.

- Attributes: STR, CON, DEX, INT, PER, WIL, PRE. Start each at 30, distribute 140 additional points, reclaim up to 15 per attribute at 1:1. Final total exactly 350, each 15–80. Injuries do not grant points. No occupational attribute minimum. Natural ceiling 100 after creation.
- General skills: 30 canonical skills; base floor(underlying governing attribute/2). Occupational total 100: BP primary 100 (30/25/20/15/10); SL primary 80 (25/20/15/10/10) and secondary 20; DZ primary 60 (20/15/10/10/5) and two secondary 20 each. Secondary distribution 10/5/5. Combined occupational cap 30 per skill; redistribute excess in relevant skills in increments of five. Personal general pool 100, maximum 20 per skill; underlying creation cap 85. Reallocate excess, do not convert pools. Custom occupations and unemployed characters use documented campaign-approved background allocation, not an automatic full professional package.
- Occupations are backgrounds, not classes. Support documented prior careers and campaign-approved experience tiers (trainee through executive leadership where applicable). Secondary general-skill backgrounds do not automatically grant full credentials, advantages or equipment. Grant actual documented occupational competencies without an artificial competency count ceiling. Merge duplicate canonical competency IDs by highest justified level, not addition; validate prerequisite closure or documented equivalent prior learning. Record credentials and actual appointment separately.
- Competencies: complexity c=1–7; acquired level L=0–5; occupational level O<=L. Personal cost c×[M(L)-M(O)], M(0)=0, M(L)=2^(L-1) otherwise. Budget BP10/SL15/DZ20; unspent competency points expire. Validate access, prerequisites and applicable CDR entry thresholds using validated grants and earlier eligible purchases, never the target's own award. Personal IV/V require documented history and GM approval. Occupational history grants are not retroactively CDR-gated. No independent legacy specialization track; contextual bonus B(L)=5×max(0,L−1), highest applicable only. CDR=sum(100×c×(2^L−1)) over unique acquired IDs; non-spendable. Slots use current era base 2/3/4 plus highest threshold bonus from CCDD-12; career retraining subset 1/1/2. No CDR conversion to skill points.
- Derived statistics use effective attributes: health max=max(1,floor((STR+CON)/10)) for living characters; stamina=floor((CON+WIL)/2); resilience=floor((WIL+CON)/2); initiative=floor((DEX+PER)/2); movement index=floor((STR+DEX+CON)/3); carrying reference=STR×0.5 kg; recovery index=floor((2×CON+WIL)/3). Stamina and resilience are distinct; resilience is not spendable. Recalculation never restores current pools; clamp to reduced maxima. Stress starts at zero unless agreed history.
- Native creation uses origin-era budgets. Transitional characters retain history, attributes, skills, competencies, CDR and actual surviving possessions; never receive a second creation budget. Document chronological training, adaptation and advancement with actual in-world time and evidence. Current era determines active slot capacity. No automatic skill loss or free later-era knowledge.
- No mandatory alignment, party composition or future occupation skill locks. Player controls identity and goals. Pregens follow the same rules. Manual tabletop and digital creation must agree.
