# F13 — Damage, Injuries and Recovery

Fictional game mechanics only, not medical advice. Canonical DESIGN-NOTES Section3 derived stats and Section4.2–4.3; F11 clock, F12 harm, F14 stress. Preserve approved numerical defaults.

## Health and injury
Hmax=max(1,floor((effective STR+effective CON)/10)) for living characters. Integer current HP 0..Hmax. Max changes clamp current down, never refill. F12 supplies post-protection harm. Severity based on post-protection harm before current-HP clamp (excess may inform severity): 0 none, 1 minor, 2–3 moderate, 4–6 severe, 7+ critical. This interpretation reconciles the earlier excess-harm rule with severity independent of current HP; record both penetrating harm and actual pool decrement. GM may escalate for documented fictional circumstances. Minor no automatic penalty; moderate -10, severe -20, critical -30 or infeasible for directly affected tasks. Apply highest relevant injury penalty once; no duplicate effective-attribute impairment. Illness, exposure and fatigue retain distinct causes and records; F19/F20 own exposure and deprivation triggers, no arbitrary injury rolls.

## Zero HP and deterioration
Zero HP incapacitates, not automatic death. Severe/critical injury or established ongoing life-threatening condition makes character unstable; otherwise stable incapacitated. Three abstract deterioration boxes; one at end of each subsequent unresolved unstable round, not initial depletion round. Death at three. Eligible helper uses main action and appropriate resources for Medicine check; success stabilizes, failure consumes action/resources, critical failure adds one box. Routine/impossible follow shared dice rules. Stabilization stops deterioration, does not restore HP or guarantee consciousness. New actual harm can reopen instability; retain boxes until definitive care resolves episode. Campaign-agreed less lethal variant selected before play. No real medical timetable implied.


## Saving a fallen teammate (existing stabilization rules)
When a character reaches zero HP, mark them incapacitated and determine whether they are stable or unstable using the injury and ongoing-threat criteria above. A teammate with applicable medical training (an appropriate Medicine check) and suitable available supplies may spend their **main action** attempting stabilization. Use the existing D100 Medicine resolution: success stabilizes; ordinary failure consumes the action and resources without stabilizing; critical failure also adds one deterioration box. Another eligible teammate may attempt care on a later turn if the patient remains alive and the circumstances permit. Other teammates can protect the patient, bring supplies or facilitate withdrawal through normal actions; these do not automatically stabilize the patient. Stabilization halts deterioration but does not restore HP, consciousness, or combat readiness. Do not add a rescue roll, bonus, free action or new die. Track the unstable round and deterioration boxes as usual.

## Falls and environmental harm (D20 conversion)

Falls use the **same single D20 harm roll** as other environmental hazards: D20 faces 1–5 = base 1, 6–10 = 2, 11–15 = 3, 16–20 = 4; add Low +0, Moderate +1, High +2 or Extreme +3. There is no success-degree bonus on environmental harm. Apply protection only if fictionally applicable; ordinary worn combat armor does not automatically mitigate impact from a fall. Classify injury using the resulting harm bands above.

For a simple reference: under 2 m generally no harm unless a hazardous landing is established; 2–4 m Moderate; 4–8 m High; 8–12 m Extreme; 12–20 m Extreme with **+2 situational harm**. These are game abstractions, not real-world fall-survival predictions. Aware characters with a feasible means to affect the landing may attempt one ordinary D100 Athletics check; success reduces severity by one profile band (Extreme to High, High to Moderate, Moderate to Low); failure leaves the hazard unchanged. Soft landing surfaces or genuine fall protection may mitigate or prevent harm as the fiction establishes. Do not stack the same mitigation twice.

For a fall over 20 m, resolve the fictional circumstances before rolling. If there is no credible mitigating circumstance, treat it as a life-threatening event causing zero HP and instability under the existing deterioration rules, **not automatic death**. If substantial mitigation is established, use the Extreme D20 profile and existing situational adjustments instead. This is an exceptional hazard ruling, not a general override of the D20 harm procedure.

## Poison, toxins, and illness (D100 resistance; D20 harm)

Define an exposure by its route, onset, potency (Low/Moderate/High/Extreme), duration or number of intervals, and available treatment. Give meaningful warning or discovery opportunities when fictionally plausible. At each consequential interval, make **one D100 CON resistance check** using the ordinary attribute-check procedure. On strong or better success, no harm; on standard success, take half of the **single D20 environmental harm result** rounded down; on failure, take the full result. A critical failure can establish a serious fictional complication but does **not** automatically add an extra damage interval. No extra dice or separate resistance ladder.

Use the D20 base and profile adjustments above, with **no degree bonus**; armor applies only if it genuinely protects against the specific exposure. A toxin may instead cause a defined functional condition or noninjurious stamina effect, not both by default. The GM sets intervals and duration according to the fictional exposure; do not add automatic daily damage or a universal poison counter. A qualified Medicine check with suitable supplies may stop or reduce further effects when treatment is feasible; success does not automatically restore lost HP. Extreme or explicitly life-threatening exposure at zero HP causes instability under the ordinary rules. Persistent illness uses the existing condition, reassessment and recovery framework rather than an independent injury system.

## Recovery
R=floor((2 effective CON+effective WIL)/3). Stable eligible rest per24h restores1 HP if R<60, otherwise2. One qualified care assessment per24h can add1 on strong or better with adequate resources; ordinary success/failure no bonus; critical failure declared fictional setback, not automatic harm. Max3 per interval, clamp Hmax. No recovery while unstable/deteriorating. No duplicate helpers or assessments. Minimum eligible rest intervals for reassessment: minor1, moderate3, severe7, critical14 days; not automatic clearance. Persistent conditions require appropriate reassessment and may last longer. Track eligible intervals separately from calendar elapsed; interruptions do not grant unearned rest. F11 governs time and resources.

## Stamina and fatigue
Smax=floor((effective CON+effective WIL)/2). Quiet eligible rest restores max(1,floor(Smax/4)) per hour capped at Smax; adequate eight-hour sleep restores remaining stamina if eligible, instead of hourly recovery for same interval. Zero stamina blocks strenuous actions, no automatic HP loss. F19/F20 own exertion and deprivation costs. Stress is separate F14 pool; no duplicate impairment penalties.

## Modes and records
Standard HP and most consequential unresolved condition; Advanced individual injury IDs, cause, severity, affected functions, stability, boxes, care and eligible rest; Simulation adds provider capacity, supplies and time ledger. Same outcome rules. Record consciousness separately. Care availability varies by actual setting conditions, not era label. Injury record fields: ID, character, source event, penetrating harm, actual HP decrement, severity, affected tasks, stability, deterioration episode, care assessment ID, eligible rest, reassessment and resolution. No automatic recovery from a change of mode or transition.
