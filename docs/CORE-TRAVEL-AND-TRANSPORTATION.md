# Core Travel and Transportation — Approved Working Rules
Status: Owner-approved 2026-11-25. Speeds are planning defaults, not maximum speeds or verified specifications for a particular vehicle. Expanded transport speeds remain subject to calibration against representative real-world vehicles. Applies to Blackbox Protocol (2026), Signal Lost, and Dead Zone; availability changes by era, not the underlying travel calculation.

## Scope and calculation
Travel time in motion = route distance ÷ applicable average speed. Total journey time also includes access, loading/boarding, stops, transfers, delays, rest, and required preparations. These values describe strategic/overland movement, **not tactical combat movement**. GM adjusts for terrain, weather, visibility, encumbrance, fatigue, road/track/waterway/airspace conditions, infrastructure, vehicle condition, traffic, fuel/power, and crew/operator qualifications. Never treat listed speeds as permission to ignore laws, hazards, route access, or equipment limits.

**Units:** 1 knot = approximately 1.15078 mph. Aircraft values are representative cruise estimates, not door-to-door averages. Watercraft values are speeds through water; currents, tides, wind, and sea state alter speed over ground. Modes have broad real-world performance ranges; these are simplifying tabletop defaults.

## Using the table
1. Establish the route and whether transport and infrastructure are actually available.
2. Select a default mode and applicable route/terrain condition; do not stack the generic road speed and a vehicle-specific speed as separate bonuses.
3. GM determines a reasonable adjusted average when conditions differ materially; note any assumption.
4. Compute moving time, then add stops, access, loading, boarding, transfers, rest, and delays. A 12-mile normal walk is 4 hours moving time before breaks.
5. For multi-leg trips, calculate each leg separately. A vehicle cannot traverse impassable infrastructure simply because it has a speed rating.

## Era application
- **Blackbox Protocol:** functioning transport networks may be available, subject to scenario and location.
- **Signal Lost:** disrupted scheduling, fuel, services, communications, maintenance, and routes can reduce or eliminate availability.
- **Dead Zone:** damaged infrastructure, scarce fuel, limited crews, and improvised transport may dominate; retain the same calculation and adjust actual achievable speed and route access.

## Integration and future calibration
Transport records should reference mode, representative vehicle class, route, condition, operating infrastructure, fuel/power, crew/operator requirements, maintenance, cargo capacity, and loading/access time. Fuel consumption, cargo, and maintenance must be specified in their respective systems rather than inferred from speed alone. Later calibration should compare representative real vehicles and terrain, then test total journey time and extreme cases. No untested numerical weather/terrain/fatigue modifiers are established by this document.

## Example
12 miles on foot at normal 3 mph = 4 hours of movement, plus rest. A 120-mile rural-road drive at 45 mph = 2 hours 40 minutes of movement, plus stops and delays. Neither result guarantees route access.

These examples use a **representative distance for each mode**, not the same distance for every mode. They show **movement time only**, rounded to the nearest five minutes for table use. Add rests, stops, loading, boarding, transfers, congestion, delays, and route access separately. These are examples, not fixed travel durations or guaranteed schedules. `nmi` means nautical miles; knot-based speeds use nautical miles per hour.

For a different distance, divide by the relevant speed and round to a convenient five-minute increment. Long-distance walking, running, cycling, and paddling **cannot** be sustained indefinitely; schedule breaks and fatigue using the applicable endurance rules. For aircraft, boats, and scheduled services, cruise or underway time can substantially understate door-to-door travel time. For example, a 1,000-mile jet flight at 500 mph has about 2 hours of cruise-equivalent movement, **not** a guaranteed two-hour total trip.
