# Chapter 1 — What this game is

*Sources: F01, F02.01, CCDD-15. Status: working draft.*

Blackbox is a classless, skill-based tabletop role-playing game set in an open world. It covers contemporary civilization, optional societal collapse, survival and reconstruction. Investigation is a major discipline, especially in the Blackbox Protocol era, but it is not required: a campaign can be about careers, business, politics, command, field operations, crime, emergency response, exploration, trade or rebuilding. No collapse, conspiracy, combat or metaplot is mandatory.

## Three eras
- **The Blackbox Protocol era** is the contemporary world, with a default starting year of 2026 and real geography and institutions. The Core Rulebook is its home book.
- **Signal Lost** is crisis and infrastructure deterioration.
- **Dead Zone** is sustained collapse, adaptation and reconstruction.

Play is forward-only within a continuous campaign, and a campaign can begin in any era. Recovery is progress within an era, never a return to an earlier one. The GM decides when a campaign moves between eras, guided by the world-state indicators in Chapter 13.

## One rules system
All three eras use the same dice, characters and rules. Eras change what the world offers: available gear, institutions, shortages, and the new competencies a character must learn. Characters keep their attributes, history, relationships and knowledge across eras; they do not get free replacement gear or free skills.

## Who decides
Players decide what their characters do. The GM adjudicates the world, decides when a roll is warranted, states the stakes before it, and narrates the result. The rules are a predictable framework. The GM may override a rule explicitly, record the house rule and explain material changes, but should not retroactively cancel a legitimate outcome just to protect a plot. Safety boundaries, consent and tone are agreed in Session Zero and revisited as needed.

## Ways to play
Tabletop with physical dice, with a dice app, or with BlackBox software, mixed freely. Digital tools are optional and never give an advantage. Detail is adjustable: **Standard**, **Advanced** and **Simulation** modes add bookkeeping on top of the same core rules without changing character records. Where a rule differs by mode, the chapter says so.

## Grounded by design
Rules describe fictional game situations, not real-world instructions. Harm, medicine, survival and crafting are abstractions for play. Optional anomalies and horror are separate (see the Anomaly Compendium); the Core is complete without them.
