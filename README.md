# Blackbox Protocol

*The world does not end all at once. Sometimes it changes while everyone is still going to work.*

**Blackbox Protocol** is an original, classless tabletop roleplaying game about investigation, human conflict, survival and the decisions people make when familiar systems begin to fail. Its world begins in a recognizable but altered 2026, where governments still govern, companies still compete, communities still function and most people are occupied with ordinary lives. Beneath that familiar surface lie decades of disputed contingency planning, institutional secrecy and unresolved questions about who should be trusted with the power to prepare for catastrophe.

The game is not built around the certainty of collapse. Players might uncover a conspiracy, investigate a crime, protect a community, expose wrongdoing, carry out a rescue, build a business or simply try to keep their lives intact. Some campaigns may follow a crisis into years of disruption. Others may prevent it, recover from it or never encounter it at all. The setting supplies circumstances and consequences, not a compulsory ending.

> **Investigate. Endure. Adapt.**  
> *Working tagline; not a finalized publication mark.*

## Three eras, one world

### Blackbox Protocol — Core, beginning in 2026

The contemporary setting is grounded in real geography, recognizable institutions and a fictional alternate history. A classified continuity-research effort that began during the Cold War eventually contributed to the Blackbox Initiative of 1991. Its legacy is not common knowledge. Public records, disputed testimony, legitimate research and private agendas intersect in ways that investigators may never fully untangle.

Core supports civilian and professional stories as readily as intelligence, political or corporate thrillers. A municipal engineer, journalist, physician, tradesperson, investigator or ordinary resident can become central to events without belonging to a special class or secret organization.

### Signal Lost — 2031–2045

Signal Lost explores periods of disruption, scarcity, contested authority and uneven recovery. The systems that supported daily life may become unreliable, but their failure is neither uniform nor inevitable. Some regions retain working institutions while others improvise. Relief workers, technicians, officials, families and local organizations make decisions whose consequences extend beyond the immediate emergency.

### Dead Zone — 2046 onward

Dead Zone turns toward survival, exploration, agriculture, repair, trade and reconstruction. Communities may inherit infrastructure they cannot easily maintain, records whose authority is disputed and skills that become more valuable than former titles. The world is not required to become uniformly lawless or hopeless. Settlements, cooperatives and new institutions can emerge from what remains.

These eras share a foundation and support character continuity. They are possible settings for play, **not a mandatory sequence of disasters**.

## The people at the center

Blackbox Protocol uses an **occupation-based, classless character foundation**. A character's earlier work and experience inform their starting abilities without dictating what they can learn or become. Skills, relationships, beliefs, responsibilities, qualifications and personal goals can change over time. A character may move from investigation to emergency response, from technical work to community leadership, or in a direction no published campaign anticipated.

There is no mandatory party composition, fixed moral alignment or single definition of success. The game is designed for one-shots, investigations, episodic adventures, long-running campaigns, sandbox play and, where supported, solo or GM-less play. Failure should change the situation rather than merely close the story.

## Rules that serve the situation

The system combines consistent underlying mechanics with **modular realism**. Groups can choose Quick Start, Casual, Standard, Advanced, Simulation or custom levels of detail, with individual subsystems configured separately where supported. Investigation, combat, health, equipment, economics, infrastructure, agriculture, factions and settlements should share the same mechanical foundation rather than becoming disconnected games.

Detail is meant to matter when it changes a decision. Routine tasks should not require constant rolling, and complexity should not become a substitute for meaningful consequences. Numerical mechanics and balance decisions remain subject to their documented approval and playtesting status; this repository is not presented as a finished rulebook.

The tabletop game must remain playable with printed materials and physical dice. Future digital tools are intended to reduce bookkeeping and support play, not to require an internet connection, subscription, external AI service or a particular device.

## A world of competing interests

The setting's institutions are neither universally benevolent nor uniformly corrupt. The **Halden Institute** began with a sincere interest in the resilience of essential systems. Its history includes legitimate research, difficult compromises and internal disagreements over accountability, intervention and institutional survival. **Cindrix Corporation** grew into a major commercial and infrastructure enterprise with real employees, customers and public responsibilities. **Veydran Group** influences medicine and scientific research; **Vantrell Group** operates through investment and institutional finance. Their interests can align in one place and collide in another.

Independent businesses such as Ridgeline Outfitters, Sentinel Supply Co., Ironfield Supply, Harvest & Homestead and CircuitPoint give the world a commercial life beyond its largest corporations. Governments, universities, hospitals, labor organizations, local communities and individual workers possess agency of their own.

A contract is not proof of conspiracy. A secret is not automatically a crime. A powerful organization is not a single personality. Players should be able to discover what happened, challenge what they are told and change the outcome.

## Development direction

The approved sequence prioritizes the foundation and core tabletop game, followed by setting materials, physical play aids, the **Zero Day** starter adventure, playtesting and refinement. Digital development follows the tabletop foundation rather than replacing it. Planned publication families include quick-start rules, a core rulebook, GM and player resources, setting books, field manuals, case files, adventures and creator tools; inclusion in the plan does not mean a publication is complete.

This repository is a working record of that process. New lore must respect established chronology and the separation between public knowledge and GM-only truth. New mechanics must consult existing authoritative systems, document dependencies and avoid duplicate rules. Approved decisions should not be silently rewritten to make a new document fit.

## Original work and rights

Blackbox Protocol is an original fictional game setting and rules-development project. References to real historical events, places and institutions provide context; fictional organizations and characters are not claims about real people or undisclosed historical conduct.

**No license has been selected for this repository.** Public access to its contents does not, by itself, grant permission to redistribute or commercially reuse the material. Third-party restricted or copyrighted sourcebooks and other nonredistributable assets must not be added to the public repository.
